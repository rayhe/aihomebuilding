# Research: AI-Generated Home Designs vs. the Design Review Board

**Slug:** ai-generated-home-design-hoa-design-review-rejection-2026
**Journalist:** Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design)
**Article #:** 978
**Date:** 2026-09-27

## Angle

AI design tools (Maket.ai et al.) can generate a complete residential design in minutes for $20 a month. They optimize for what they can measure: room count, area efficiency, cost, even zoning answers via a "regulatory assistant." What they cannot measure is the thing that kills more residential designs than any code violation: whether a committee of your neighbors finds it pretty enough. HOA Architectural Review Committees judge on "similarity in architectural style," "neighborhood character," and "compatibility" — qualitative, human, political criteria no generator is trained on. Elena's voice: skeptical of tools that flatten design into optimization, sees buildings as art first. The original contribution is the **ARC Pre-Flight Audit** — a 30-minute street survey that tells you which architectural vocabulary to feed the generator before you spend a dollar on drawings.

## Kill test

Does this help someone building a home? Yes. An AI-generated design that the ARC rejects costs months of redesign and a second round of drawing fees; the pre-flight audit costs one walk around the block. This is the missing step between "generate" and "submit."

### 1. Maket.ai — company data (AI home design generator)
- https://aitools.fyi/maket
- https://illustrarch.com/articles/design-softwares/73352-maket-ai-review.html
- AI floor plan generator: text prompt to editable residential plan + 3D render in minutes. Free tier 50 credits; **Homeowner $20/mo (300 credits)**, Pro $100/mo (3,000 credits). **20 credits per floor, 10 per render, 5 per optimization.** Includes an "AI-Powered Regulatory Assistant" that answers zoning/code questions in plain language. Pro tier adds "human-validated plan uploads" — an admission the raw output is not a drawing you build from. Maket's own site notes professionals should review plans before construction or permitting.

### 2. HousingWire — permit shot-clock research (Soltas et al., 2026)
- https://www.housingwire.com/articles/permit-shot-clocks-research/
- **1.1 million permits across 60 U.S. cities, 2000-2025.** Permitting delays (not construction time) account for **30% to 43%** of the completion-time gap between slow and fast cities. In LA, developers pay a **50% premium ($48/sq ft)** for land with pre-approved permits. Texas: 30-day plat-review deadline, miss it and the application is **deemed approved**.

### 3. PubliCola — Seattle design review (2022)
- http://publicola.com/2022/05/03/is-it-time-for-seattle-to-do-away-with-design-review/?ref=blog.freopp.org
- Design review judges appearance, relation to terrain and adjacent properties, materials, landscaping — **not code compliance**. All-volunteer boards, decisions final, no formal appeal. Aesthetic dictates add **months or years of delay and hundreds of thousands of dollars**; boards have held up projects over "the color of brick facades." (Seattle exempts single-family, which is exactly why the HOA ARC is the single-family equivalent.)

### 4. Realtor.com — Portland suspends design review (2026)
- https://www.realtor.com/advice/hyperlocal/portland-temporarily-suspend-design-review-requirements/
- Portland moving to temporarily suspend design review requirements in most cases; could cut **weeks or months** off approval timelines. Design review evaluates "how buildings look and fit into their surroundings."

### 5. Salem Run HOA — ARC guidelines (primary HOA document)
- https://hoaproxy.org/hoas/Salem%20Run%20HOA/documents/file?path=Salem%20Run%20Homeowners%20Association%2Farc_guidelines.pdf
- Review criteria: (a) **Design Compatibility — "similarity in Architectural style, quality of workmanship, and construction details"** with the homeowner's house, adjoining houses, and the neighborhood setting; (b) location/sunlight/ventilation/drainage; (c) impact on neighbors (affected neighbors' signatures required; approval may be denied if it "adversely affects neighboring homeowners"); (d) scale relative to adjacent structures; (e) color consistent with existing house; (f) materials — "continuity established by use of the same or comparable materials"; (g) workmanship; (h) timing (commence within 6 months; >90 days requires resubmission). New construction must comply with "village character in house design, scale, character, materials."

### 6. Streamline Design & Permitting — pre-construction cost analysis, San Diego County (Sep 2026)
- https://www.mycarrollcountynews.com/online_features/press_releases/article_21f7223b-6a13-5787-8565-a74de15d5c68.html
- Pre-construction (site assessment, drafting, Title 24, permit prep) typically **3-4% of total project cost**; for a $400,000 ADU/addition, roughly **$12,000-$16,000** in drafting and permitting fees. San Diego DSD plan check: each correction round addresses only prior-round corrections, so incomplete drawings compound into more cycles. CA **SB 543 (effective Jan 1, 2026)**: agencies must complete permit reviews within **60 days of a complete application** — the clock starts only when the application is deemed complete.

### 7. Rioo / TNWLC — HOA architectural review process checklists
- https://riooapp.com/blog/hoa-architectural-review-process
- https://tnwlc.com/hoa-architectural-review-checklist/
- Submission typically requires: application form, project description, **detailed plans/elevations/renderings**, material specs, color samples, site photos, contractor info, permits, timeline. Some CC&Rs deem a request approved if the committee misses its deadline; others do the opposite; some are silent. A denial citing only "general community standards" is hard to act on and hard to defend — which cuts both ways for AI-generated designs.

## The gap (original contribution)

The generator answers the wrong question. Maket's regulatory assistant will tell you whether your setback complies. Nobody asks the tool whether the design reads as "compatible with the architectural characteristics of adjoining houses," because that sentence is not computable — it is a judgment call made by volunteers with taste, grudges, and long memories.

The failure mode, stated plainly: AI generators optimize the plan (rooms per square foot, cost per square foot) and render it beautifully. ARCs reject the elevation (wrong style vocabulary, wrong materials, wrong scale relative to the street). The homeowner then discovers the rejection applies to the *design*, not the *drawings* — you cannot fix it with another 20-credit regeneration pass; you need a different house.

**The ARC Pre-Flight Audit** (the actionable core) — 30 minutes, before you open the generator:
1. **Walk the street and count.** Tally the dominant roof profile, cladding material, and window rhythm on your block. The Texas HOA roofing guide's rule generalizes: match the dominant vocabulary, not your personal preference — the closer the match, the easier the committee's decision.
2. **Read the actual criteria.** Pull your HOA's ARC guidelines (or the city's design guidelines) and highlight every adjective: "compatible," "similar," "consistent," "traditional." Those adjectives are the real design brief. Feed them to the generator as style constraints, not afterthoughts.
3. **Get the neighbor signatures early.** Salem Run-style rules let affected neighbors sink an application. Show them the render before you submit — the render is free; the resubmission costs months.
4. **Budget the redesign, not the generation.** $20/mo generates; $12,000-$16,000 (Streamline's San Diego figure) draws and permits. Price the project on the second number, and keep a redesign reserve for the round the committee sends back.

## Counterargument (steelman)

Design review exists because unregulated taste produces genuinely bad streetscapes, and the data on its costs (Seattle, Portland) comes mostly from multifamily, where the stakes and delays are largest. For single-family infill, a good ARC protects the single largest asset most families own. Also: Maket-style tools arguably *help* with ARC approval — a photorealistic render communicates intent better than hand sketches, and the Pro tier's human-validated uploads close part of the credibility gap. The tool is not the enemy of the committee; the unbriefed prompt is.

## Limitations

- No published data on AI-generated residential design approval/rejection rates at ARCs or design review boards; the rejection risk is reasoned from criteria language, not measured outcomes.
- Seattle/Portland design-review data is multifamily; single-family HOA ARC timelines are anecdotal and vary wildly by CC&Rs.
- Maket pricing/credits from third-party reviews of Maket's pricing page (Aug-Sep 2026); verify before quoting as current.
- The audit is a heuristic, not legal advice; CC&Rs and state HOA law vary by jurisdiction.
