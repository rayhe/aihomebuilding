# Research: ADU Feasibility — AI vs. Architect Information Gap

## Angle
Homeowners spend $5,000–$15,000 on architectural design and $8,000–$12,000 on consultation before learning whether their lot can even support an ADU. AI-powered site feasibility tools answer "Can I build here?" in seconds using zoning data, setback rules, overlays, and geospatial analysis — for $0 to $250. But most homeowners don't know these tools exist.

## Key Data Points

### ADU Market Scale (California)
- 23,000+ ADUs legally completed in 2023 (CA Assembly Housing Committee background report)
- ADUs went from <1% of new construction before 2017 to ~20% now (same source)
- Market feasibility estimated at ~1.8 million potential ADU sites in California (same source)
- Median ADU construction cost: $100,000–$177,500 (UC Berkeley 2021 survey of permitted ADU owners)
- Orange County alone: 1,490 net ADUs in 2024, up from 65 in 2017 (CSU Fullerton CDR Housing Inventory Survey)
- State laws (SB 1069, SB 13, AB 68) transformed ADU landscape post-2016

### Feasibility / Design Costs (Traditional)
- Architectural design fees: $5,000–$15,000 (design-otb.com cost comparison)
- Consultation fees: $8,000–$12,000 (GatherADU comprehensive guide)
- Design costs = 10–15% of total project cost (Angi 2026 data)
- On a $120K project, that's $12,000–$18,000 for design alone
- House planning/drafting: $1,100–$5,500 (RenoFi ADU cost breakdown)
- Land survey: $400–$1,900 (RenoFi)
- Site excavation: $1,300–$5,000 (RenoFi)
- Architect fees: $125–$250/hour (Angi)
- Soft costs total (design + consult + permits): 10–15% of project

### AI Feasibility Tools
1. **canibuild.com** — 4.2+ million site assessments. Instant zoning, setbacks, overlays. Serves US, AU, NZ, CA. Answers "Can I build here?" in seconds. For builders, homeowners, and government.
2. **ADUscale** (feasibility.aduscale.com) — Free "ADU Reality Check" tool. Parcel-specific assessment.
3. **LlamaIndex/GenAI ADU Planner** — GPT-4V + satellite imagery + Google Maps. Parses local building codes automatically, identifies buildable zones, matches with local builder floor plans. 3 days vs. traditional feasibility timeline.
4. **Deloitte Smart Permitting Agent** (AWS Marketplace) — Reads CAD drawings, detects violations, references regulations. For government agencies.
5. **Custom design software** (Builder Online article) — 3-day design process at $250 for full custom floor plan, vendor quotes, pricing, and renderings.

### The Information Asymmetry
- Many homeowners invest in full design work only to discover during plan check their lot won't work (Medium/Terrakan article)
- Common rejection reasons: setback violations, lot coverage limits, special zoning overlays, unpermitted work on main house, building/energy code failures
- "Too many homeowners invest in design work, only to discover during plan check that their lot cannot accommodate the ADU they had in mind."
- Traditional feasibility requires: site visit, survey, zoning research, setback analysis, overlay identification, easement check, environmental constraints, utility connection assessment
- AI tools do all of this from satellite imagery + public zoning data

### Municipal AI Adoption
- LA County: AI reviews building plans in one business day (HousingWire)
- Altamonte Springs, FL: AutoReview.AI pilot — "What used to take us a week took us three to four minutes" (City Manager Frank Martz)
- Seattle: Mayor executive order for AI pilot on all development applications, 2026 rollout
- Austin: AI tool with Archistar for zoning review (beta)
- Honolulu: AI permitting speedup planned
- San Luis Obispo County: Pre-approved ADU plans program (free to applicants)

### Key Barriers
- Santa Cruz County 2023 APR: "Major barriers to ADU construction include permit and construction costs as well as property owner unfamiliarity with the project planning, design, and construction process"
- Reason.org analysis: "homeowners must navigate sign-offs across multiple agencies, meet infrastructure requirements, absorb high upfront costs, and navigate fragmented approval processes"
- Hawaii Act 39: Even after state-level legalization, "local governments retain the authority to set requirements that discourage construction"
