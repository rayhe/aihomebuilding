# Research Notes: Replacement Cost Estimation Algorithms & Systematic Underinsurance

## Slug: replacement-cost-algorithm-underinsurance-gap
## Journalist: Catherine Chen
## Date: July 30, 2026

## Thesis
The algorithms that estimate your home's replacement cost — Verisk's 360Value and CoreLogic's Marshall & Swift — systematically undervalue what it costs to rebuild. Insurers know this. They use them anyway. The result: most American homeowners cover roughly 70% of what a rebuild would actually cost. After the 2025 LA fires, 11,500 families learned the hard way.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **Yes.** Every homeowner with an insurance policy should check whether their Coverage A limit reflects actual rebuild costs. This article gives them the numbers to understand why it probably doesn't, who's responsible, and what to do about it.

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing the 360Value error rates (50% wrong on foundation shape) with the CalFund survey shortfall data ($660K average gap) and the Marshall Fire displacement data (25% fewer rebuild permits) to quantify the downstream human cost of algorithmic undervaluation. Nobody has connected these three datasets in a single analysis.

## Counterargument (Full Strength)
Insurers argue that replacement cost estimators are tools, not guarantees. The estimates are reviewed by agents, and homeowners have the option to request higher coverage limits. Inflation guard endorsements (2-4% annual increases) are designed to address drift. Extended replacement cost endorsements (25-50% above Coverage A) exist specifically for catastrophe scenarios. The problem isn't the algorithm — it's that homeowners don't read their policies and agents don't push for adequate limits because higher premiums lose sales. Blaming the software lets agents and homeowners off the hook.

## Limitations
- 360Value's 50% foundation shape error rate comes from a 2019 internal report. Current accuracy may have improved.
- CalFund survey is self-reported (respondents estimating their own gaps, not independently verified rebuild costs).
- The $400/sqft vs $850/sqft comparison from United Policyholders is anecdotal and reflects post-disaster demand surge pricing, not normal conditions.
- This analysis focuses on California, which has unique regulatory history and fire exposure. Underinsurance patterns differ in other states.

## Actionable Takeaways for Reader
1. Pull your declarations page — find your Coverage A dwelling limit
2. Calculate: Coverage A ÷ (sqft × local $/sqft rebuild cost) = your coverage ratio
3. Below 1.0 means you eat the difference in a total loss. Below 0.8 triggers coinsurance penalty on partial losses.
4. Ask your agent for the 360Value or MSB report they used. Check the foundation type, exterior finish, roof type, and quality grade against reality.
5. Consider guaranteed replacement cost or extended replacement cost endorsements (25-50% above Coverage A).
6. In fire/disaster zones: factor in demand surge (rebuild costs can spike 30-50% after a regional disaster).
