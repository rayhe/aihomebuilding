# Research: Canadian Plywood Tariff → OSB Substitution Code Gap

## Angle
Canadian plywood just got hit with a new 50% tariff (effective Aug 19, 2026). Builders have 19 days to re-spec their sheet goods. The obvious move: swap plywood to cheaper, domestic OSB. But OSB has dramatically different moisture performance — 32.9% thickness swell vs 9.4% for plywood — and a vapor perm rating of 2 vs plywood's 10. In humid climate zones, that swap can violate building science principles and fail code-required air barrier standards. No AI estimating or takeoff tool cross-references tariff-affected materials with IRC code sections and local climate zone requirements to tell a builder which panel applications can be swapped and which ones can't.

## Kill Test
✅ Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — directly relevant to any builder with a project starting in the next 6 months who needs to decide whether to switch from plywood to OSB sheathing under tariff pressure. Gives them the specific code sections and climate zone boundaries they need to make the call.

## Primary Sources

### 1. New 50% Canadian Tariff on Plywood (July 27, 2026)
- Trump imposed 50% tariffs on many Canadian imports, scheduled to take effect Aug 19, 2026
- 18-page White House list explicitly includes "many different types of plywood, a key homebuilding material"
- Source: USA Today, NY Post, Telegram & Gazette (July 24-27, 2026)

### 2. Cumulative Canadian Lumber Duties
- Combined duties on Canadian softwood lumber now exceed 45%
- Anti-dumping duty: 20.56% (Commerce Dept, July 25 determination)
- Countervailing duty: 6.74% → rising to 14.38% (pending Aug 8 final determination)
- Section 232 tariff: 10% (Oct 2025)
- NAHB: tariffs "act as a tax on American builders, home buyers and consumers"
- US imports 1/3 of lumber consumed; Canada = 85% of all US lumber imports
- Canadian mills closing across British Columbia — reducing supply
- Kitchen cabinet tariffs already at 50% since Jan 1, 2026
- NAHB estimates: ~$11,000 cost increase per new home from tariffs
- Framing lumber = 15-20% of new home cost (Ohio State University)
- Source: NAHB, ProBuilder, LBM Journal, Inman, Ohio State University

### 3. OSB vs. Plywood: Material Performance Data
- **Cost:** OSB is 20-30% cheaper than equivalent plywood (most sources agree 10-30%)
- **Thickness swell:** Plywood 9.4% vs OSB 32.9% (oven dry to vacuum soak) — OSB swelling is PERMANENT
- **Vapor permeability:** OSB ~2 perms vs plywood ~10 perms (commonly held industry values). Canadian building code: 7/16" OSB at 44 NG (2/3 perm), 3/8" plywood at 57 NG (1 perm)
- **Air barrier performance:** RDH study found OSB could NOT meet air-barrier standard (0.02 L/s·m² at 75 Pa). Plywood passed dry but failed after wetting/drying cycles. ZIP sheathing passed in all conditions.
- **Nail withdrawal:** OSB 20-30% lower than plywood under cyclical loads
- **Shear strength:** Roughly equivalent when both meet PS 2 or APA-rated structural designations
- **Edge swelling:** OSB edge swelling is permanent and progressively reduces nail-holding strength along that edge
- **Drying behavior:** Plywood absorbs moisture, swells slightly, recovers. OSB absorbs more, swells more, does NOT recover.
- Source: GreenBuildingAdvisor (RDH study), Ecohome (Oak Ridge Research Laboratories data), VinawoodLTD, DIYEcoHomes, Homedit, Family Handyman

### 4. Building Code Context (IRC)
- IRC R503: Subfloor sheathing requirements (span ratings, fastening schedules)
- IRC R602: Wall framing/sheathing (bracing methods, including let-in bracing and structural panel)
- IRC R803: Roof sheathing requirements
- Both OSB and plywood are accepted structural panel products under IRC when properly rated
- BUT: Climate zone, exposure category, and wall assembly design affect which is appropriate
- Key distinction: In climate zones 4A-7 (marine and cold), vapor permeability of exterior sheathing is critical for wall drying potential
- IRC R702.7: Weather-resistive barriers — OSB's poor air barrier performance after wetting matters here

- Togal.AI: $299/mo, automatic plan analysis, counts materials from blueprints — NO material substitution
- STACK: $1,999/yr, cloud-based takeoffs — counts, doesn't substitute
- Bolster: AI-driven takeoff from blueprints — measures, doesn't advise on material equivalents
- Kreo: $35/mo, AI-driven design insights — basic
- Higharc: Just raised $95M Series C — claims "design through construction" AI but focused on plan generation, estimating, and sales. No tariff-responsive material substitution.
- ProEst (Autodesk): $108/mo — integrates with BIM but no code-compliance crosscheck
- NONE of these tools cross-reference: (1) tariff-affected materials, (2) IRC code sections for the specific application, (3) climate zone requirements, (4) wall assembly vapor profile

## Strongest Counterargument
Most residential construction in dry climate zones (climate zones 1-3, covering the Sun Belt where most new homes are built) can safely use OSB for all structural panel applications. The moisture performance gap only becomes code-critical in marine and cold climates. So for D.R. Horton building tract homes in Phoenix, the swap is straightforward. The tool gap matters most for custom builders in the Pacific Northwest, Great Lakes, and Northeast — a smaller but significant market.

## Limitations
- Exact tariff impact on plywood prices is uncertain (tariff schedule could change before Aug 19)
- OSB is largely domestically produced (Norbord, LP, Ainsworth in US/Canada) — tariff on Canadian OSB matters too
- IRC code sections cited are from 2021 IRC; local amendments may be stricter
- No builder survey data on actual substitution rates under tariff pressure
- Material performance data varies by manufacturer and grade
