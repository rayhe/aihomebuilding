# Research: AI Fall Protection Cameras vs. OSHA's 15-Year Repeat Offender

**Article #869 | Journalist: Marcus "Steel" Washington | Date: 2026-09-15**
**Proposed slug:** `ai-fall-protection-camera-roofing-residential-2026`
**Proposed headline:** "Fall Protection Topped OSHA's Violation List for the 15th Straight Year. A Camera Cut Violations 41.7% in 90 Days."

## Angle
Falls kill more construction workers than anything else, and fall protection has been OSHA's #1 most-cited standard for 15 consecutive years — mostly roofing, framing, and siding contractors, i.e., residential work. AI cameras can now watch every worker, every hour, and flag untied-off workers in real time: viAct on Alliad's existing cameras cut PPE violations 41.7% in three months. But the tech is landing at large commercial GCs with compliance departments. The people falling off roofs are on small non-union residential crews where the harness hangs in the truck and the only "competent person" is whoever the foreman pointed at. Plus the surveillance paradox: the same camera that saves a life logs every minute of every worker's day, and labor lawyers say employers who deploy it without legal prep are buying a lawsuit. Marcus angle: tell it through the workers, the disproportionate toll, and the uncomfortable conclusion that knowing is not the same as doing.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. If you are building or remodeling: fall citations now carry five-figure penalties and work stoppages, a serious fall on your project is a schedule killer and a liability nightmare, and most residential roofers/framing crews have no written fall protection plan for your specific roof. Three questions in the article (the Rescue Plan Test) give a homeowner or GC a concrete way to separate contractors who manage fall risk from contractors who don't. For GCs running residential crews: AI vision on existing job-site cameras is the cheapest way to demonstrate good-faith compliance.

## Supporting facts
- NRCA: "Falls are the leading cause of death in the construction industry."
- 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(13): residential construction workers exposed to falls of 6 feet or more must be protected by guardrail, net, or personal fall arrest system.
- 29 CFR 1926.502(d)(20): employer must provide for "prompt rescue of employees in the event of a fall" — almost no residential contractor has a documented rescue plan; suspension trauma can kill within 15-30 minutes.
- Penalty context: OSHA's per-violation maximums are adjusted annually for inflation (serious violations now ~$16,000+ each).

## Skepticism to include
- The Alliad result is a vendor case study on a large engineering/construction firm — commercial, not residential. No published evidence yet that AI vision changes behavior on a 5-person residential roofing crew.
- AI vision watches PPE/harness presence; it does not verify the anchor point is rated or the lanyard is attached to anything real. A tied-off harness on a dead anchor is a decoration.
- Surveillance creates its own backlash: existing site articles cover worker resistance to surveillance; the Bloomberg Law piece warns about legal exposure (privacy, bargaining obligations).
- Compliance ≠ safety culture: OSHA fines did not fix it in 15 years; a camera can only escalate, not convince.
- Cost/pricing of AI vision platforms is mostly undisclosed; viAct's Alliad deployment used existing cameras, which residential sites often lack.

## Original contribution
**The Rescue Plan Test** — three questions to ask any roofer, framer, or GC before they set foot on your roof:
1. "Show me the written fall protection plan for this roof" (pitch, edges, anchor points — not a generic template).
2. "Who is the competent person on site today, and where is the rescue plan?" (OSHA requires prompt rescue; a worker hanging in a harness has 15-30 minutes before suspension trauma).
3. "What camera or monitoring do you run to verify tie-off?" — frames AI monitoring as a compliance differentiator when hiring.
Almost no residential contractor can answer #2. That gap is the story.
