## Working headline
"Your Adjuster's Software Says $18,000. Your Contractor Says $31,000. The Software Is Negotiable."

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **Yes.** Every year, millions of homeowners file property damage claims (hail, wind, water, fire) and accept the carrier's Xactimate estimate as gospel. The gap between the adjuster's number and the actual repair cost routinely runs 30-60%, and the shortfall comes out of the homeowner's pocket or the contractor's margin. A homeowner who knows how to audit the estimate — line by line, with AI help — recovers thousands.

## Core thesis
Xactimate is not a price. It is a starting bid, generated by software the carrier configures, using price lists that lag the market by design. Homeowners who treat the adjuster's estimate as final leave money on the table; the ones who audit it — checking overhead & profit, matching, depreciation games, and omitted line items — close the gap. AI tools now do in 60 seconds what used to require a public adjuster.

## Primary sources

### 1. United Policyholders — "Xactimate Demystified" (nonprofit consumer guide)
- URL: https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/xactimate-demystified/?print=pdf
- Key facts: Xactimate is "best suited to tract homes"; for custom, historic, or high-value-area homes the numbers "will likely not be sufficient." Lowballing from improper Xactimate use "factored into many claim disputes and lawsuits, particularly in the aftermath of Gulf Coast hurricanes."
- Most GCs do not use Xactimate — they bid from sub bids. Apples-to-apples comparison is hard; the fix is an independent estimate from a construction professional trained in Xactimate.

### 2. Property Insurance Coverage Law Blog — "When Is a Policyholder Entitled to Overhead and Profit?"
- URL: https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/when-is-a-policyholder-entitled-to-overhead-and-profit/
- Key facts: *Gilderman v. State Farm* — where more than one trade is required, it is "reasonably likely" a GC is needed, so the carrier must include O&P in the ACV payment even if the insured DIYs or hires a handyman. *Mee v. Safeco* — appellate court reversed summary judgment for the carrier on O&P. The "three trade rule" is the prevailing test.
- Typical O&P: 10% overhead + 10% profit = 20% of the job. On a $50,000 repair, that is $10,000 the estimate may silently omit.

### 3. Adjusters International, "Adjusting Today" — Overhead and Profit
- URL: https://www.adjustersinternational.com/pubs/adjusting-today/overhead-and-profit/
- Key facts: Texas Department of Insurance bulletin (June 12, 1998): deducting prospective contractors' O&P and sales tax from replacement cost when calculating ACV "is improper, is not a reasonable interpretation of the policy language, and is unfair to insureds." Colorado Division of Insurance (June 4, 1998) took the same position against State Farm. PLRB (insurer-used resource) position: contractor O&P are included in ACV because they are part of replacement cost.
- Carriers have moved from the three-trade count to subjective "complexity" tests — harder for homeowners to argue against.

### 4. United Policyholders — Matching memo (PDF)
- URL: https://uphelp.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/matching_memo_4-2016_to_post.pdf
- Key facts: *Greene v. USAA* — court held "the replacement cost of that part of the building damaged" required at most one roof slope, calling full-roof replacement an "absurdity"; the "line of sight" approach. But where damaged shingles cannot be matched in "function, color, and shape," the matching principle supports broader replacement. *Collins v. Allstate* (E.D. Pa. 2009) follows the same line.
- Takeaway: matching is the second-most-fought line item after O&P, and outcomes are state- and fact-specific.

### 5. C&R Magazine — "Allstate Pays Contractor $335,000 to Settle Dispute Over $33,000 Restoration Invoice"
- URL: https://www.candrmagazine.com/allstate-pays-contractor-335000-to-settle-dispute-over-33000-restoration-invoice/
- Key facts: A claims manager for American Reliable testified "the rule" is O&P paid to a GC when three or more trades are involved. Restorers are not required to match each other's prices; "it is illegal" (antitrust). On non-program jobs, carriers may not set prices or dictate billing models. A $33,000 invoice dispute settled for $335,000 — a 10x multiple showing how far carriers will go to defend a pricing position, and what happens when they lose.

### 6. Claims Journal — State Farm Xactimate class action dismissed (Belotti, March 2025)
- URL: https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2025/03/28/329731.htm
- Key facts: Federal judge dismissed PA homeowners' class action alleging State Farm undervalued claims via Xactimate's "new construction" vs "repair/reconstruction" price settings. The parties were ~$200,000 apart on replacement cost. Lesson for the article: the software has settings and price-list choices the homeowner never sees, and courts treat the *method* as the carrier's prerogative — which is exactly why auditing the *output* line by line matters more than fighting the tool.

### 7. LJB Legal — "Xactimate, Replacement Costs, Actual Cash Value, and Depreciation"

## Original contribution (the novel analysis)
1. **The O&P math nobody shows the homeowner:** On a $50,000 repair estimate, 10% overhead + 10% profit = $10,000. Carriers routinely omit it citing "complexity" or trade counts; *Gilderman*/*Mee* and the Texas/Colorado DOI positions say the three-trade test controls. The article walks a sample water-loss scope (drywall, paint, flooring = three trades) and shows the $10K appearing/disappearing.
2. **The 30-day lag as structural underpayment:** Verisk's own EULA admits pricing is 30-day-old "historical" data. In post-catastrophe markets (labor +25-100% per adjuster forum rules of thumb), the lag is not a rounding error — it is a built-in haircut. Nobody in the consumer press has connected the EULA language to the surge-pricing problem.
3. **The audit checklist as a decision tool:** the five most-omitted line items (dust barriers, HEPA scrubbers, ram board, outlet resets, texture blending) + O&P + matching + sales tax on materials, assembled into a one-page audit the reader can run against any adjuster estimate with free AI help.

## Strongest counterargument
The carriers' case, stated fairly: Xactimate standardizes pricing across thousands of adjusters and prevents contractor price-gouging after catastrophes — without it, every claim becomes a bespoke negotiation and premiums rise for everyone. The Belotti dismissal shows courts agree carriers may choose their estimation method. AI "audit" tools are mostly sold to contractors chasing supplements, not to homeowners — their $4,820 "average recovery" is a marketing number with no independent audit, and a tool that finds "missing" line items on every estimate is a revenue machine for roofers, not a neutral arbiter. The honest version: auditing works, but the auditor's incentives matter as much as the software's.

## Limitations
- O&P entitlement and matching rules are state-specific; the article's legal summary leans on PA (Gilderman/Mee), TX and CO DOI positions — not a 50-state survey.
- RoofGenius's $4,820 average recovery figure is vendor marketing, unverified by any third party; treated as a claim, not a fact.
- Xactimate price lists are proprietary; the "below market" characterization comes from policyholder-side sources (UP, LJB Legal), not from Verisk.
- No independent test of AI audit tools was performed; capabilities described from vendor/public materials.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
1. Get an independent estimate from a contractor trained in Xactimate — not just any GC bid — so the two estimates speak the same line-item language (United Policyholders).
2. Check the O&P line first: three or more trades on the scope = O&P is owed in most states under the prevailing case law. If it is missing, that is your first supplement.
3. Run the five-item omission checklist (dust barriers, HEPA scrubbers, ram board/masking, outlet cover resets, texture blending) against the adjuster's sketch.
4. On roofs, photograph the undamaged slopes: if new shingles cannot match in function, color, and shape, you have a matching argument — know your state's line-of-sight rule before you make it.
5. Never accept "the computer says" as final: the Belotti case proves the carrier chooses the settings. The settings are negotiable even when the software is not.
6. If the gap exceeds ~$10,000 or the carrier will not supplement, price a public adjuster (typically 10% of the claim, capped in many states) against the expected recovery before hiring one.
