# Research Notes — Thread 79: AI-Audited Design Center Options (article #822)

## Thread
**AI design-center price auditing: buyers use AI to split builder options into "buy now" vs "buy later," priced against aftermarket data.**

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes — directly. Every new-construction buyer sits through 3-4 design studio appointments and signs an option sheet worth 5-15% of the home price with almost no price transparency. This gives them a concrete method to decide which options to buy from the builder and which to do after closing.

## Working headline
"Your Builder Charged $4,200 for the Quartz Upgrade. The AI Priced It at $2,900 Installed."

## Journalist
Frank "The Foreman" DeLuca (project management & operations) — methodical, process-obsessed, thinks in timelines and critical paths. The article is fundamentally a sequencing decision: what must be decided before drywall vs. what can wait.

## Primary sources

### 1. ProBuilder — "Margin vs. Markup: The True Impact of Pricing Decisions on Home Builder Profits"
- URL: https://www.ProBuilder.com/business-management/article/55229316/margin-vs-markup-the-true-impact-of-pricing-decisions-on-home-builder-profits
- Key fact: large production builders target ~30% MARGIN on options/upgrades/design-center selections. A 30% margin = 42.85% markup on installed cost (margin is on sales price, markup on cost).
- Also: builders typically carry ~50% margin on the base house direct cost; options priced sloppily at 30% "markup" instead of 30% margin leak 3+ points of home margin.
- Implication: a $4,200 quartz option priced at a true 30% margin implies ~$2,940 installed cost.

## Original contribution: the options-vs-aftermarket audit table
Methodology: take the builder's published option price; divide by 1.4285 (reverse the 30% margin / 42.85% markup from ProBuilder) to estimate the builder's installed cost; compare against aftermarket installed cost ranges from Houzz median-spend data and Home Depot AI material pricing. Then sort each option into BUY NOW (structurally impossible or uneconomic later: 9-ft ceilings, bump-outs, 3-car garage extension, pre-wire/low-voltage, 220V/EV circuit, gas stub, egress windows, covered patio slab, upgraded electrical panel) vs BUY LATER (finishes cheaper aftermarket: quartz counters, LVP/hardwood, backsplash, cabinet hardware, light fixtures, closet systems, window treatments, paint upgrades).

Worked example (model): $550K base, $55K options at 10% (Shea benchmark).
- Options spend $55,000 at 30% margin → builder's installed cost ≈ $55,000 / 1.4285 ≈ $38,500; builder gross on options ≈ $16,500.
- If half the options ($27,500) are finishes that aftermarket would cost ~$20,000 installed (using builder's own ~$19,250 cost + normal contractor 30-40% margin ≈ $25-27K... careful: show the math honestly), the buyer saves ~$5-8K by deferring. Financing nuance: options roll into the mortgage (6-7% over 30 years); aftermarket is cash or HELOC — the true comparison is monthly payment vs. cash outlay, not sticker price.

## Strongest counterargument
The builder's option is not pure markup — it buys warranty integration (one throat to choke), schedule certainty (no post-close contractor hunt), and mortgage financing at ~6.5% instead of cash/HELOC at 8-10%. Houzz shows aftermarket remodeling is expensive ($24K median) and contractor scheduling is its own risk. For structural items there is no aftermarket at any price. The honest answer is a sort, not a boycott: buy the bones from the builder, buy the finishes later.

## Limitations
- The 30% margin target is from a ProBuilder trade article citing one large builder's division manager — not a published industry survey; actual option margins vary by builder and market (25-40%).
- No public dataset of builder option-sheet prices exists; the worked example is a model, not a measurement. Aftermarket costs vary wildly by metro.
- AI price-checking tools hallucinate prices; Home Depot's tool is pro-facing and material-only (no labor). The Etsy kit's prompts are only as good as the quotes fed in.
- Design-center credits as incentives (per Friedman) can change the math on inventory homes.

## Skepticism notes
- AI can't negotiate with the design consultant; it can only arm the buyer.
- Some builders prohibit post-close modifications during the warranty period for systems work — check the warranty docs.
- The "AI audit" framing is a workflow, not a product — no single tool does option-sheet OCR + aftermarket pricing end-to-end today.
