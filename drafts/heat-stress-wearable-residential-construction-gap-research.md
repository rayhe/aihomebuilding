# Research Notes: Heat Stress Wearables — The Residential Construction Gap

## Angle
Heat stress wearable monitors ($500/device) have eliminated heat injuries at every site they've been deployed — military bases, nuclear facilities, industrial plants. Residential construction accounts for 33-40% of all construction heat deaths but has essentially zero adoption of this technology. Meanwhile, NAHB (the national homebuilders' trade association) is actively lobbying Congress to kill even the basic OSHA heat standard that would require water breaks at 80°F. The technology that solves the problem costs less than a table saw.

## Journalist
Marcus "Steel" Washington — workforce, labor, human-centered investigation

## Key Data Points

### Heat Death Statistics
- **OSHA estimates:** 559 annual heat-related fatalities nationwide, 24,656 annual heat-related injuries across all industries (OSHA proposed rule, Aug 2024)
- **Construction's share:** 40%+ of heat-related worker deaths occur in construction (OSHA/ABC)
- **CPWR data:** In 2023, 18 of 55 (32.7%) recorded heat-related workplace deaths were in construction. In 2022: 17 of 43 (39.5%). Construction = 7% of total workforce.
- **2024 BLS CFOI:** 48 work-related deaths from environmental heat exposure; 7,100 DART cases in 2023-2024 combined; 5,280 DAFW cases
- **Monthly pattern:** July is deadliest month — 40 construction heat deaths from 2011-2023, followed by June (27), August (23) (CPWR)
- **State leaders:** Texas (25 construction heat deaths 2011-2023), California (13)
- **2024 total construction fatalities:** 1,034 (down from 1,075 in 2023) — rate 9.2/100K
- **GWU/Harvard 2025 study:** ~28,000 U.S. workplace injuries/year linked to heat

### OSHA Heat Rule Status (Critical Context)
- Biden proposed rule (Aug 2024): Initial trigger at 80°F heat index (water, shade, acclimatization), high trigger at 90°F (mandatory 15-min rest every 2 hours, monitoring)
- Trump admin: Regulatory Freeze Pending Review paused rule Jan 2025
- OSHA Heat NEP (National Emphasis Program) expired April 8, 2026
- Between April 2022 and Dec 2024: OSHA conducted ~7,000 heat-related inspections (up from ~200/year pre-NEP)
- NEP results: 60 General Duty Clause citations, 1,392 hazard alert letters, ~1,400 workers removed from hazardous heat
- Rep. Mark Messmer (R-Ind.) introduced Heat Workforce Standards Act of 2025 to repeal the rule
- **NAHB lobbied to kill the heat rule**, calling it "impracticable requirements" for residential construction
- Comment period extended to Oct 2025; rule has not been finalized, changed, or withdrawn as of mid-2026

### Wearable Technology — Proven Solutions
**SlateSafety (BAND V2)**
- Atlanta-based, founded 2016 by Georgia Tech engineers (Zack Braun, Tyler Sisk, Joe Boettcher)
- $500/device (includes software, cellular data, support). Bulk discounts available.
- Rental: $80/day, $160/week per unit
- Palm-sized armband worn on upper arm
- Measures: heart rate, skin temperature, exertion, core body temperature (validated within ±0.3°F vs ingestible pills)
- Revenue: $2-5M annually, up 65% YoY (as of mid-2025)
- 11 employees, $1.7M total funding
- **US Air Force result:** Texas base had 30+ serious heat injuries/year. After SlateSafety deployment in 2020: ZERO heat injuries.
- **Perrigo (NSC case study):** Zero reported heat incidents in 3 years since adoption
- **DOE Savannah River Site:** SRMC adopted SlateSafety for nuclear waste workers
- Sectors using: military, nuclear power, environmental remediation, steel construction, renewable energy, utilities

**Kenzen**
- Founded 2014
- Subscription per-worker per-month
- Arm-mounted sensors monitoring heart rate, skin/ambient temperature, activity
- Piloted at large industrial sites: construction, oil & gas, renewable energy
- Worker behavioral finding: Workers won't heed their own warnings on personal phones; they respond when supervisors alert them

**Market pricing range:** $200-$800 per device (constructionplacements.com)

### The Residential Gap
- Big commercial, military, nuclear, utilities use these wearable systems
- Residential construction: virtually zero adoption
- Residential construction subcontracting model = fragmented safety responsibility
- Small crews (3-8 workers typically on residential sites) — no dedicated safety manager
- NAHB itself argues the OSHA rule "imposes impracticable requirements that are not sufficiently tailored to... dynamic construction jobsites"
- The irony: NAHB opposes a regulation requiring water and shade while the technology that makes the regulation unnecessary costs $500

### Original Analysis
- Cost comparison: $500 SlateSafety device vs. $15,000-$50,000 average workers' comp claim for heat illness
- A 5-person residential framing crew: $2,500 to equip with heat monitors. One heat stroke incident typically costs $40K+ in direct medical + lost productivity
- 620 nonfatal heat injuries in construction with days away from work (2021-2022)
- Break-even: One prevented incident pays for 80+ devices

### Strongest Counterargument
- Privacy: Workers fear biometric surveillance / discriminatory employment decisions
- The GWU/Harvard agricultural worker study explicitly recommends: "raw physiological data should remain exclusively accessible to individual workers to privately manage their hydration and rest needs"
- Small residential builders can't afford the overhead of managing a connected-worker platform
- Many residential workers are day laborers or subcontractors without employer-provided safety equipment

### Limitations
- SlateSafety's zero-incident claims come from self-reported data and company PR, not independent peer-reviewed studies in residential settings
- OSHA's 559 annual fatality estimate has "considerable uncertainty" per their own filing
- BLS CFOI recorded only 48 heat deaths in 2024 — massive undercount acknowledged by all parties
- No published study has tested wearable heat monitors specifically on residential construction crews
- Residential construction heat death numbers are not separated from commercial in most datasets
