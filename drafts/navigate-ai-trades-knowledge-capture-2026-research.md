## Original contribution: the retirement pipeline arithmetic
ABC: 349,000 net new workers needed in 2026, "a majority" attributable to retirement → floor of 0.50 × 349,000 = **174,500 retirement-driven openings** (majority means strictly more than half, so this is a conservative floor).
ABC: registered apprenticeship completers FY2025 = **~40,000**.
40,000 / 174,500 ≈ **23%**: the entire national registered-apprenticeship pipeline's annual output covers less than a quarter of a single year's retirement-driven demand, before accounting for growth demand, non-retirement attrition, or the 456,000 needed in 2027.
Cross-check: Lightcast independently attributes ~40% of annual skilled-trades openings to retirements — same order of magnitude, different methodology.
Assumptions/limits: ABC's "majority" is qualitative, so 174,500 is a floor, not a point estimate; completers include nonunion (93% of RAPs) and union programs; some completers don't stay in construction; the 349,000 figure is ABC's model, sensitive to spending forecasts (Basu himself hedged it could prove conservative).

## Uncomfortable questions (Marcus's beat)
1. Who owns footage of a worker's hands doing 30 years of learned technique — the worker or the builder?
2. Once the master's knowledge is in the model, does the employer still need the master? (deskilling leverage)
3. The QC camera that catches defects before the crew leaves is also a camera that watches the crew all day. Who reviews that footage, and for what?
4. 93% of registered apprenticeship programs are nonunion and growing 18% since 2020 while union programs shrank 1.5% — AI coaching scales fastest where labor has the least bargaining power over its own data.
