# Research: Autonomous Earthmoving Equipment — The Residential Lot Gap

## Angle
Autonomous earthmoving machines now save $74,000+/year per machine and boost productivity 30% on infrastructure projects across 7 countries. But the residential homebuilder paying $20,000+ for site clearing and grading on a single lot will never see one. The technology exists. The economics don't.

## Kill Test
✅ Passes. If you're building a custom home and paying $20K-$60K for site prep, this explains why the technology that could cut your cost 30% isn't available to you — and what would need to change.

## The Gap Analysis

### Why autonomous earthmoving works for infrastructure but not residential:

1. **Utilization hours**: Infrastructure machines run thousands of hours/year. A residential lot prep takes 3-5 days (24-40 machine hours). At $74K/year savings, you need ~2,000+ hours of utilization to break even on the retrofit cost.

2. **Repetition vs. one-off**: Pipeline trenching is the same task repeated over 60 miles. Residential lots are each unique — different terrain, different constraints, different soil conditions. The AI's advantage (learning over time) is minimal when every job is different.

3. **Scale**: Infrastructure moves millions of cubic feet of earth. A residential lot moves hundreds to thousands. The precision advantage matters less at small scale.

4. **Constraints complexity**: Residential lots have neighboring structures, trees to preserve, utility lines, setback requirements, HOA restrictions. Infrastructure projects have wide-open terrain with few obstacles.

5. **Equipment size mismatch**: Gravis deploys on 36-ton excavators. Residential lot prep typically uses compact equipment (3-8 ton mini excavators, skid steers). The retrofit hardware and sensor suite may not scale down economically.

6. **Mobilization costs**: Moving autonomous equipment to a single residential lot, calibrating sensors, and setting up the GNSS base station could cost more than the grading job itself.

## Novel Contribution
Original calculation: At $20,000 average residential site prep cost and 30% productivity improvement, autonomous equipment could theoretically save $6,000 per lot. But with the retrofit hardware cost ($50K+ per machine estimated from $74K annual savings over ~3,000 hours utilization), a residential contractor would need to prep 8-10 lots per year with the same machine to break even — possible for a production builder doing 50+ lots/year, but not for a custom home GC doing 3-5 custom lots per year.

The residential adoption path would need to go through volume builders (D.R. Horton, Lennar, NVR) who do thousands of identical lots per year. But even they haven't adopted yet.

## Strongest Counterargument
Production homebuilders (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup) do thousands of lots per year with standardized site plans. They have the utilization to justify autonomous equipment. The fact that none have adopted it suggests either: (a) the ROI doesn't work even at scale, (b) the regulatory/insurance framework doesn't support it, or (c) grading subcontractors resist technology that threatens their business model.

## Limitations
- Gravis $74K/year savings figure is self-reported, not independently verified
- No published data on retrofit hardware cost per machine
- Residential lot prep cost data is from Angi/consumer estimates, not builder cost books
- No production builder has publicly discussed autonomous grading pilots
- ASI's data center/solar numbers aren't broken out separately
