# Research: The RFI Tax on a Custom Home — Every Question Costs $1,080
**Slug:** ai-rfi-tax-custom-home-question-cost-2026 | **Journalist:** Frank "The Foreman" DeLuca | **Date:** 2026-10-04

## Thesis
A Request for Information is the most boring document in construction and one of the most expensive: the Navigant Construction Forum's analysis of 1M+ RFIs across 1,362 projects puts the average processing cost at $1,080 per RFI, with ~800 RFIs on a typical commercial project and ~$800K in added costs and delays. Scaled to residential, a $650K custom home carries roughly a $7,000 formal RFI tax — and the real number is higher, because residential questions mostly travel by text message and phone call, never entering a log. The AI industry's answer is document-reading agents (Trunk Tools' TrunkRFI, SmartPM on Autodesk Forma) that kill the RFI already answered in the plans. The original contribution: scaling the Navigant 9.9-RFIs-per-$1M figure to custom-home math, the informal-question multiplier, the schedule-drag arithmetic against a 7.2-month residential build, and the residential pricing gap (enterprise AI, no public pricing, seven-figure deals — the same $50M-portfolio pattern as Buildots and ALICE).

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A GC or owner-builder learns (1) the RFI tax exists on residential jobs too and roughly what it costs per project, (2) which questions are preventable (already answered in the documents), (3) what the AI tools actually do and what they cost, and (4) the Monday-morning version that costs nothing: centralize the documents, log every question even the texted ones, and run pre-task document reviews.

## Original calculations (novel contribution)
- **Formal RFI tax on a custom home:** 9.9 RFIs per $1M × $650K home ≈ 6.4 formal RFIs × $1,080 ≈ **$6,900 in processing labor alone** — before a single day of delay is counted. Nobody budgets this line item; it hides inside the super's salary and the PM's evenings.
- **The informal multiplier:** residential RFIs rarely enter a log — they travel by text, call, and trailer conversation. Trunk Tools measured 36 minutes saved per document lookup; if a custom home generates 50-100 informal questions (a conservative count across a 7-month build with a dozen trades), that's **30-60 hours of super/PM time per project** spent answering questions the documents already answered, at a loaded cost of roughly $75/hr ≈ $2,250-$4,500.
- **Schedule drag:** US average RFI response 8 days (Hughes et al.). Census: average residential build 7.2 months (~155 working days). Five critical-path RFIs at 8 days each = 40 working days of serial drag — over a quarter of the build calendar — if they land in sequence. They usually overlap, but the one that lands on the critical path (the footing detail, the beam pocket) stops everything behind it.
- **The preventable fraction:** Gilbane's TrunkSubmittal data — 72% of submittals non-compliant — implies a large share of downstream questions are preventable: the answer was in the spec, the sub just didn't read it. SmartPM: 1 in 5 RFIs never gets a formal answer at all, which means 20% of the questions that cost $1,080 to ask evaporate into voicemail.
- **The residential pricing gap:** Trunk Tools publishes no pricing; Gilbane signed a seven-figure enterprise deal. Same pattern as Buildots and ALICE: the AI that kills RFIs is priced for $50M+ portfolios. A custom builder doing 8-12 homes a year cannot buy this — yet.

## AI angle (grounded, with skepticism)
- What AI actually does: TrunkRFI checks whether the question is already answered in the documents or prior RFIs before it becomes a formal RFI; auto-drafts the ones that are genuinely needed; converts a design-team response into trade impact + schedule impact + tasks. SmartPM's Forma agent maps open RFIs to the schedule activities they threaten. This is real capability aimed at the right bottleneck: most project truth still lives in 2D documents (ENR), and the AI finally reads them.
- Skepticism: Trunk Tools' 87% accuracy is a vendor case study on one project (Baird Center), validated by the project team — 13% wrong answers on a jobsite get installed, and installed wrong answers are rework. No public pricing means no independent ROI math exists for small builders. The 4-year training partnership was with the largest GCs, on commercial document sets — residential plan sets are thinner (often no specs at all), which is exactly where the AI has less to read. Gilbane's 50% cycle-time cut is vendor-reported.

## Strongest counterargument
RFIs are not waste — they are the paper trail that keeps everyone honest. A formal RFI with a written architect response is a legal document; a texted question answered verbally is a future dispute. The "preventable RFI" framing blames the asker when the disease is bad drawings: if the detail was clear, nobody would ask. An AI that kills questions can also kill documentation — and the liability for a wrong AI-drafted RFI, or a wrong AI answer the super relied on, is allocated nowhere in AIA A201 or any CGL policy (see the AI insurance-exclusion piece, #739: ISO's CG 40 47/48 exclusions took effect January 2026). The super who picks up the phone and asks the architect is doing quality assurance. Automate the lookup, fine — but the question itself is the job.

## Limitations
- Navigant/Hughes et al. 2013 data is 13 years old, commercial/infrastructure projects, 1,362 projects in the Aconex dataset — residential counts are my extrapolation (9.9/$1M) plus anecdotal; no published study counts RFIs on custom homes specifically.
- The 50-100 informal questions per custom home is an estimate, not a measured figure; the 36-min lookup saving is Trunk Tools' measured average on commercial projects.
- Trunk Tools accuracy (87%), rework avoidance ($100K/month), and Gilbane cycle-time cuts (50%) are vendor-reported; no independent audit exists.
- No public pricing for Trunk Tools, SmartPM, or Forma AI features — the residential ROI case cannot be closed with published numbers.
- AIA A201 §3.2.2/§4.2.12 summarized from secondary sources; the $200-$400 change-processing figure is ProBuilder (trade press).

## Actionable takeaways (required gate)
- **If you run $500K-$2M residential projects:** you are paying the RFI tax whether you log it or not. Start logging every question — including the texted ones — in one shared RFI log with dates. What gets measured gets answered.
- **The free version of TrunkRFI:** one complete, current plan set in the trailer (not three revisions deep in the truck); a 15-minute pre-task document review before each new trade mobilizes; a rule that no question gets asked twice — the answer goes in the log with the sheet number.
- **The math that justifies it:** preventing 10 RFIs saves ~$10,800 in processing labor (Navigant) plus the schedule latency. One prevented critical-path RFI at 8 days is worth more than the labor — it's the difference between October and December.
- **If you evaluate AI doc Q&A:** demo it on YOUR messiest plan set, not the vendor's; ask who owns a wrong answer contractually; ask what happens when there are no specs (most custom homes); get pricing in writing — "enterprise" means "you can't afford it" until proven otherwise.
- **For homeowners:** ask your builder how RFIs are tracked on your project. "We just call the architect" means your schedule lives in someone's voicemail. A builder with a written RFI log is a builder whose delays have names and dates.
