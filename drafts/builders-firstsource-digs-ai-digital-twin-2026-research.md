# Research: Builders FirstSource Bets $25.3M on Digs — The "CarFax for Your House"

## The news (primary source 1: Business Wire press release, Aug 25, 2026)
Builders FirstSource, Inc. (NYSE: BLDR) announced strategic partnership with Digs, an AI platform for homebuilders and homeowners.
- BFS is the solo lead investor on Digs' $25.3 million Series A financing.
- 5-year commercial agreement: accelerate product development, strengthen platform integration, expand AI capabilities.
- Goal: "single living source of truth" connecting plans, specifications, selections, products, approvals, warranties, conversations, and project history.
- For builders: every stakeholder works from same trusted information (estimating, purchasing, construction, design, sales, homeowner care).
- For homeowners: transparency during construction + "complete digital twin" delivering value long after move-in.
- Planned capabilities: AI chat, files, e-signatures, QR codes, comments, tasks, project collaboration; AI-powered diagramming, finish selections, visual coordination; AI-powered homeowner handoff, warranty, aftercare.
- Peter Jackson (BFS CEO): combining BFS's "scale, deep customer relationships, product data, and extensive digital ecosystem" with Digs' AI platform.
- BFS serves 140,000+ builder customers today.

## The product detail (primary source 2: HousingWire interview with Digs CEO Ryan Fink, Aug 25, 2026)
- Digs ingests disjointed info (plans, change orders, emails, texts, selections), uses patented AI to structure it into one uniform platform.
- Builders ask questions about the project: calculations, decisions, info contained in floor plans. Fink: "Think ChatGPT, but specifically trained on build data and it has access to your specific project."
- Takeoffs: "our AI can perform about 700 takeoffs in the time it takes one estimator to perform one."
- AI automates design-studio diagramming from takeoffs: potentially 80-90% time reduction on those tasks.
- Thesis: reduce cycle times, be more efficient and cost-effective.

## Context: the graveyard (skepticism ammunition)
- Katerra ($2B raised, collapsed 2021), Veev ($647M, collapsed 2023). Both tried to own the full-stack homebuilding tech dream. BFS is the nation's largest supplier of structural building products (LBM), so distribution is real — this is not a startup selling to nobody.
- Autodesk bought MaintainX for $3.6B (2026) — incumbents buying data + distribution to build AI products faster (per Inman Tech Roundup, Sep 2026).
- RICS AI in Construction Report 2026: ~two-thirds of construction professionals use AI in some form; regular AI use only 19% in construction. The gap between pilots and production is the story of this industry.

## Kill test: does this help someone building or buying a home?
Yes. Two concrete payoffs:
1. Homeowner buying from a builder on this platform gets an actual record of what went into the house — stud locations, plumbing runs, warranty terms, finish selections. That is worth real money at resale and during warranty claims. Today, homeowners lose that information by design; the builder's trailer gets demoed and the binders vanish.
2. Builder running $2-5M in residential work: automated takeoffs (700:1 speed claim) and structured project data mean fewer misorders, faster change orders, shorter cycle times. Worth testing the claims against, which this article does.

## Original contribution (required)
1. The cycle-time math: translate the "700 takeoffs per estimator-hour" claim into what that actually saves a mid-size builder on a 40-home subdivision. If an estimator takes ~2 hours per takeoff on a typical plan, AI at 700:1 implies the bottleneck was never takeoff time — it was data availability. Show the math and name what the claim does NOT imply (it doesn't fix framing labor or permit waits).
2. The resale value of a "CarFax": work through what a documented maintenance record plausibly adds at resale using the homeowner-record-keeping gap (NAR: most sellers can't document upgrades; agents estimate documented renos recover more). State assumptions openly.
3. Distribution leverage analysis: BFS's 140,000 builder customers vs. Digs' current "thousands of homes" — the adoption math. Even 1% conversion = 1,400 builders. This is what makes the partnership structurally different from past contech bets: the go-to-market already exists.

## Strongest counterargument
The best case against: builders have heard "one platform to rule them all" for 20 years (Procore, Buildertrend, CoConstruct, Hyphen...). AI search over messy project docs is genuinely useful, but it depends on data quality that job sites are terrible at producing. If the trades don't use it, the digital twin is a ghost — accurate on paper, empty in practice. And a supplier-owned platform raises an obvious question: whose data is it, and what happens to your project history if you switch lumber yards?

## Limitations
- SaaS pricing undisclosed. No per-home cost to model ROI for a specific builder.
- "700 takeoffs in the time of one" is a vendor claim from the CEO; no independent benchmark.
- The digital twin's value at resale is speculative — no resale-premium study exists for AI-maintained home records yet.
- The 5-year agreement's terms (revenue share, exclusivity, data rights) are undisclosed.
