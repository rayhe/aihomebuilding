# Research: COI Fraud Detection — AI Verification Gap in Residential Construction

## Core Thesis
AI platforms now verify 400,000+ certificates of insurance per month for commercial construction, catching forged documents, lapsed policies, and coverage gaps in seconds. Your residential contractor hands you a piece of paper that says "insured." Nobody checks if it's real.

## Key Data Points

### COI Verification Technology (Commercial)
- **TrustLayer:** Processes 400,000+ COIs/month across a network of ~300,000 companies. Uses AI confidence scoring to flag forged documents, tampered PDFs, falsified carrier signatures. (Source: TrustLayer press releases, Baret News May 2026)
- **BCS RiskBot AI:** Reviews certificates in 30 seconds vs. 30-minute manual review. Blocks deficient contractors before site access. (Source: getbcs.com, Feb 2026)
- **Manual COI review:** Takes ~30 minutes per certificate. 15-20 hours/week wasted for typical GC managing subcontractors. (Source: BCS blog)
- **Nomad Data Doc Chat:** Validates COIs across multilingual documents, checks endorsement forms, flags COI date gaps against project timelines. All with page citations.

### The ACORD Disclaimer Problem
- Standard ACORD Certificate of Liability Insurance explicitly states: "This certificate is issued as a matter of information only and confers no rights upon the certificate holder. This certificate does not affirmatively or negatively amend, extend or alter the coverage afforded by the policies below."
- Translation: The document that proves your contractor is insured literally says it doesn't prove your contractor is insured.
- Multiple court cases confirm COIs alone don't guarantee coverage (Tribeca Broadway v Mount Vernon Fire, 2004; Vivify Constr. v Nautilus Ins., 2018; Bucon v PA Mfg. Ass'n)
- NY appellate divisions split on whether insurers can be estopped from denying coverage based on a COI

### Residential Gap
- Commercial construction: GCs track and verify COIs for every sub, often with automated platforms
- Residential: Homeowner asks "are you insured?" Contractor says "yes." Maybe hands over a paper COI. Nobody verifies:
  - Is the policy actually active?
  - Does it cover this type of work?
  - Are the limits adequate?
  - Is the homeowner named as additional insured?
  - Does the policy have exclusions that eliminate the relevant trade hazard?
- If a subcontractor's workers' comp lapses and a worker falls on your property, the claim flows upstream — potentially to your homeowner's policy
- NH Insurance Department guide advises homeowners to verify contractor insurance by calling the agent — acknowledging the paper is unreliable
- Contractor misclassification (W-2 as 1099) shifts exposure and understates premium, potentially voiding coverage

### Legal Framework (Seyfarth Shaw, Q1 2026)
- AI tools on construction projects now influence schedules, estimates, submittals, safety reporting
- Contract framework governing AI tool outputs lags behind adoption
- Who relied on the output? Was it reviewed? Does the contract treat AI output as authoritative?
- No residential equivalent of these questions exists

### The Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. 
- Homeowner can verify their contractor's COI isn't just paper
- ewccv.com provides free workers' comp verification
- Requesting to be named as additional insured is free and provides real protection
- Understanding that a COI disclaimer means the paper itself is legally meaningless
