# Research: Construction AI Vaporware — Build vs. Buy Gap

## Angle
A $1B contractor's SVP of Technology built a cost history web app in four hours with AI and called the typical vendor demo "vaporware." Meanwhile, NAHB says 49% of builders claim to "use AI," but fewer than 5% use it for anything beyond email and marketing. The construction AI vendor market is full of API wrappers charging $500-2,000/month for what a determined builder can now produce in an afternoon. The question isn't whether AI works in construction — it's whether the tools being sold actually do what the demos promise.

## Key Data Points

### NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (2026)
- 49% of single-family builders say they use AI "in some capacity"
- 20% use it for marketing materials
- 11% for market analysis or project planning
- <5% use it for any of 10 other business functions
- Most of the 49% = ChatGPT for emails and social posts
- Source: NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI survey, cited in LinkedIn analysis by Grant Fuellenbach

### ServiceTitan 2026 State of AI in the Trades (1,032 contractors surveyed)
- 66% expect AI to bring moderate or major transformation in 1-3 years
- Only 12% have embedded AI into operations
- 34% are actively experimenting
- 62% of current AI users report measurable efficiency gains
- 74% cite efficiency as top expected benefit
- 44% cite lack of training as #1 barrier
- 44% cite integration complexity
- Only 18% cite employee resistance
- Source: ServiceTitan 2026 Reports (both commercial and residential)

### ServiceTitan Residential Report (1,000 residential contractors)
- 74% view AI as key to efficiency
- Only 25% currently using AI
- 48% of early adopters report increased productivity
- 45% report time savings
- 73% believe starting early creates competitive advantage
- Source: GlobeNewswire, April 7, 2026

### BuildOps Survey (600+ contractors, US & Canada)
- 78% using or testing AI
- 45% with AI budgets spend on outside consulting
- #1 barrier: training, not cost
- Source: BuildOps 2026 survey, cited in LinkedIn analysis

### McCownGordon Construction (Dustin Burns, SVP Technology)
- $1B+ revenue GC, scaled from 30 employees
- Built a cost history web app with AI in 4 hours (real cost data, region benchmarking, conceptual estimates, one HTML file)
- His verdict: "And it is vaporware" — indistinguishable from expensive vendor demos
- AI automates 80% of traditional estimating quantification time
- Looks for companies "building their own models, not the ones wrapping someone else's API"
- Source: Bricks & Bytes podcast (Jul 16, 2026); McCownGordon website; Procore podcast

### NAHB BuilderBooks — "AI in Residential Construction" (246 pages)
- Author: Grace Tsao Mase (Yale-trained architect, 3 US patents, multi-state contractor's license)
- Claims builders using AI can complete projects up to 30% faster
- Released at 2026 IBS, became one of top-selling titles
- Covers: estimating, bookkeeping, design visualization, client communication, workforce management
- Source: NAHB press release, March 17, 2026; builderbooks.com

### Retool Build vs. Buy Shift Report (817 builders surveyed)
- 35% of teams have already replaced at least one SaaS tool with a custom build
- 78% plan to build more custom tooling in 2026
- SaaS pricing hasn't adjusted to AI-era cost reductions
- Source: VentureBeat, Retool 2026 report

### Construction Industry Context
- Residential construction lost 43,600 jobs over past 12 months (11th consecutive annual decline)
- NAHB economic damage estimate: $10.8 billion/year from labor shortage
- Construction remains one of least R&D-intensive sectors in US economy
- Only 20% of contractors run on a single platform (Franken-stack problem)
- Source: NAHB, HousingWire analysis

### Notable Vendor Claims
- All3 (European robotics): $25M seed, claims 30% cost savings, 50% timeline reduction
- Buildxact: AI estimating with live material pricing
- Multiple vendors claiming AI quantification, scheduling, project management
- Katerra ($2B failure) and Veev ($647M failure) loom as cautionary tales

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing the NAHB "49% use AI" claim against ServiceTitan's breakdown of what "using AI" actually means reveals that most builders claiming AI adoption are using general-purpose chatbots for writing tasks, not construction-specific operational tools. The McCownGordon case study provides a concrete, verifiable example of the build-vs-buy gap: the same functionality vendors charge hundreds/month for can be produced by a technical leader in hours. The novel insight is that construction AI's adoption problem isn't builders resisting technology — it's that the vendor market is selling the wrong products to the wrong segment.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — builders reading this will learn to distinguish operational AI (worth paying for) from writing-assistant AI (already free), and will have a framework for evaluating whether to build custom tools or buy vendor products. Homebuyers benefit indirectly by understanding which builders are actually using technology vs. just claiming to.
