# Research Notes: AI Hallucinations in Construction Documents

## Angle
AI tools are now generating construction specs, scopes of work, material schedules, code citations, and contract clauses. When those outputs hallucinate — fabricated product model numbers, wrong code sections, miscalculated loads — the errors don't stay on paper. They enter the build pipeline and become physical defects. The legal framework for liability is wide open: standard construction contracts (AIA, JCT, NEC) have zero AI provisions. Nobody knows who pays when an AI-generated spec causes a $40,000 rework.

## Journalist
Catherine "Code" Chen — policy, legal, building codes

## Kill Test
If you're a homeowner: your contractor may be using AI to write the documents that govern how your house gets built. You should know that, and you should know what to ask. If you're a builder: you're liable for AI-generated errors the same way you're liable for your own errors, even if the AI made the mistake. Your E&O policy may not cover it.

## Primary Sources

### ConsensusDocs (construction industry trade org)
- "Generative AI tools like ChatGPT are now being used to draft key project documents like scopes of work, RFIs, meeting notes, and change order narratives"
- "They sometimes generate false, misleading, or fabricated information without any clear warning"
- "An AI-generated scope of work may mistakenly rely on outdated regulatory requirements resulting in costly compliance issues"
- "These AI tools will present incorrect information with confidence, making errors difficult to catch"
- Source: https://www.consensusdocs.org/news/the-hidden-risks-of-using-ai-on-construction-proje/

### Multi-Housing News (July 2026)
- "AI hallucinations can lead to construction defects and legal problems as teams automate tasks that were previously manual"
- "Contractors and estimators are using AI to create proposals, estimates and blueprints. Engineers and consultants are embracing AI to generate renderings, specifications, designs and plans."
- "Builders cannot plug AI into their projects and deem the results risk-free"
- Source: https://www.multihousingnews.com/ai-in-construction-builder-beware/

### GIRI (Get It Right Initiative) — UK construction quality body
- "In construction, we cannot afford hallucinations"
- "We can't have a risk assessment or method statement generated by AI that contains a mistake which could cause harm"
- Recommends 4 safeguards: risk assessments before applying AI, chain-of-thought reasoning, human-in-the-loop, "assurative AI" (knowledge graphs + automated checks)
- "Most big contractors are using it a bit now — co-pilots, small pilots — but not much beyond that"
- PAS 4010 productivity standard due 2026
- Source: https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/ai-artificial-intelligence-construction-defects-management/

### Bernstein Shur — Construction law firm (Feb 2026)
- AI can facilitate "algorithmic bid rigging" through price normalization
- Uploading plans/specs into AI platforms likely exceeds IP license scope, creates unauthorized derivative works
- Copyright risk: "Architects and engineers typically retain copyright ownership of their design documents"
- Source: https://www.bernsteinshur.com/insights-events/the-hidden-legal-risks-of-using-artificial-intelligence-in-construction/

### Suffolk Law Journal — "Prepare Your Forms for Battle: AI Construction Claims"
- "If AI generates a faulty plan, the contractor likely remains liable because they chose to rely on the tool"
- "Using AI does not lower that standard. Courts expect contractors to evaluate their tools before relying on them."
- EU AI Liability Directive would allow harmed parties to bring claims against AI developers
- Source: https://sites.suffolk.edu/jhtl/2025/10/15/prepare-your-forms-for-battle-ai-construction-claims/

### Construction Dive — "Why ChatGPT (still) needs a construction lawyer" (2026)
- Attorney: "Oh my God, no. I would be terrified if I heard of anybody using it to generate a contract"
- ChatGPT-generated construction contract for 600-unit mixed-use: "could be legally enforceable" but "missing clauses and poor risk management"
- Source: https://www.constructiondive.com/news/why-chatgpt-still-needs-a-construction-lawyer/806320/

### HCR Law — International construction AI analysis (May 2026)
- UK standard form contracts (JCT, NEC) "were drafted on the assumption that project decisions are made by human professionals"
- "Currently, they do not address the use of AI systems or allocate risk where project outcomes are influenced by algorithmic tools"
- "Standard forms provide no guidance on liability allocation in these circumstances"
- Potentially responsible parties: contractor deploying AI, data providers, technology provider, those reviewing output
- Source: https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/artificial-intelligence-and-construction-contracts/

### MDPI Academic Paper (June 2026) — "Governing Data Integrity Risks in Digital Construction"
- Table mapping traditional tort elements vs. AI disruption: "AI outputs are dynamic, probabilistic, and affected by model updates, retrieval contexts, and prompt interactions"
- "Proximate cause requires prompt logs, model versions, audit trails, and data-origin verification"
- "Damage recognition may lag behind the original data corruption, complicating limitation periods and evidence"
- Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/13/2577

### Legal Precedent Context
- Sullivan & Cromwell (April 2026): filed AI-fabricated citations in federal court
- Ninth Circuit: suspended two attorneys 6 months for AI-generated hallucinations in filings
- Mata v. Avianca (2023): first major AI hallucination case
- Fabricated-citation count in legal filings: past 1,450 documented cases
- If top law firms can't catch AI hallucinations, construction firms won't either

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing: no standard construction contract (AIA A201, ConsensusDocs 200, JCT, NEC4) addresses AI-generated outputs, yet AI is already drafting project documents. The liability gap is real and measured — E&O insurers are already writing exclusions for AI-generated work (covered in our article #579), but builders are using the tools anyway. The gap between adoption and legal protection is the story.

## Limitations
- No dataset exists quantifying how many construction documents are AI-generated
- No reported court case yet where an AI-generated construction spec caused a physical defect (cases exist in legal filings, not construction outcomes)
- GIRI safeguards are recommendations, not mandated standards
- Most evidence is from commercial/multi-family construction; residential data is even thinner
