# Research: AI-Designed Multigenerational Suites — The Two-Kitchen Home

## Thesis
Multigenerational living is now the default American household form — nearly 1 in 5 Americans (18%) live in one, per Pew. Production builders have responded architecturally (Lennar's Next Gen "home within a home" since 2011), and NAR reports 14% of 2025 buyers purchased a multigenerational home. The design problem is genuinely architectural, not just square footage: two kitchens in one envelope triggers code reclassification, privacy demands acoustic separation, and every wet room placed far from the plumbing stack adds thousands. AI floor-plan generators (Maket.ai-class tools, $0–$50/plan) can now explore dozens of suite layouts with these constraints baked in — the $3,000–$10,000 architect's sketch phase collapses to a free exploration. But no AI tool checks the kitchenette-vs-kitchen code line that decides whether the suite is still "single family" in your jurisdiction.

## Kill test
**Does this help someone building or buying a home?**
- Buyers: YES — the suite cost/payback math (national ~$83K vs. rent/childcare offsets) decides buy-vs-build; the kitchenette-vs-kitchen distinction decides whether your "suite" is legal in single-family zoning.
- Builders: YES — Next Gen-style suites are a spec'd product line now; the plumbing-stack-adjacency rule and the interior lockable door are concrete design moves with costs attached.

## Original contribution / angle
- **The kitchenette-vs-kitchen code line (the architectural crux nobody prices):** A "kitchenette" (wet bar, microwave, undercounter fridge, no range/cooktop) keeps the suite inside single-family zoning in many jurisdictions; a full second kitchen with a range triggers ADU or duplex reclassification — parking minimums, setback rules, separate utility connections. AI generators that offer "add second kitchen" as a one-click feature don't flag this. The cheapest AI exploration should start from the code line, not from the layout.
- **Suite payback math (worked example):** 450 sq ft attached suite at the national mid-range (~$185/sq ft from Sage calculator ADU band, ≈ $83K, matching Angi's $82,750 average) vs. the offsets it replaces — parent's $2,000/mo apartment ($24K/yr ≈ 3.5-yr simple payback) or full-time daycare $1,300/mo ($15.6K/yr ≈ 5.3-yr). In LA at $175K+, the same math needs both offsets to clear.
- **The plumbing-stack rule:** every wet room (suite bath + kitchenette) lands within ~10–15 ft of the main stack, or rough-in plumbing (~$6,500, HomeAdvisor) multiplies. Generative tools let you test ten stack-adjacent layouts in an hour; an architect's revision cycle is days each.
- **Lennar's interior lockable door as design pattern:** the single architectural move that makes multigenerational work — a door that modulates between "one house" and "two households." AI suites should generate with it, not as an afterthought.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- **Buyers/builders:** Before drawing anything, call your planning department and ask where the kitchenette-vs-kitchen line is in your zoning — it decides suite legality, ADU fees, and parking requirements. Design from that answer.
- **Buyers:** Use Maket.ai-class generators ($0–$50/plan) to explore 15+ suite layouts with constraints you write down first: separate entrance, bathroom stacked on the main plumbing wall, STC-rated party wall, zero-threshold entries. Pay an architect ($2,400–$14,000) to verify and permit the best one — the AI does exploration, not construction documents.
- **Builders:** Spec the interior lockable door + stacked wet rooms + mini-split-ready electrical as standard in multigenerational plans; they cost hundreds now and decide resale later.
- **Red flag:** Any plan tool that drops in a full second kitchen without asking about your zoning district. The range is a legal device, not an appliance.

## Skepticism notes / limitations
- Cost figures (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Sage) are national averages with enormous regional spread ($83K national vs. $175K+ in LA) — treat as order-of-magnitude, and LA buyers need local bids.
- The "second kitchen triggers ADU reclassification" claim is jurisdiction-dependent; some cities are permissive, some prohibit second kitchens outright. The point is the variance, not a universal rule — and that AI tools ignore it.
- Maket.ai/Planner 5D-class tools produce exploration sketches, not permit documents (IRC egress, structural, MEP gaps documented in our #45 article). No claim here that AI output is buildable as-generated.
- Payback math is simple (not discounted cash flow), ignores financing cost, and assumes the offset (rent/daycare) is real — a parent moving in who wasn't renting elsewhere changes the math.
- NAR's multigenerational buyer share fell 14% (down from 17%) even as Realtor.com shows owner-occupied multigenerational households growing — the two metrics measure different things (buyer cohort vs. household stock); don't conflate.

## Strongest counterargument
The market may be smaller than the trend stories suggest. NAR's multigenerational buyer share dropped from 17% to 14% in a year; multigenerational homes carry a price premium that undercuts the "cost savings" motive (USA Today/Realtor.com 2025); and the architectural reality is that a well-designed suite is just an expensive addition — 41% of these purchases are about caregiving, not investment, so payback math is irrelevant to most of the demand. AI layout generation is also the wrong end of the problem: the binding constraint is zoning and permitting (second-kitchen rules, ADU fees, utility connection charges), which no generator evaluates. An AI that draws 30 floor plans for a suite your city won't permit is a toy, not a tool. And multigenerational design done badly — a "suite" that is really a windowless basement bedroom with a hot plate — creates the caregiver burnout and family conflict the arrangement was supposed to solve.
