# Research: Your Finished House Can't Get Power Because a Data Center Is First in Line

## Thesis
Data centers aren't just stealing your electrician (article #565 covered that). They're claiming the grid itself. Across the country — and most dramatically in London, Nevada, Virginia, and Wisconsin — new housing developments are being told to wait years for electrical connections because data center load has consumed available grid capacity. The interconnection queue, once an obscure utility planning concept, is now a direct barrier to building homes. Homebuyers may also end up subsidizing the transmission lines that serve these facilities.

## Journalist
**Elena Vasquez** (Architecture & Design) — Frame this through the spatial absurdity: a house that is designed, permitted, built, and finished... but cannot be occupied because the grid has no capacity to connect it. The architecture is ready. The wires aren't.

## Differentiation from Existing Coverage
- Article #565 (data-center-electrician-drain): Labor shortage angle — data centers poach electricians from residential projects
- energy-model-rate-assumption-research: Electricity PRICE impact — rate assumptions in HERS models are wrong
- vpp-home-battery-data-center-revenue-research: Home batteries as revenue source for data center grid services
- **THIS article:** Physical grid CAPACITY — the literal inability to get a power connection because data centers have claimed all available interconnection slots and transmission capacity. Different problem, different mechanism, different timeline.

## Key Data Points & Primary Sources

### 1. London Housing Delayed by Grid Capacity (BBC / London Assembly, Dec 2025)
- London Assembly Planning and Regeneration Committee report: some new housing developments in west London (Ealing, Hillingdon, Hounslow) **temporarily delayed** after electricity grid reached full capacity
- Some completed projects told they would have to **"wait until 2037"** to get a grid connection
- Committee chair James Small-Edwards: energy capacity had become a "real constraint" on housing and economic growth
- Fears boroughs might have to **"pause new housing altogether"** until resolved
- GLA found short-term fixes with National Grid and Ofgem, but several projects still set back
- UK data center demand projected to rise from 10 TWh to 71 TWh between 2025-2050
- 100+ data centers planned for UK, half near London
- Source: BBC via London Assembly report; TechRadar coverage (https://www.techradar.com/pro/data-centers-are-throttling-home-building-as-infrastructure-struggles-to-keep-up-with-ai-demands)

### 2. Nevada: 49,000 Lake Tahoe Residents Losing Power to Data Centers (Electrek / Fortune, May 2026)
- NV Energy told Liberty Utilities it will **stop providing power after May 2027** — redirecting capacity to Google, Apple, Microsoft data centers near Tahoe-Reno Industrial Center
- Data centers consumed **22% of Nevada's electricity** in 2024; could rise to **35% by 2030**
- 12 data center projects in Northern Nevada alone: **5,900 MW** of new demand by 2033 (Desert Research Institute analysis)
- 75% of major-project load growth attributed to data centers (NV Energy 2024 filing)
- 49,000 residential customers competing against major utilities and data center operators for wholesale power — "zero leverage"
- Liberty Utilities asked California regulators for emergency procurement authorization
- Source: Electrek (https://electrek.co/2026/05/13/data-centers-grid-strain-driving-residential-solar-battery-demand/), Fortune

### 3. Wisconsin: $2 Billion in Data Center Transmission — Who Pays? (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, Jul 24 2026)
- American Transmission Co. planning **$2+ billion** in new high-voltage power lines, substations for data centers
- Microsoft Mount Pleasant campus (former Foxconn site): $625 million in transmission projects already approved
- Vantage Data Centers Port Washington: **$1.5 billion** high-voltage power line pending approval
- Meta Beaver Dam data center also requires infrastructure
- ATC charges costs to utilities like We Energies → passed to residential customers through energy bills
- We Energies earmarked **$620 million** in transmission charges for data centers in 2027-2028
- New ATC filing: first attempt to **directly assign data center transmission costs** to tech companies
- Marquette University Law School July poll: **75% of Wisconsin voters** view data centers unfavorably (up from 55% in October)
- Citizens Utility Board of Wisconsin: "ensuring customers aren't left holding the bag for billions of costs for new power lines"
- Source: Milwaukee Journal Sentinel (https://www.jsonline.com/story/money/business/energy/2026/07/24/will-residential-customers-have-to-pay-for-data-center-power-lines/91024148007/)

### 4. National Grid Capacity Crisis — The Numbers (Multiple sources, Jul 2026)
- **Data centers to triple US electricity share:** 4.4% (2023) → 12% (2028)
- **Data centers drove half of all US electricity demand growth** in 2025
- **Virginia:** Data centers consume >25% of all kWh generated in the state; 398 operational data centers + 287 more planned (Pew)
- **PJM capacity auction:** $28.92/MW-day (2024-25) → $329.17/MW-day (2026-27) — **nearly 10× increase** in two years
- **Grid interconnection queue:** 23.1 GW of data center capacity under construction globally across 831 sites; 73.5% in the Americas; 3-4 year grid connection delays in PJM and ERCOT (Energy Central)
- **National average residential rate:** 17.45¢/kWh (Jan 2026), +9.5% YoY
- **Dominion Energy (Virginia):** first base-rate increase since 1992 — +$8.51/month in 2026
- Utility capital spending: **$1.4 trillion** planned over 5 years (up 20% from last year), 51 investor-owned utilities (PowerLines)
- **NERC:** peak demand to rise 24% over next 10 years
- **Electricity prices up ~40%** between 2020-2025; expected to climb another 10-30% by 2030 depending on data center concentration
- Source: WSJ, Electrek, Le Monde (https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/07/27/in-the-us-opposition-to-data-centers-is-growing-and-becomes-political_6755898_19.html)

### 5. Political Backlash — Moratoriums and Legislation (Le Monde / Multiple, Jul 2026)
- **New York:** First state to enact data center moratorium (July 14, 2026) — halts construction on facilities consuming ≥50 MW
- **Data Center Watch:** 75 major projects blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 alone — **as many as all of 2025**
- **300 bills** introduced across US to legislate data centers
- **Gallup (May 2026):** 70% of Americans opposed construction of a data center near their homes — higher than nuclear power plants (53%)
- **Trump Ratepayer Protection Pledge (July 23, 2026):** nonbinding promise that AI companies cover infrastructure costs; covers 80% of US electricity delivery
- Reality: experts say household utility bills will continue rising through at least 2030
- **Goldman Sachs:** AI capex $755 billion in 2026, $920 billion in 2027 — nearly 1/3 of US economic growth in 2026
- Source: Le Monde, Reuters

### 6. The Grid Emergency — Real-Time Evidence (Jul 2026)
- **DOE Emergency Order 202-26037** (July 26, 2026): authorized Southwest Power Pool to use specific generation units during 17-state heat wave
- SPP under Conservative Operations Advisory since July 20 — "higher than normal loads and outages, seasonally hot temperatures, and the potential for lower wind and solar"
- PJM planning emergency auction post-summer to ensure data centers pay for required generation
- Source: Daily Caller, Bloomberg/Communications Today

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **YES.** If you're building a new home in Virginia, Nevada, or parts of the PJM footprint, your electrical interconnection timeline may be months or years longer than expected. If you're buying in a data center corridor, your utility bills are rising faster than any energy model predicted. If your development's grid connection is delayed, understanding why — and what your builder or utility should be doing about it — is directly actionable.

## Actionable Insights for Homebuilders/Buyers
1. **Check your utility's interconnection queue** before committing to a building timeline. In PJM states, 3-4 year delays are now standard for large connections.
2. **Ask your builder about grid capacity** in your development's service area. Completed homes waiting for power connections is no longer a theoretical risk.
3. **Factor in rate trajectory, not current rates.** Energy models using 2023-2024 rate assumptions in data center corridors are already wrong by 10-20%.
4. **Pre-wire for solar + storage.** In Nevada, Lake Tahoe homeowners who went solar before the crisis have options; those who didn't are scrambling.
5. **Watch for transmission cost pass-throughs.** Wisconsin's ATC case is precedent-setting — if it fails, residential customers in data center corridors pay for power lines they'll never use.
6. **Check local moratorium status.** If your jurisdiction has passed or is considering a data center moratorium, understand the grid capacity implications for your build timeline.

## Strongest Counterargument
Data center advocates argue their industry drives "long-overdue investments" in the grid that benefit everyone. The White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge commits hyperscalers to covering their infrastructure costs. And some data centers are building behind-the-meter generation, reducing grid dependency. The investment in grid modernization could ultimately benefit residential customers through improved reliability — if cost allocation is handled properly.

## Limitations
- London data is from a Dec 2025 Assembly report; some delays may have been resolved by short-term fixes
- Nevada situation is evolving; Liberty Utilities emergency procurement ruling not yet issued
- Wisconsin ATC proposal is newly filed; FERC has 60 days to respond; outcome uncertain
- Rate projections (10-30% increase by 2030) depend on multiple variables beyond data centers
- The interconnection queue problem affects large-load commercial connections more directly than individual residential service; the residential impact is primarily through delayed developments and rate increases, not individual homeowner connection queues
