# Research Notes — AI Trench/Shoring Vision Safety (#891)
Slug: `ai-trench-shoring-vision-fatality-watch-2026`
Journalist: Marcus "Steel" Washington (workforce & labor, investigative)
Date: 2026-09-17

## Angle
Three of every four trench-collapse deaths happen on residential jobsites. OSHA runs a "zero tolerance" trench enforcement program with criminal referrals. AI vision systems (DroneDeploy Safety AI, EarthCam, Protex-class tools) can now flag unshored trenches, missing egress, and absent competent persons from site cameras. The article: what the data says about who dies and why, what shoring actually costs vs. what a citation costs, and whether a camera can substitute for the competent person who was absent 86% of the time.

## Kill test
Passes. Helps: homebuilders/GCs (avoid fatalities, fines, criminal referrals), subcontractors and crews (the people in the trench), and homeowners (hiring a contractor who digs — you can get named in the fallout; and this is where 3 in 4 deaths happen).

## Key facts for the piece
- OSHA 1926.652: protective system required in excavations 5+ feet deep (sloping, shoring, or shielding); spoil piles and equipment kept 2+ feet from edge; ladder/stairway/ramp within 25 feet of lateral travel; competent person must inspect daily and as conditions change.
- Trench death trajectory: 39 → 15 → 12 (2022–2024), but 11 already in 2025 (per July 2025 OH&S).
- 2022 OSHA national emphasis program: inspectors can open an inspection on any observed unprotected trench.
- Cost math (original contribution): small aluminum trench box = $400/week. One serious OSHA citation = $16,550 (2026 max) = ~41 weeks of rental. One willful citation = $165,514 = ~414 weeks. The Jarrell fine ($250,272) equals ~625 weeks of trench box rental — 12 years of boxes for a 2-week sewer job. A 2-week residential sewer-lateral trench needs the box for 2 weeks: ~$800 in rental vs. the statistical cost of going without.
- AI cost: EarthCam/Sitemetric-class AI cameras are per-camera subscription services (verify pricing at draft; do not state unverified numbers). DroneDeploy Safety AI analyzes captured imagery against violation categories.
- The "competent person" gap: 86% absent at fatal incidents — the article's central tension is whether an always-on camera can stand in for the human who wasn't there. OSHA requires a *human* competent person with authority to stop work; no regulation recognizes AI as a substitute.

## Strongest counterargument
Cameras watch workers, not just trenches. Continuous AI surveillance of small residential crews (42% of fatal-incident employers had <10 workers) raises consent and retaliation issues; unions and worker advocates argue safety data can become discipline data. False positives create alert fatigue on jobsites where the super is also the excavator operator. And the worst failure mode: a builder leans on the camera, the camera misses a changing soil condition after rain (68% of fatalities had environmental contributing factors), and the AI log becomes evidence the builder "knew." Detection is not protection — a camera cannot shore a wall.

## Limitations (dedicated section in article)
- Fatality counts lag: 2024 figure (12) was partial-year; 2025 (11) is July YTD; BLS CFOI final counts take ~2 years.
- Could not verify per-site pricing for DroneDeploy Safety AI, EarthCam AI, or Sitemetric — treated as "subscription camera services" without stated prices.
- The 70% decline (2022→2024) coincides with intensified enforcement AND outreach; cannot attribute shares to enforcement vs. reporting vs. AI monitoring.
- BLS-derived residential-share stat (3 in 4) comes via a safety-center compilation, not a directly linked BLS table.
- Trench box rental rates are 2026 market snapshots (Phoenix/Charlotte + one Ohio vendor), not national averages.
