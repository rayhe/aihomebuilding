# Research: Skylights — The Hole in the Roof, the Light It Buys, and the Flashing That Decides

**Topic:** Skylight leak forensics, curb-vs-deck flashing, the energy math of a roof hole, and the re-roof timing rule
**Journalist:** Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design)
**Kill test:** PASS — a skylight decision during a re-roof or remodel is a $1,500-$5,000 commitment that either delivers the best daylight in the house or a ceiling stain shaped like a continent. A buyer or builder choosing curb vs deck, timing vs standalone, and knowing the condensation wipe-test is a real money decision.

## Core narrative
A skylight is a hole cut in the most abused surface of the house, and everything about whether it succeeds lives in the flashing, not the glass. VELUX, the category's dominant manufacturer, rebuilt its product line around a "No Leak" promise with three layers of water protection and a 10-year installation warranty because leak perception was the category's number-one problem. The failure taxonomy is well understood: the curb termination fails, the flashing was site-built instead of kitted, or the "leak" is condensation on the coldest surface in the room. Meanwhile the daylight dividend is real and unusually well measured: Heschong Mahone's field studies found skylighting delivered 98% of predicted savings while half of window daylighting retrofits failed outright. Elena angle: light as a building material, the light well as architecture, and an unsentimental accounting of what the hole costs in heat.

## Primary sources (8)
1. **VELUX "No Leak Skylight" system** (Metal Architecture, 2023; Streamline Enterprises; SolarTown) — redesigned deck/curb line after 3 years R&D; three layers of water protection: (1) preattached deck seal (closed-cell foam steel bracket) on deck mounts / pre-attached gasket on curb mounts, (2) adhesive all-weather underlayment in every box, (3) engineered flashing; 10-year installation warranty + 20 years on glass + 10 years on product + 5 years blinds/controls; VELUX flashing kit REQUIRED to qualify for the 10-year installation warranty; president Timothy B. Miller: leak perception was "the number one concern" from consumers and trade; existing VELUX units "when properly installed, don't leak" but "older plastic bubble skylights have" leaked and stigmatized the category. Flashing kits $221-$461 (EDW tile, EDM metal; EDL shingle, ECB curb). 75+ years engineering per company.
2. **Triple-I water damage statistics** (Insurance Information Institute via 2019-2023 ISO data) — water damage/freezing is 22.6% of all homeowners claims (2nd after wind/hail); ~1 in 67 insured homes files per year; average claim $15,400 (2019-2023), up from $13,954 (2018-2022); industry pays ~$13B/year; water damage carries the HIGHEST claim denial rate (~10%) because "sudden and accidental" is covered but gradual seepage/neglect is not. Skylight leaks that develop slowly live exactly on this denial boundary.
3. **IRC / IECC code** — R308.6 (unit skylights); R308.6.8-type provisions: unit skylights on roof pitch flatter than 3:12 must sit on a curb extending at least 4" above the roof plane (unless manufacturer instructions say otherwise); R903.2.2: unit skylights installed per R308.6 and flashed per manufacturer instructions are EXEMPT from the cricket/saddle requirement that applies to chimneys/penetrations over 30" wide; R905.2.8 flashing (sidewall step flashing minimum 4"x4"); IECC Table N1102.1: maximum skylight U-factor 0.65 (climate zone 3), 0.60 (zones 4-8) — notably laxer than the 0.30-0.40 required of vertical fenestration.
4. **Heschong Mahone Group daylighting studies** (1999, for PG&E / California Board for Energy Efficiency) — 108 nearly-identical retail stores: skylighted stores showed statistically compelling higher sales; Capistrano schools: students in the most-daylit classrooms progressed 20% faster in math and 26% faster in reading over one year; field follow-ups: skylighting (top-lighting) delivered 98% of design-predicted savings and ~80% lighting-energy savings in daylit areas, while 50% of window (side-lighting) applications were not working at all. Skylights are the most predictable daylighting strategy measured.
5. **NRCA best practice via contractor reporting** (Kanga Roof, Sept 2026; Four Seasons) — NRCA, GAF, CertainTeed guidance: replace skylights DURING roof replacement; standalone skylight replacement costs 30-50% more than doing it with a re-roof; rule of thumb $1,500-$2,500 with re-roof vs $3,000-$5,000 standalone (Detroit market, 2026).
6. **Thermal drone roof inspection** (TechBullion; DroneXL, March 2026) — thermal cameras flown in the evening cooling cycle read subsurface moisture: "a raised temperature pattern near a skylight curb may signal saturated insulation or a bad flashing detail"; drone surveys cut residential inspection from 60-90 min to 15-30 min. Counterpoint: insurers now scan roofs with AI without notice; United Policyholders' Amy Bach: "A grainy aerial image might flag a skylight as damaged roofing material"; CA 2025 legislation would require insurer notification before aerial-data underwriting.
7. **ENERGY STAR / NFRC / energy.gov** — ENERGY STAR certified skylights can cut heating/cooling bills up to 13% vs non-certified; U-factors range 0.2-1.2 Btu/(h·ft²·°F); guidance: north-facing glazing wants the lowest U-factor affordable; "Even certified windows can suffer condensation in cold weather... that is the dew point showing up on the coldest surface in the room. It is information, not a defect."
8. **Failure-mode field reporting** (County Roofing Systems, 2026; Sunsquare Skylights) — "The curb, not the glass, is where most leaks start": membrane termination at the curb top is the single most common flat-roof skylight failure; sealant dries, termination bar loosens, membrane pulls at corners; condensation is "mistaken for a leak constantly"; fogging between panes = failed IGU seal, not intrusion; curb mounts are "more foolproof," required on low slope; deck mounts are sleeker with shorter light shafts but "highly dependent on sealants" and harder to replace (surrounding shingles must come off).

## Contradictory/context sources (skepticism required)
- **The building-science position:** a skylight is a penetration through the roof's water, air, vapor, and thermal control layers simultaneously — four control layers compromised at one detail. Every additional skylight is another future leak location. Lstiburek's school treats roof penetrations as guilty until proven innocent.
- **VELUX's own admission:** the No Leak line exists because the category was "stigmatized" by leaking plastic bubble skylights — the warranty is as much a marketing repair as an engineering one. The 10-year installation warranty requires VELUX flashing; use another kit and the headline warranty evaporates.
- **Heschong is 1999, commercial:** retail stores and schools, not homes; fluorescent-era lighting baselines; residential daylighting value is real but the 20-26% school figures should not be quoted as residential outcomes.
- **Insurer AI:** the same aerial-imagery tech that can find your failing flashing can also non-renew your policy over a misidentified skylight. Homeowners in most states have no right to see the images.
- **Water damage stats are all-cause:** Triple-I's $15,400 average covers burst pipes, appliances, and seepage — not skylight-specific. Use as the cost of being wrong about water, not as the cost of a skylight leak.

## Original contribution for this article
**The hole-penalty math:** a 2x4-ft (8 sq ft) skylight at U-0.55 against a 40°F indoor-outdoor delta loses 0.55 × 8 × 40 = 176 BTU/hr. The R-38 ceiling it replaced (U-0.026) lost 0.026 × 8 × 40 = 8.3 BTU/hr. The skylight leaks heat 21x faster than the insulation it displaced — per square foot, the worst thermal surface in the house except the front door left open.
