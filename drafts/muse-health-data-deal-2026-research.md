## Topic
Muse can read your health data. Since launch day, September 8, 2026, Apple Health has been a live Muse connector on iPhone, and Health Connect plays the same role on Android. Connect it and the agent that books your flights and drafts your emails can also summarize your sleep, spot patterns in your heart rate, and prep a one-page brief for your next doctor visit. The pitch is obvious and genuinely useful. The deal underneath it is not obvious at all: one tap grants a cloud agent access to a body of data your phone's operating system guards with per-type, per-direction permissions, and Meta's own defaults then decide what happens to that data after Muse reads it. This article audits the whole chain, permission by permission, and ends with a grant ladder: what to allow first, what to hold back, and what to turn off before you connect anything.

## Primary sources (verified)
1. **BuiltWithMuse, "How to Use the Apple Health Connector in Muse," updated ~Nov 13, 2026** — Apple Health live in Muse since launch day, September 8, 2026; iPhone-only, phone-only connector; access managed in device settings, not in the web connector list. Muse can read steps, workouts, sleep, and other Health app metrics. Meta has published no detailed list of exactly which data types Muse can read, and no statement that it can write anything back. Treat as read-only where the source is silent. URL: https://builtwithmuse.com/blog/muse-apple-health-connector
2. **Apple Platform Security / support.apple.com, "Protecting access to user's health data"** — HealthKit is a central on-device repository. All access requires user permission, granted separately per data type and separately for reading and writing. Data stored in Data Protection class Protected Unless Open; access relinquished 10 minutes after the device locks. Apps cannot use health data for advertising, must provide a privacy policy, and cannot determine whether a user denied read access — a denied query returns the same empty result as an empty database. Permissions reviewed and revoked under Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices. Health data end-to-end encrypted in iCloud with iOS 12+ and two-factor authentication. URL: https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/security/sec88be9900f/1/web/1
3. **Apple Developer Docs, "Protecting user privacy" (HealthKit), updated 2026-11-01** — users grant or deny permission separately for each type of data; an app may not use HealthKit data for advertising or similar services, may not disclose it to a third party without express user permission, and may not sell it to advertising platforms, data brokers, or information resellers. URL: https://apple-docs.everest.mt/docs/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy/
4. **Android Developers, HealthPermissions API reference** — Health Connect permissions are per record type and per direction: READ_STEPS, READ_HEART_RATE, READ_SLEEP, READ_BLOOD_GLUCOSE, READ_BLOOD_PRESSURE, READ_MEDICAL_DATA_CONDITIONS, READ_MEDICAL_DATA_LABORATORY_RESULTS, READ_MEDICAL_DATA_MEDICATIONS, READ_MENSTRUATION, READ_SEXUAL_ACTIVITY, plus two meta-permissions: READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY (entire history of any type) and READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND (read any type in background). Apps declare every type in a Play Console Health apps declaration. URL: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/health/connect/HealthPermissions
5. **Meta launch and safety materials, September 8, 2026 (via OpenVeil, AiCybr, XenoSpectrum, NordVPN, Squad.so, BetterClaw, all cross-checked Nov 2026)** — Muse runs in a dedicated Muse Secure VM per user; Sentinel approves or blocks outbound actions; Muse does not share conversations or VM data with Meta's ad systems; conversations, tool calls, and subagent handoffs (trajectories) are sanitized and used to train new model checkpoints BY DEFAULT, with an opt-out switch in Settings > Data controls; the current Secure VM "does not prevent Meta from accessing data when necessary to support, secure, or operate the service"; Muse Confidential VM (encrypted with a key only the user holds, so even Meta cannot read it) announced for later in 2026 and NOT live as of November 3, 2026. Muse Privacy Policy took effect September 17, 2026. Training setting "is on when you first use Muse"; switching it off covers earlier interactions too.
6. **The Markup, 2022 (via Senior Tech Cafe, Oct 12, 2026)** — Meta's tracking code found on 33 of Newsweek's top 100 hospitals' websites and inside the password-protected patient portals of seven health systems; the code sent Facebook details including medication names and the medical condition behind an appointment booking. Meta's response: a filter supposed to strip such information. Context for why a promise about health data from this company deserves verification, not just repetition.
7. **MuseDirectory, "Doctor Visit Starter Kit for Meta Muse" (updated Sep 23, 2026)** — the concrete use case: with Apple Health connected, Muse sums up sleep, activity, heart rate, and anything logged, in plain language, and writes the questions worth asking; it does not diagnose. HealthEx can add lab results. URL: https://musedirectory.ai/kits/get-ready-for-a-doctor-visit
8. **GetMuseHelp, "Apple Health x Meta Muse"** — setup is 60 seconds; approve only the HealthKit categories you want in iOS Settings; "Health data is the most sensitive grant — read-only, always." Disconnect path: Muse > Connectors > Apple Health > Disconnect, then iPhone Settings > Health > Data Access to remove categories. URL: https://getmusehelp.com/connectors/apple-health/
9. **TIME, October 6, 2026 (via MuseTaskHub / Senior Tech Cafe)** — Muse updates a running dossier ("page") every hour on the user and every person mentioned, including people who never signed up for Muse. Meta did not dispute the findings. Relevant here because health talk is rarely solo: you tell Muse about your mother's blood pressure or your child's inhaler, and that goes in a file about them.

## Facts established
- Apple Health connector live in Muse since September 8, 2026; iPhone-only; access managed in iPhone settings. Health Connect is the Android equivalent. Meta has published no detailed list of which HealthKit data types Muse reads and no statement about write access.
- Apple HealthKit permission model: separate grant per data type, separate for read and write. Denied read access is invisible to the app (empty result, no signal). No advertising use, no sale to brokers, privacy policy required, data encrypted on device and end-to-end encrypted in iCloud with 2FA.
- Google Health Connect: 42+ record types, each with its own READ/WRITE permission, plus READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY and READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND as separate gates. Revoking a permission stops future access; it does not erase data the app already copied (umatechnology.org). Same asymmetry on Apple: switching off a category takes effect immediately, "but what the app has already read stays with it."
- Meta's side: training ON by default (sanitized trajectories, opt-out in Settings > Data controls, covers earlier interactions too). Meta CAN access VM data when necessary to operate the service. No sharing with ad systems. Confidential VM announced, not live as of Nov 3, 2026. Privacy Policy effective Sep 17, 2026.
- Deleting does not mean forgetting: Meta's privacy policy says that after you delete something, Muse "may still 'remember' information it learned from what you deleted" (Senior Tech Cafe, quoting Meta).
- Muse is not a doctor: it can summarize and spot patterns; it cannot diagnose or say whether a reading is safe.
- Availability: Muse in US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, Mexico; 18+ only.

## Original contribution (novel analysis for this article)
1. **The permission count.** One "Connect Apple Health" tap looks like one decision. Count what it actually is: Apple's model makes every data type a separate grant in each direction, and Health Connect lists 42+ record types with separate READ/WRITE permissions plus two meta-permissions (full history, background reads). A reader who grants "health" is really granting a bundle of dozens of individually revocable permissions they never saw itemized, because Muse's connection flow does not publish which types it reads. The article's core number: the gap between the 1 tap the user makes and the 40+ permissions it stands for.
2. **The revoke asymmetry, priced.** Revoking stops future reads on both platforms. It erases nothing already read, and on Meta's side deletion does not even guarantee forgetting (the policy's own words). So the real cost of a bad grant is not ongoing access — you can cut that in seconds — it is the copy already sitting in a cloud VM that Meta can access when necessary and that may have been sanitized into a training trajectory. The honest advice follows from this: the decision that matters is the first grant, not the revoke.
3. **The grant ladder.** A concrete, ordered sequence nobody in the sources lays out end to end: (a) before connecting, turn OFF training in Settings > Data controls; (b) connect with steps and sleep only, read-only; (c) use it for a month and check what Muse actually summarizes; (d) add heart rate only if the summaries earn it; (e) never grant write access, never grant history or background-read permissions, never connect HealthEx lab results until the Confidential VM ships. Each rung names the exact setting and the exact risk it removes.
4. **The third-person health problem.** Health talk is rarely about one person. You mention your mother's blood pressure, your child's inhaler, your partner's sleep apnea, and Muse keeps an hourly-updated file on each of them (TIME, Oct 6). Apple's per-type permissions protect YOUR data. Nothing in the chain protects the people you talk about. That is the privacy gap no toggle covers, and it belongs in a Catherine Chen piece.

## Limitations / what we could not verify
- No hands-on test: no Muse account was used, so no firsthand confirmation of which HealthKit categories Muse requests, whether it asks for write access, or what the connection screen discloses.
- Meta has published no list of the exact HealthKit data types Muse reads; the read-only characterization is an inference from silence, flagged as such.
- Confidential VM status as of Nov 3, 2026 (not live); no update checked after that date.
- No independent audit of whether Muse's health summaries are accurate, or of what "sanitized" removes from training trajectories in practice.
- No confirmation of whether Health Connect (Android) is live in Muse or only Apple Health; sources describe Apple Health as the live connector and Health Connect as the Android equivalent in Muse's connector list, but the live status of the Android path is less firmly documented.

## Strongest counterargument
The cautious case overstates the risk. Apple and Google built the permission systems precisely so a user can grant steps and sleep while withholding heart rate, glucose, and medications, and Apple bars advertising use of HealthKit data outright. Meta's promises — no ad sharing, credentials out of the model's reach, Sentinel approval on outbound actions, an audit trail — are specific and testable, and the training opt-out exists and covers past interactions. A reader who follows the grant ladder gets a genuinely useful doctor-visit brief from data their phone already collects, and the agent never sees anything the phone did not already hold. The real failure mode is not the architecture; it is the user tapping "Allow all" without reading the list, which no system can fix. The article must give that argument its full weight before recommending restraint.
