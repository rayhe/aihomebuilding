# Research: Prop 19 — The Inheritance Tax Cliff and the 55+ Basis Portability Nobody Models

**Slug:** prop-19-parent-child-exclusion-55plus-basis-transfer-2026
**Journalist:** Catherine "Code" Chen (Policy & Regulation)
**Date:** October 6, 2026

## Angle
Prop 19 did two opposite things in one ballot measure: it gutted the parent-child exclusion (the inheritance half) while making the 55+ tax-basis transfer dramatically more generous (the portability half). Nobody covers both halves together with real math. The original contribution: worked tax-basis arithmetic using the CURRENT inflation-adjusted exclusion cap ($1,044,586, per BOE News Release 25-02, Mar 7 2025, for transfers Feb 16 2025–Feb 15 2027) — the BOE's own published example still uses the original $1,000,000 figure. Plus a side-by-side of old-law vs new-law annual bills, the one-year move-in deadline math, and the 55+ buy-up math for new construction.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. Two audiences: (1) anyone inheriting a California home — a single missed filing converts ~$8,900/yr into ~$21,200/yr; (2) 55+ owners selling and building/buying new anywhere in CA — the portability math decides whether the new build carries a 1978-era tax basis.

## Key facts
- Prop 19 passed Nov 3, 2020; parent-child provisions effective Feb 16, 2021; 55+/disabled/disaster portability effective Apr 1, 2021.
- New exclusion: child keeps parent's factored base year value ONLY IF the home was the parent's principal residence AND becomes the child's principal residence within 1 year AND the child files for homeowners'/disabled-veterans' exemption within 1 year. Cap: FBYV + $1,044,586 (current period). FMV above that sum gets added to the assessment. Rentals/vacation homes: full reassessment, no exclusion.
- The one-year clock runs from the date of transfer (deed/death), not from probate close; probate delays do not extend it.
- 55+: sell, then buy or newly construct a replacement principal residence within 2 years, anywhere in CA, up to 3 times (old Prop 60/90: one time, intra-county or ~10 cooperating counties, replacement had to be equal-or-lesser value). Buy-up allowed: market-value difference added to transferred base.
- Claims are NOT automatic and do not happen in escrow: BOE-19-P within 3 years; BOE-19-B (55+) filed with the *replacement* county assessor within 3 years of replacement purchase/completion.
- BOE-19-P (reassessment exclusion claim) and the Homeowners' Exemption (BOE-266) are separate filings; filing one does not substitute for the other.

## Original contribution (my math, current cap)
Scenario: parents' FBYV $280,000; date-of-death FMV $1,800,000; child moves in within 1 year and files everything correctly.
- Excluded amount = $280,000 + $1,044,586 = $1,324,586
- Excess = $1,800,000 − $1,324,586 = $475,414
- New assessed value = $755,414
- At illustrative 1.18% effective rate (1% base + typical local bonds/parcel taxes; varies by jurisdiction):
  - Old law (Prop 58): $280,000 → **$3,304/yr**
  - Prop 19 done right: $755,414 → **$8,914/yr**
  - Miss the move-in/exemption: $1,800,000 → **$21,240/yr**
- The one-year move-in + filings are worth **$12,326/yr** vs. missing them. Prop 19 costs **$5,610/yr** more than the old law, compounding at ≤2%/yr under Prop 13.
55+ buy-up scenario: sell $900k home (taxable $300k); build/buy $1.15M replacement → new taxable = $300,000 + $250,000 = $550,000 → ~$6,490/yr vs ~$13,570/yr without the transfer → saves ~$7,080/yr.

## Strongest counterargument
Prop 19 closed a genuine loophole: heirs were inheriting Malibu homes, never living in them, and paying Fresno-level taxes while renting them out — a subsidy for inherited wealth the voters chose to end, with the revenue earmarked for fire protection and schools. And the portability half is unambiguously more generous than Prop 60/90 (statewide, three uses, buy-ups allowed, new construction eligible). The one-year clock is harsh during grief and probate, but the exclusion isn't lost forever for late filers — a late claim applies going forward from the lien date of the claim year (intervening years reassessed). Also note: the assessor determines market value, not the purchase price — buying below market doesn't shrink the gap.

## Limitations
- 1.18% effective rate is illustrative; actual rate = 1% + voter-approved debt, varies by tax-rate area. Bills scale linearly, so the *deltas* hold regardless.
- I could not verify county-level Prop 19 claim-filing volumes or denial rates; no public dataset found.
- AI estate-review tools' Prop 19 detection is inferred from their described capabilities (document ingestion, gap flagging), not independently tested; no vendor publishes a Prop 19-specific feature.
