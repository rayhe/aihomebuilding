# RESEARCH: The Factory Can Build Your House in 9 Weeks. Your Bank Still Pays Like It's 1890.
**Slug:** modular-housing-financing-draw-gap-2026
**Article #:** 828
**Journalist:** Catherine Chen (policy/legal beat — financing structure, building codes, federal law)
**Date:** 2026-09-11

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. A buyer considering a factory-built modular home today faces a financing structure designed for stick-built construction: the bank releases money in inspection-gated draws as work is completed on site, but the modular factory needs most of its money BEFORE the house ships. That mismatch, plus appraisals that compare modular to stick-built comps and banks that price off-site as higher risk, can erase the cost savings factory building promises. This article explains the mismatch, what the new federal law does and does not fix, and the concrete moves buyers/builders should make today.

## Angle
Modular/prefab factory-built housing (state/local-code homes built in factories — NOT manufactured homes, which are a separate federal HUD-code category) can cut timelines 20-50% and hard costs up to 25%. But its national market share is stuck at ~3% of completions. One under-covered reason: the financial plumbing. Traditional construction loans disburse in draws tied to on-site completion milestones. Factories need large upfront payments for materials and assembly. HUD is now ordered to study the barriers, but the study is not a policy change. Signal, not substance — yet.

## Primary sources (5)

1. **Wall Street Journal — Lily Belle Poling, "New Housing Law Boosts Prospects for Factory-Built Homes" (Aug 2026).**
   - Off-site construction market share flat at ~3% of completions nationally.
   - Code fragmentation: "Every city, every municipality has a different code. That fragmentation prevents the scale economics from taking off" — Michael Tillman, CEO of PTM Partners.
   - Financing mismatch: traditional bank loans pay out step-by-step as a house is built on site; modular factories need a large portion of cash upfront to buy materials and start assembly. Cautious banks price these homes as higher risk.
   - New law directs HUD to identify barriers to financing programs for modular housing. Tillman's read: "it's really a study and rule-making directive. It's not a policy change." Signal, not substance.
   - Greystar (largest apartment operator) experimenting with modular in Pennsylvania and Massachusetts.
   - Traction with disaster victims (hurricanes, wildfires) whose insurance payouts fall short of traditional rebuild costs.

2. **Bipartisan Policy Center — 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act Implementation Tracker (updated Sep 2026).**
   - §302 Modular Housing Production Act: directs HUD to review FHA construction financing programs to identify barriers for modular housing developers, with administrative fixes. Review due July 11, 2027 (one year after enactment); rulemaking to address barriers initiated within 120 days of the review's publication. HUD may fund a study on a standardized code for modular homes.
   - §303 Property Improvement and Manufactured Housing Loan Modernization Act: HUD must study and report to Congress on cost-effectiveness of off-site construction within one year (July 11, 2027). Increases FHA-insured manufactured housing loan limits (self-executing); ADU construction added as acceptable use for FHA-insured property improvement loans.
   - States must submit certifications of parity treatment for non-chassis homes by July 11, 2027 (2028 for biennial legislatures) — the chassis-mandate repeal tail.
   - Enactment date: July 11, 2026 (one year earlier than the July 11, 2027 deadlines).

3. **California Construction News — Assembly Select Committee on Housing Construction Innovation, first hearing of 2026 session (Jan 2026).**
   - Ben Metcalf, Terner Center for Housing Innovation managing director, testified: California ~2.5M homes short; state housing production second-lowest per capita in the nation; construction costs risen at double inflation since 2019; multifamily construction costs 2.3x Texas for same product.
   - Terner Center research: factory-built housing reduces timelines 20-50%, cuts hard costs up to 25%.
   - Tahanan project, San Francisco: 30% time savings, 25% cost savings vs. traditional build.
   - Industrialized construction = 3D printing, robotics, factory-built modular; some units ship as flat packs with integrated plumbing/electrical, others as stacked boxes.
   - Labor shortage angle: moving work into factories addresses aging skilled-trades workforce.

4. **HousingForward Virginia / The FWD — Fauquier Habitat for Humanity, Haiti Street modular project (published ~Apr 2026).**
   - First modular project: mix of standalone one-bedrooms, duplex, triplex on one street in Warrenton, VA; first modular family moved in March 2026; 13 families (~30 people) housed on the street.
   - Real-world logistics lesson: narrow one-way street; crane set took hours angling homes up a steep grade; the specialized "set crew" does far more on delivery day than first-timers expect; site conditions are everything.
   - Flexibility within zoning: four units where one stood before, no road widening or extra parking; "factory-built housing turned out to be a doorway, not a destination."

5. **MIT News — 3D-printed recycled-plastic structural framing (Feb 2026).**
   - MIT engineers printed floor trusses from recycled plastic; four trusses in a plywood-topped frame held over 4,000 lbs, exceeding HUD building standards; each truss ~13 lbs, printed in under 13 minutes on an industrial printer.
   - Context for the innovation frontier (and its limits: lab-scale, not a product a buyer can order).

## Skepticism points (must include)
- The ROAD Act provisions are studies and reviews, not policy changes — Tillman's "signal, not substance" quote.
- Modular's 3% share persists despite decades of promises; Katerra ($2B) and Veev ($647M) are the industry's cautionary graveyard.
- Cost savings (25% hard costs) are claimed by the industry; independent per-project verification is thin; transport, crane, and set-crew costs can eat the spread.
- Code fragmentation: no national code; every municipality differs (vs. Germany/Japan/Sweden national codes per Tillman).
- Disaster-zone traction comes partly from desperation (insurance payouts short of rebuild cost), not pure economics.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE — required in article)
1. If you're buying a modular/prefab home: ask the factory for its payment schedule BEFORE you pick a lender — the factory's upfront requirement dictates what loan product can work.
2. Use a construction-to-permanent lender that has actually closed modular deals; a conventional stick-built draw schedule will stall at the first factory invoice.
3. Budget for the hidden on-site stack: transport, crane, set crew, utility hookups, site prep — these are outside the factory price and where savings evaporate.
4. Get the appraisal conversation early: insist the appraiser has modular comps or understands the product; a stick-built-only comp set can undervalue the finished home.
5. Watch the §302 timeline: HUD's FHA barrier review is due July 11, 2027, with rulemaking to follow — if your project is 2028+, the financing landscape may improve; if it's 2026-27, plan for today's plumbing.
6. For disaster rebuilds: factory-built can close the insurance-payout gap, but only if the loan funds the factory deposit — check this before signing the factory contract.
