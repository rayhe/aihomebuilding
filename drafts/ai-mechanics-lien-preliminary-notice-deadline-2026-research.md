## Angle
Mechanics lien rights are the subcontractor's nuclear option for getting paid — but they expire on brutal, state-specific deadlines that most subs track on memory and spreadsheets. A 2026 Siteline survey found 56% of subcontractors missed a critical lien deadline in the past two years. AI-powered lien management tools (Levelset/Procore, Bectran integrations, Handle) now auto-track state deadlines, verify property info, and generate notices. The article: the 20-day trap, the state-by-state minefield, what the software actually does vs. what it claims, and an original expected-loss calculation.

## Kill test
A residential sub owed $40K who misses a preliminary notice deadline can lose lien rights entirely — the single most expensive paperwork mistake in residential construction. This directly helps anyone building (subs, GCs) and, on the flip side, homeowners who need to understand why that lien appeared on their property.

## Key facts / numbers
- CA & AZ: preliminary notice within 20 days of first furnishing labor/materials. Late is not fatal in CA — protection limited to work done within 20 days before the notice + after. (Levelset)
- NC: 15 days to lien agent, hard deadline, projects $30K+ (except single-family residences).
- MT: 20 days, non-commercial, must deliver AND file with county clerk.
- MN: 45 days minimum recommended, certified mail.
- OK: 75 days on owner-occupied dwellings — miss is fatal to lien rights.
- CA §8190 / AZ §33-993: owners recording a Notice of Completion must serve a copy on anyone who sent a preliminary notice — so sending the prelim buys you early warning before your lien-filing deadline shortens (60 days in CA if completion recorded).
- 56% of subs missed a lien deadline in past 2 years (Siteline 2026).
- $273B cost of slow payments, 2023 (PYMNTS).
- 72% of subs waited >30 days for payment in 2023 (PYMNTS).

## Skepticism / counterarguments to engage
- The "AI" in these tools is mostly rules engines + research teams + databases, not magic. Levelset's own marketing admits Scout is "a team of people" plus databases. Garbage in (late job entry) = garbage out (missed deadline anyway).
- Software doesn't replace a construction attorney; wrong legal property description or wrong recipient can still void a notice. Illinois courts have upheld minor service defects but drew the line at fax (Seasons-4 v. Hertz) — technical compliance still matters.
- Cost: lien platforms charge per document or subscription; for a 2-person sub doing 10 jobs/year, the ROI is thinner than for a 50-job supplier.
- 56% miss stat is from a vendor (Siteline) survey of its audience — directionally alarming, not a randomized study. State this.
- Prompt-pay statutes exist in all 50 states but haven't fixed the problem (ENR survey: 30%+ of payments late, avg 36.4 days) — software treats a symptom of a power imbalance, not the cause.
