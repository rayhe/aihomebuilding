# Research: Soft-Story Mandate Compliance Audit (Article #981)

**Journalist:** Catherine "Code" Chen (Policy & Regulation)
**Slug:** `soft-story-mandate-92-percent-compliance-math-2026`
**Working headline:** "San Francisco Ordered 4,900 Apartment Buildings Earthquake-Proofed in 2013. 92 Percent Complied. The Other 8 Percent Still House 9,600 People."
**Date:** September 27, 2026

## Angle

California lets cities order owners of wood-frame soft-story apartment buildings to retrofit or else. San Francisco did it in 2013; Los Angeles in 2015; Oakland, Berkeley, and a dozen more followed. A decade-plus later, the compliance data tells a clean story about what mandates can and cannot do, and the cost-sharing math explains exactly why the last 8 percent stalls. The actionable core: how a renter or buyer checks whether their building complied, who pays for the work, and where grant money exists right now.

## Kill test

Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A renter in the Bay Area or LA can look up whether their building is on a mandatory retrofit list and whether it complied. A small landlord facing a notice-and-order gets the cost math, the tenant pass-through rules, and the EMR grant window. A buyer of a 5-10 unit building in a mandatory city gets a due-diligence checklist item most inspectors never flag.

## Original contributions (computed, not quoted)

1. **The 8-percent headcount.** SF: 92% of ~4,900 buildings retrofitted, housing "over 110,000 people." If the 110,000 is proportional to the 92%, the remaining ~8% (~390 buildings) house roughly 9,600 people in unretrofitted soft-story buildings. Caveat: occupancy may not be perfectly proportional; frame as "on the order of."
2. **The pass-through cap math.** LA's deal: 50% cost pass-through, $38/month cap, 10-year recovery = maximum $4,560 recoverable per unit from tenants ($38 x 120). At the city-cited $5,000/unit estimate, the owner absorbs at least ~$440/unit. At a realistic $100,000 retrofit on a 10-unit building ($10,000/unit), the owner absorbs $5,440/unit. The cap, not the split, is the binding constraint, and it is why owners stall even under a mandate.
3. **The mandate-vs-voluntary contrast.** SF's mandatory program: 4,921 buildings identified, 98% permit applications, 65% completed by 2019, 92% by ~2023. Contrast with the pre-mandate voluntary era (SF tried fee breaks and expedited permits; "only about 50 property owners jumped on board" per ProgramBusiness/DBI). Mandates move the needle; incentives barely register. That is the policy finding.

## Strongest counterargument

The mandate's costs land on the same small landlords and tenants it claims to protect. Berkeley's own data showed owners absorbing costs without pass-through; Oakland tenants argued it is unfair to make current tenants pay for work they may not benefit from; USGS's Given warned retrofits could take stock off the market or price people out once costs are built into rent. The 92% compliance figure also flatters the program: it counts buildings, not the hardest cases, and the last 8% are disproportionately the buildings whose owners cannot finance the work. A mandate without money is a fine schedule with extra steps.

## Limitations

- SF's 92% figure is from ~2023 city reporting; no newer official completion percentage found in the public record as of September 2026. Treat as the latest published.
- The 9,600-people estimate assumes proportional occupancy across compliant and non-compliant buildings; non-compliant buildings may skew smaller or larger.
- Retrofit cost ranges ($60K-$150K/building) come from 2015-2016 estimates; current contractor pricing in a post-inflation market is likely higher, and no current public dataset tracks actual per-building retrofit costs.
- EMR grant amounts per building were not published on the program page reviewed; the article states eligibility and registration window, not award size.
- Oakland tier deadlines ran through 2025; current compliance status for Oakland's 1,500 buildings was not available in the sources reviewed.

## Actionable takeaways (for the checklist)

1. Look up your building: SF DBI publishes the soft-story list and retrofit status ("See if your building has already been retrofitted" on sf.gov); ask the same of Oakland, Berkeley, LA building departments before signing a lease or making an offer.
2. If you got a notice-and-order: you need an engineer/architect evaluation first (Oakland's Step 3), then a retrofit design (Step 4), then permitted construction (Step 5). Budget $60K-$150K for a small apartment building; 2-4 months of ground-floor-only work.
3. Know the pass-through rules before you budget: LA caps tenant recovery at $38/month over 10 years ($4,560/unit max). Price the unrecoverable remainder into your financing, not your hopes.
4. Check EMR grant eligibility now: 5-10 units, pre-1991 wood frame, tuck-under parking, mandatory-ordinance city, notice-and-order received, full ground-floor retrofit per FEMA P-807 or equivalent. Registration windows are annual and short.
5. Buyers: add "soft-story mandate status" to due diligence on any pre-1991 multifamily in a mandatory city. An unretrofitted building with an open order is a six-figure liability wearing a rent roll.
6. Renters: a building on the mandatory list with no completed retrofit is not just a code story, it is a disclosure question. Ask in writing.
