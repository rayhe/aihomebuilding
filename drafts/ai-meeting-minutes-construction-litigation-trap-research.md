# Research: AI Meeting Minutes as Construction Litigation Trap

## Angle
AI notetakers (Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai, Zoom AI Companion) are being adopted by contractors, architects, and builders for progress meetings, design reviews, and owner-builder discussions. These tools create perfect, timestamped, searchable transcripts of every informal statement, half-commitment, and offhand remark. In an industry where 1 in 4 projects has a claim, these AI records are discoverable and create new legal exposure that didn't exist when someone just scribbled notes on a legal pad.

The core tension: historically, the party with better documentation wins construction disputes. AI meeting tools now create documentation for everyone — including documentation of things people wish they hadn't said.

## Key Data Points

### Construction Dispute Frequency
- 1 in 4 construction projects has a claim (IPA study, cited in National Academies "Reducing Construction Costs" proceedings, Paul Barshop presentation)
- HKA CRUX Insight Report 2024: Additional costs from claims/disputes averaged 33.2% of project budgeted CapEx globally, 31.7% in the Americas
- Americas: analysis of 662 projects, average CapEx $583M (US subset: 490 projects)
- Average dispute resolution time in North America: 16.2 months (+18% increase)
- Primary drivers: increased profit pressures on contractors (30%), poorly developed contracts (20%), risk allocation to contractors (20%)

### AI Meeting Minutes Legal Risks
- **Discoverability:** AI-generated meeting transcripts are discoverable in litigation (multiple court holdings)
- **Heppner ruling:** Canadian court held that sharing content with public AI platform = waiving confidentiality and legal privilege
- **In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation** (N.D. Cal. Dec. 5, 2025, Case 5:25-cv-06911-EKL): class action alleging Otter records non-account-holding meeting participants without consent, uses voices for AI training
- **Dixon v. Royal Live Oaks Academy** (D.S.C. 2024): court declined to consider auto-generated transcripts at summary judgment because transcripts contained "errors" — key precedent on AI transcript reliability
- **AIA Trust warning** (Jonathan Shoemaker, Lee/Shoemaker PLLC): AI search histories could become standard discovery requests in construction litigation
- **ABA GPSolo (Sep 2025):** AI notetakers risk inadvertent privilege waiver, expose conversations to discovery, expand scope of discoverable material
- **Morrison Foerster analysis:** Wiretapping theories now being tested against AI notetakers
- **White & Case analysis:** When AI transcripts differ from formal minutes, adverse parties may rely on the more detailed AI version

### Recording Consent Laws
- 12+ US states require all-party consent to recording (California, Florida, Illinois, etc.)
- California: two-party consent state (Cal. Penal Code § 632) — particularly relevant for construction
- AI notetakers typically only get consent from the account holder, not all meeting participants
- Otter.ai reached $100M annual revenue while allegedly recording non-consenting participants

### Construction-Specific Documentation Standards  
- HCR Law analysis: "The gold standard of construction evidence has traditionally been the contemporaneous record, including meeting minutes and site diaries. As these records become AI-generated or assisted, their reliability will inevitably be challenged."
- Meeting minutes historically are curated — someone selects what to record. AI transcripts are comprehensive — everything is recorded.
- Michael Vardaro (construction attorney, 30 years): "The hidden danger of AI meeting minutes in construction — they could actually make your legal position worse"

### The Specific Construction Problem
- Progress meetings often include informal status updates: "yeah, that crack doesn't look great but it'll be fine"
- Design reviews have candid discussions about trade-offs that look terrible in discovery
- Owner-builder meetings have moments of frustration, half-commitments, and verbal agreements
- AI captures ALL of this — not just the curated, CYA version

### MDPI Academic Paper (Jun 2026)
- "Governing Data Integrity Risks in Digital Construction" (Buildings 2026, 16(13), 2577)
- Identifies two recurrent harm pathways: autonomous hallucination entering safety-relevant info, and adversarial/erroneous data entering digital-twin feedback loops
- Proposes proportional-liability framework across developers, deployers, and professional users

## Original Contribution
Analysis of the documentation asymmetry shift: before AI, better-documented parties won disputes. Now AI creates records for everyone, but these records include things the carefully-documenting party would have omitted. The "AI meeting minutes trap" — the tool that helps you remember also helps opposing counsel remember.

## Counterargument
AI meeting minutes could actually REDUCE disputes by creating clear, unambiguous records of agreements, scope discussions, and commitments. The transparency could prevent the "he said, she said" that drives many construction claims. Some dispute prevention experts argue better documentation = fewer disputes, period.

## Limitations
- No published statistics on AI notetaker adoption rates specifically in construction (general adoption data only)
- Heppner ruling is Canadian; US courts may diverge
- Most AI meeting litigation is in early stages (Otter suit filed 2025, no resolution yet)
- Construction-specific case law on AI transcripts is thin — most precedent is from other industries
