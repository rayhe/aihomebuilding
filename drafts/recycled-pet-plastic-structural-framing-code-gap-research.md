# Research: Recycled PET Plastic Structural Framing — Code Gap

## Core Story
MIT HAUS lab (AJ Perez, David Hardt, Lab for Manufacturing and Productivity) 3D-printed floor trusses from recycled PET plastic reinforced with glass fibers. The trusses exceeded HUD building standards by a massive margin. But the IRC and IBC have no structural framing category for plastic. Getting this into a real house requires jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction alternative materials approval, and the recycled PET industry is actually contracting.

## Primary Sources

### MIT HAUS Research (Feb 2026)
- Published in Solid FreeForm Fabrication Symposium Proceedings
- 4 printed trusses, each 8 ft long × 1 ft high × ~1 inch wide
- Each truss: ~13 lbs (lighter than comparable wood truss)
- Print time: ~13 minutes per truss (80 lbs/hour throughput)
- Material: recycled PET polymer pellets + glass fibers (from aerospace materials company)
- Assembled into 4×8 ft plywood-topped floor frame
- Passed HUD deflection standards at 300 lbs easily
- Didn't buckle/crack until 4,000+ lbs
- Source: news.mit.edu, dezeen.com, MIT Technology Review
- Lead researchers: AJ Perez (lecturer/research scientist), David Hardt (prof mechanical engineering)
- MIT HAUS founded 2019
- Quote: "We've estimated that the world needs about 1 billion new homes by 2050. If we try to make that many homes using wood, we would need to clear-cut the equivalent of the Amazon rainforest three times over."
- Currently using "crème de la crème" recycled feedstock — factory-discarded, not truly "dirty" plastic yet
- Working toward using truly dirty PET (used bottles with residue)
- Vision: shipping container micro-factories near waste sources, print parts light enough for pickup truck transport

### Building Code Gap
- IBC Chapter 26 covers "Plastic" but focuses on foam plastic insulation, not structural framing
- IBC Section 104.11 / IRC Section R104.11: alternative materials provision
  - Building official CAN approve alternative materials if "at least equivalent" in quality, strength, effectiveness, fire resistivity, durability, safety
  - But this is jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction, project-by-project approval
  - No national acceptance pathway for structural plastic framing exists
  - Fire performance of PET in structural applications is untested in code-relevant scenarios
- Structural framing in IRC assumes wood, cold-formed steel, or concrete/masonry
- No ICC Evaluation Service (ES) report exists for structural PET framing components

### PET Recycling Data (NAPCOR 2024 Report, Dec 2025)
- US PET bottle recycling rate: 30.2% in 2024 (down from 32.5% in 2023)
- Collection: 1,889M lbs collected (down 3.9% from 2023)
- ~70% of PET bottles NOT recycled
- Only 971,215 tonnes of 3,412,310 tonnes generated are recycled (Recycling Partnership data)
- rPET industry contracting: plant closures since early 2025 (Alpek NC/PA, Evergreen CA, Phoenix Technologies OH, rPlanet Earth CA)
- Imports now 25% of US rPET supply, up to 50% in some regions
- $42 billion investment gap in recycling infrastructure (Plastics Today, 2026)
- PET takes ~450 years to break down naturally — hence durability for structural use

### Lumber Context
- Lumber prices volatile since 2020
- Framing package for typical single-family home: varies $20K-$50K depending on market
- NAHB: lumber costs roughly $35K-$50K of average new home

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — but not yet directly. The article reveals that a legitimate structural alternative to wood framing exists in the lab, explains why it can't be used in your house today (code gap), and quantifies both the waste stream that could feed it and the industry contraction that threatens its feedstock. For architects and forward-thinking builders, it maps where this technology sits on the adoption curve.

## Journalist: Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design)
She'd love the material elegance — trash becoming load-bearing structure — while critically examining why the gap between lab proof and buildable reality is wider than MIT's press coverage suggests.

## Angle
MIT proved you can print a floor truss from recycled plastic bottles in 13 minutes that holds 4,000 pounds. The building code has no idea what to do with it. The recycled PET industry that would supply the feedstock is closing plants.
