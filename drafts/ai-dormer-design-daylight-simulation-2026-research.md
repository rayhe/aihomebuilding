# Research: AI-Optimized Dormer Design — Daylight Simulation Meets the Roofline

## Slug: ai-dormer-design-daylight-simulation-2026
## Journalist: Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design)
## Working Headline: "An AI Ran 2,000 Dormers on Her Roof. The Winner Was Ugly."

## Thesis
Generative design tools (Higharc's tokenizing layout model, cove.tool's ML daylight/energy optimization) can now simulate thousands of dormer configurations — type, size, placement, orientation — and score each on daylight autonomy, glare, energy, and cost. The technology is genuinely useful: dormers are $15K–$90K decisions where orientation and type determine whether you get morning light or a July oven. But the optimizer's answer is almost always the cheapest shed dormer facing the "optimal" azimuth. Architecture is also about the street, the roofline rhythm, and the way an eyebrow dormer makes a house look loved. The algorithm optimizes lux. It does not know beauty. Elena's job: report the tech honestly, then defend the unmeasurable.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A homeowner planning a dormer addition gets: (1) the real cost bands by dormer type, (2) the egress code math that determines whether the new bedroom is legal, (3) why orientation matters more than size for usable light, (4) when paying for simulation is worth it vs. trusting the architect's eye, (5) the ROI reality (67% cost recovery).

### 2. cove.tool — ML daylight/energy/glare optimization
- Sources: cove.inc (product + api.tool announcement); Metro Atlanta CEO (Series A coverage); DOE recognition noted in company materials
- URLs:
  - https://cove.inc
  - https://cove.inc/newsroom/cove-tool-announces-api-tool

- Key data: platform "runs thousands of simulations to find the lowest cost solution to meet performance targets" across daylight, glare, energy, carbon, water. Daylight scored as sDA (spatial Daylight Autonomy) per IES LM-83: percentage of floor area receiving at least 300 lux for 50% of occupied hours (8am–6pm). Glare scored as ASE (Annual Sunlight Exposure). Automating energy modeling "cutting what once took consultants 150 hours to do manually into just 30 minutes." Recognized by DOE for optimizing building designs for sustainability; 2021 ARCHITECT Magazine R+D Award.
- Relevance: this is the exact simulation stack you'd run on dormer variants — sDA tells you which dormer actually delivers usable light; ASE tells you which one cooks the room in August.

### 3. IRC Section R310 — Emergency escape and rescue openings (the egress math)
- Source: ICC Digital Codes, 2020 Idaho Residential Code (mirrors IRC R310); county handouts (Duluth MN, Emmet County MI, Gwinnett County GA)
- URL: https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IDRC2020P1/chapter-3-building-planning
- Key data: every sleeping room needs an operable emergency escape opening. Minimum net clear opening 5.7 sq ft (5.0 sq ft at grade). Minimum clear height 24 in, minimum clear width 20 in. Sill height not more than 44 in above the floor. Operable from inside without keys, tools, or special knowledge.
- Relevance: the hard gate on every dormer-bedroom scheme. A dormer sized purely for looks can fail the 5.7 sq ft net-clear test once sash/frame deductions are applied — the AI that doesn't model operable sash geometry will happily design an illegal bedroom. Actionable: check net-clear, not rough opening.

### 4. Dormer cost bands (2026)
- Sources: Angi (dormer framing guide, updated Jul 2026); Pegasus Builds Chicago (dormer addition FAQ); Homeyou cost guides (Sep 2026)
- URLs:
  - https://www.angi.com/articles/dormer-framing.htm
  - https://pegasusbuilds.com/home-remodeling-services/dormer-addition/
- Key data: Angi: $2,500–$20,000 for dormer installation, average ~$15,000 depending on roof slope and structure. Full dormer additions (Chicago builder): $40,000–$90,000; shed dormers add the most headroom/floor space (200–400 sq ft, 6–7 ft headroom on bungalows); gable dormers are the "architectural accent" choice; typical build 6–10 weeks. Homeyou: ~$115/sq ft. Gable dormers bear weight on sidewalls; shed dormers transfer load to the front wall (different structural engineering).
- Relevance: the cost ladder the optimizer climbs — and why it always lands on the shed dormer. The price gap between "most space per dollar" and "best looking" is the article's central tension.

## Secondary / context
- IES LM-83-12 (now ANSI/IES LM-83-23): sDA 300/50% and ASE 1000,250h thresholds — the standard behind the simulation scores.
- Eyebrow dormers: low-profile, curved roof; minimal headroom gain, maximal charm; hardest to flash, highest labor per sq ft (general trade knowledge — attribute as such).
- Chicago bungalow dormers as the vernacular case study (Pegasus Builds).

## Skepticism (required by STORY_GUIDE)
- Higharc's customer metrics (25–50% faster, 10–15% margin) are vendor-reported, not independently audited.
- cove.tool's "150 hours to 30 minutes" compares consultant-grade manual modeling to automated runs — true for early-stage massing, less true for permit-grade documentation.
- No public evidence that any production tool today optimizes *dormer* design specifically; the article's honest framing is "the simulation stack exists and applies directly," not "there is a dormer app."
- Simulation optimizes what it can measure (lux, dollars, BTUs). It cannot score roofline rhythm, street character, or the way morning light moves across a plaster wall. Say so.
- Katerra/Veev rule: construction-tech graveyard is deep; treat vendor claims as claims.

## Original contribution / angle
The dormer as the perfect Elena subject: a small architectural element where physics (daylight, structure, heat), law (egress), money ($15K–$90K, 67% recovery), and beauty (shed vs gable vs eyebrow) collide. The AI can now brute-force the physics. It cannot adjudicate the beauty. The article's verdict: run the simulations, then hire the architect anyway — and the piece should be honest about which dormer the math picks (shed, south-ish, big) versus which one the street remembers (eyebrow, gable).

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE — must appear in article)
1. If you're adding a dormer bedroom: check the egress math FIRST — 5.7 sq ft net clear opening, 44" max sill, operable from inside. Rough opening ≠ net clear; sash deductions kill more dormer schemes than budgets do.
2. Cost bands (2026): $2,500–$20,000 per dormer unit installed (Angi, avg ~$15K); full dormer additions $40,000–$90,000, 6–10 weeks (Chicago builder data). Shed = most space per dollar; gable = accent; eyebrow = charm at premium labor.
3. Orientation beats size for usable light: a smaller east/west-balanced dormer with good sDA beats a big south-facing one that spikes ASE (glare/heat) — ask your designer for the daylight/glare simulation, not just the rendering.
4. ROI reality: attic conversions recover ~67% at resale (NAR 2025). You're buying light and headroom, not an investment.
5. When simulation earns its fee: complex roofs, multiple dormer options, or hot climates where ASE/glare risk is real. For a single gable dormer on a simple roof, the architect's eye plus the egress tape measure is enough.
