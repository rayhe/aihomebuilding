## Original contribution: the missed-call math for a 10-tech residential shop

Inputs: 10-technician HVAC/plumbing shop; 30 inbound calls/week (busy season); 40% missed = 12 missed calls/week; average job value $485 (Precision Plumbing figure); answered-call conversion 40%.

- Lost jobs/week: 12 × 40% = 4.8 ≈ 5 jobs.
- Lost revenue/week: 5 × $485 = $2,425.
- Lost revenue/year (40-week busy season): ~$97,000.
- AI receptionist cost: ~$3,600/yr (upper end, $300/month plan).
- Payback: the AI pays for itself if it converts ONE missed call per quarter. The rest is margin.

Cross-check: CallRail says 25–35% of potential revenue lost to missed calls; TradeWorks says voice agents recover 28–30% of missed leads. Conservative take: even recovering 20% of the $97K = $19,400/yr for $3,600. ROI ~5.4x before counting after-hours coverage.

## The labor angle (Marcus's beat)

- The job being automated is the $35K–$50K/yr front-desk role — overwhelmingly held by women in residential contracting (office managers, dispatchers, CSRs).
- The AI-first + human escalation hybrid scores 92% satisfaction — highest of any model (AI Answering Review Q1 2026). So the honest version of the story: the human doesn't disappear, but the headcount math changes. One office manager + AI covers what used to need three.
- Pink Callers (human call center for home services) is leaning in rather than fighting it — co-founder publicly embracing AI.
- FieldCamp's Arizona HVAC case: 32 → 45 jobs/week with the same office headcount. Growth absorbed without hiring — that's the quiet displacement: not layoffs, but jobs that never get posted.
- NABTU/Microsoft AI literacy angle (published #500): training programs target field workers, not office staff. The office side is getting automated, not upskilled.

## Skepticism / counterargument

- Vendor case studies (28 calls/week → $52K/month) are marketing, not audited. Treat as directional.
- Emergency triage: gas leak, burst pipe, sparking panel — an AI that books "first available Thursday" for a flooded basement is a liability event, not a conversion.
- Language: residential job sites are Spanish-speaking; office staff who can't triage in Spanish lose jobs today. AI receptionists claim Spanish support (Contractor+ lists it) but accent/regional-slang handling claims are vendor-sourced (Hook Agency notes improvement, not perfection).
- Complex custom-home work can't be phone-quoted by anyone, human or AI — the AI's job is intake, not estimating.
- The double-AI phone call: Google's agent calling your AI agent. Nobody has tested whether AI-to-AI phone negotiation books jobs or both bots politely hang up. Unknown territory as of Sep 2026.
- Trust: homeowners calling at 2 AM about a water heater may not want to talk to a machine; the 92% hybrid satisfaction number suggests escalation paths matter more than the voice itself.

## Limitations

- No independent, peer-reviewed study of AI receptionist conversion rates in residential contracting exists; figures come from vendor marketing, industry surveys, and aggregators.
- The $485 average job value and 30 calls/week profile is a constructed composite for illustration — real shops vary wildly by trade and season.
- The AI Answering Review Q1 2026 industry report is a commercial product (ai-answering-review.com), not an academic publication; its methodology cites surveys but the underlying datasets aren't public.
- Pricing ($200–$300/month) reflects published 2026 list prices; enterprise or integrated plans (ServiceTitan) cost more.
- Google's agentic calling was announced at I/O 2026 with summer rollout; real-world performance data doesn't exist yet.

## Actionable takeaways (for the article)

- If you run a 5–20 tech residential shop and miss calls after hours: an AI receptionist at $200–$300/month is the cheapest revenue on your books. Break-even is roughly one converted call per quarter.
- Buy the hybrid: AI-first with human escalation (92% satisfaction beats AI-only). Route emergencies (gas, water, electrical) to a human number, always.
- Tell your office staff the truth: the phone-answering half of the job is going away; the customer-relationship and problem-solving half isn't. Shops that retrain CSRs as customer success keep them.
- Audit your Google Business Profile now — Google's AI will be calling you on customers' behalf by fall 2026; if your hours, service area, and pricing are stale, the bot reports the wrong answer.
