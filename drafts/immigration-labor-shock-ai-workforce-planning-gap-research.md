# Research: Immigration Labor Shock × AI Workforce Planning Gap

## Core Thesis
Net international migration projected to fall from 2.7 million (2024) to ~321,000 (2026) — an 88% drop. The construction industry needs 349,000 new workers in 2026, and 41% of the current workforce hits retirement age by 2031. Large GCs with Bridgit-style AI workforce planning tools can model this. Small residential builders can't. The AI tools that could predict crew availability at residential scale don't exist.

## Primary Sources

### ABC 2026 Workforce Analysis (Jan 15, 2026)
- Industry must attract 349,000 net new workers in 2026
- "A majority of new worker demand in 2026 will be attributable to retirement rather than increased demand for construction services"
- Immigration enforcement + aging workforce cited as twin pressures
- Source: GlobeNewsWire press release

### Bridgit 2026 Construction Workforce Benchmark Report
- 233 companies, 114,000 people analyzed (nearly 40% of ENR 400)
- Median attrition rate: just below 20%
- 46% of companies had no net growth in 2025 (20% contracted, 26% flat)
- To grow by 100 people at 20% attrition: need to hire 125
- To grow by 100 people at 35% attrition: need to hire 154
- Senior super/PM attrition = 1/4 the rate of non-senior
- Superintendent median tenure: 3.7 years (senior: 7.0 years)
- Top 50 ENR 400 planning horizon: 6.8 years vs industry avg 4.7 years
- Average rookie ratio: 36.4%; climbs to 56.2% on teams of 51+
- Key finding: Top 50 face same attrition rates as broader industry, but 3x higher growth rate — proactive planning, not lower turnover, separates them

### Net Migration Data
- Net international migration: 2.7M (2024) → projected ~321K (2026) — 88% drop
- This removes "the demographic backstop the industry has leaned on"
- Source: Bridgit citing CBO/Census projections

### 50-person firm retention math
- 30% annual turnover = 15 departures/year
- Average replacement cost $12,800 = $192,000/year
- 25% investment in retention ($48K) → 4:1 ROI

### Wage vs Employment Data (Deloitte)
- 21% wage increase → only 8.8% increase in construction employment
- Shows money alone can't solve the workforce problem

### South Texas Example (Mario Guerrero, South Texas Builders Association)
- Immigration enforcement at construction sites disrupting active crews
- "Mid-pour" disruptions — enforcement actions during active concrete pours
- Delays compound costs, especially for builders on short-term construction loans
- Costs ultimately passed to buyers

### Procore Future State Report (2026)
- 18% of project time lost to data retrieval
- 28% of effort wasted on rework
- 55% of industry leaders expect automation to disrupt within 5 years
- 53% of workers expected to retire by 2036
- 47% already have upskilling programs; 41% plan to add them
- 45% of workers cite burnout as main reason for leaving

### AGC 2025 Workforce Survey
- 45% expect AI will positively impact construction jobs
- 44% believe AI will improve job quality and safety
- Only 12% worry about negative job market impact (down from 17%)
- 47% report difficulty filling AI specialist positions (up from 30% in 2024)

### Academic Research (Howard, Wang, Zhang)
- Immigration enforcement → large, persistent reductions in construction workforce
- Treated counties experience reductions in homebuilding + increases in home prices
- Undocumented labor is a COMPLEMENT to domestic labor — deporting undocumented workers also reduces labor by domestic workers (both extensive and intensive margins)
- This is the key academic finding: enforcement doesn't just remove immigrant workers, it reduces native-born worker productivity too

## AI Workforce Tools — Commercial vs Residential Gap

### What exists for large commercial GCs:
- **Bridgit Bench**: AI-powered workforce planning, forecasting, scenario modeling. Built for ENR 400. $$$
- **Procore Resource Planning (LaborChart)**: People management, forecasting, analytics. 10-10,000+ employees. Commercial.
- **BuildOps**: Commercial contractors. Drag-and-drop, trade-level filtering, crew templates.

### What exists for residential builders:
- **Pro Crew Schedule**: Calendar views, crew assignment. Basic. No forecasting.
- **Workyard**: GPS timecards, skill-tagged scheduling. $6/user/month. No predictive.
- **Buildern**: AI scheduling from timelines. Residential-focused but limited workforce prediction.
- **Agendrix**: Shift scheduling, timesheets. Generic, not construction-specific.

### The gap:
None of the residential tools model:
- Regional labor supply changes (immigration enforcement, retirement waves)
- Attrition risk prediction for individual crew members
- Crew continuity impact on safety and quality outcomes
- Sub-contractor availability forecasting
- Cross-project crew allocation optimization

The Bridgit benchmark data PROVES this matters — the Top 50 ENR 400 companies have 3x growth with the same attrition, purely because they plan around turnover. But their tools cost 10-100x what a residential builder can afford, and they require 100+ person datasets to train on.

## Original Contribution
A cross-reference nobody made: The 88% net migration decline PLUS the academic evidence that immigrant labor is complementary (not substitutive) to domestic labor means the actual workforce impact is roughly 1.3-1.5x the direct immigration decline. The models that would quantify this for a given metro area exist in academia but not in any commercial construction planning tool. Bridgit tracks attrition at company level but doesn't incorporate macro labor supply shocks. The residential builder has neither.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. If your builder's crew planning is a whiteboard and a phone, and the labor pool just contracted 88% in one channel while retirement accelerates in another, you need to know:
1. Your project timeline is at risk and nobody's modeling it
2. The replacement cost when crew members leave isn't just the recruit — it's 8-12 weeks of ramp-up during which quality drops
3. The 36.4% rookie ratio means more than a third of the people building your home are new to their team
4. The tools that would warn your builder exist but aren't built for him
