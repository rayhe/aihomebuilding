# Research: 45L New Energy Efficient Home Credit — Terminated June 30, 2026

**Slug:** 45l-credit-died-june-2026-builder-claim-deadline-2026
**Journalist:** Catherine "Code" Chen (policy/legal beat)
**Article #:** 857

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. Three audiences:
1. Buyers who took title on a new efficient home before July 1, 2026: the builder (not the buyer) gets the credit, up to $5,000/home. Ask whether the builder certified and whether any of it reached your price.
2. Builders with 2026 closings before the deadline: can still claim on the 2026 return (Form 8908). Certification paperwork must exist.
3. Anyone building now: the federal credit is gone; decision math for ENERGY STAR/ZERH certification now rests on energy savings + utility rebates alone.

## Primary sources (verified 2026-09-14)
1. **IRS — Credit for builders of energy-efficient homes** (irs.gov/credits-deductions/credit-for-builders-of-energy-efficient-homes): eligible contractors, up to $5,000/home; homes acquired 2023 through June 30, 2026 qualify; guidance in Notice 2008-35, 2008-36, 2023-65; Form 8908. https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/credit-for-builders-of-energy-efficient-homes
2. **26 USC 45L(h) Termination** (uscode.house.gov): "This section shall not apply to any qualified new energy efficient home acquired after June 30, 2026." Prevailing-wage tiers in 45L(g): $2,500 for ENERGY STAR, $5,000 for ZERH. https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:26%20section:45L%20edition:prelim)
3. **DOE — Section 45L Tax Credits for DOE Efficient New Homes** (energy.gov): IRA amended 45L; applies to homes acquired after 12/31/2022 and before 7/1/2026 per the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025; eligible contractor claims in the tax year the home was acquired. https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/section-45l-tax-credits-doe-efficient-new-homes
4. **IRS Instructions for Form 5695 (2025)**: residential clean energy credit and energy-efficient home improvement credit both terminated for expenditures/property placed in service after 12/31/2025. Confirms the broader 2025-2026 federal incentive wipeout context. https://WWW.IRS.GOV/ru/instructions/i5695
5. **ENERGY STAR Certified Homes v3.1 Cost & Savings Summary** (energystar.gov): EPA's incremental cost/savings estimates for the National Rater Field Checklist (HVAC section net cost -$416 to -$698 depending on climate zone). https://www.energyStar.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/ENERGY%20STAR%20Version%203.1%20Cost%20%20Savings%20Summary.pdf
6. **HERS rating cost data**: national average rating fee ~$450 (Energy Solutions Center/RESNET training material, training.myescenter.com); rating-only fees $350-$573 in SEE Alliance rater variability study (seealliance.org); comprehensive assessment $1,500-$3,000 (realtor.com citing RESNET). Full ENERGY STAR certification (rating + checklist inspections + registration) realistically $800-$1,500/home in most markets.

## Key facts
- 45L credit goes to the ELIGIBLE CONTRACTOR (the builder/developer who constructed the home), not the homebuyer. 26 USC 45L(a)(1).
- Amounts (2023-6/30/2026): single-family ENERGY STAR $2,500; DOE Zero Energy Ready Home $5,000. Multifamily: $500/unit ENERGY STAR base, $1,000/unit ZERH base, rising to $2,500/$5,000 with prevailing wage compliance (45L(g)).
- "Acquired" = the home is acquired by a person for use as a residence. Close of escrow after 6/30/2026 = no credit, even if construction finished in May.
- Claim via Form 8908 in the tax year of acquisition. Builders with pre-deadline closings claim on 2026 returns filed in 2027.
- Certification requires a certified HERS rater, blower-door/duct testing, and checklist verification during construction. Cannot be backfilled after drywall.
- IRA originally extended 45L through 2032; OBBBA (2025) pulled the termination forward to 6/30/2026.

## Original contribution (worked math)
Net-value analysis for a single-family builder: ENERGY STAR certification cost vs $2,500 credit.
- Inputs: rater certification $800-$1,500 (midpoint $1,150; sources above); ENERGY STAR incremental construction cost per EPA's own estimates (HVAC checklist net -$416 to -$698, i.e., roughly cost-neutral to slightly negative in many climate zones; envelope/efficiency upgrades vary).
- Result: on certification cost alone, the $2,500 credit clears by roughly $1,000-$1,700/home before counting any incremental build cost or energy savings. For a 10-home subdivision: ~$25,000 credit vs ~$11,500 certification = ~$13,500 net.
- ZERH tier: $5,000 credit; incremental cost to hit ZERH (HERS ~40-55) is the real question and varies wildly by climate zone; certification ~$1,200-$2,000.
- Assumptions stated in article; regional variance flagged in Limitations.

## Strongest counterargument
The credit's economics were marginal for small/custom builders: rater scheduling during construction, checklist paperwork, and the risk of failing verification after drywall made many builders skip it; multifamily prevailing-wage documentation was a compliance burden that ate the bonus; and the credit went to the builder while the marketing value ("ENERGY STAR certified") was the buyer's benefit, a split incentive. Some builders also note the credit rewarded what Title 24 already required in California, making it a windfall for compliance rather than an incentive for going beyond code.

## Limitations
- Rater fees vary by market; no national survey of 2026 ENERGY STAR certification fees exists. Used ranges from published sources.
- IRS has not published claim statistics for the 2023-2026 45L regime; uptake figures are anecdotal.
- State/utility replacement incentives vary; DSIRE database is the reference, not enumerated here.
- Not tax advice; readers should consult a tax professional (echoing DOE's own disclaimer).

## Actionable takeaways (for article)
- Bought a new home that closed before 7/1/2026? Ask your builder for the ENERGY STAR certificate / HERS rating. If they certified, they banked up to $5,000. Negotiate accordingly on warranty/extras, or at least know where the money went.
- Builder with pre-deadline closings: gather rater documentation now; Form 8908 on the 2026 return.
- Building now: no federal 45L; run the ENERGY STAR math on energy savings + utility rebates alone. In high-rate utility territories (California, New England) the bill savings still carry it.
