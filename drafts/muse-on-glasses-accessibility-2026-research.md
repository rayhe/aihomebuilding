## Why this passes the kill test
A person deciding whether smart glasses plus an AI agent can help a blind/low-vision or hard-of-hearing family member needs: what's real today, what costs what, and what's still a keynote slide. This gives all three — with primary sources.

## Angle (Priya voice)
The accessibility stack arrives in pieces with three different price tags and three different "coming soon" dates. The honest question: for the person who needs help seeing a shelf or hearing a table, what can Muse-plus-glasses do *this week* — and what's still a promise? The math nobody ran: the actual cost of the assistive setup vs. the alternatives, and the cost of the wait.

## Original contribution
Cost-stack calculation: the full "agent that helps you see and hear" setup = glasses ($249–$449) + hearing enhancement ($149.99) + Muse free tier or $20/mo subscription. Compare to AirPods Pro 2 hearing-aid path and prescription aids. Plus: mapping which accessibility-relevant features are SHIPPED vs ANNOUNCED — a real availability ledger, something none of the coverage assembled.

## Limitations (honest)
- Muse-on-glasses is not shipped (Meta says "coming months," no date, no supported-glasses list); all accessibility scenarios for the agent are prospective.
- Hearing enhancement not at launch on Ray-Ban Meta; only select Meta Glasses first, "later this year."
- 142-person study, mild-to-moderate only, no clinic-visit substitution for worse loss.
- CNET hands-on was a floor demo, not an everyday-use test.
- "Cleared" not "approved" — 510(k) substantial equivalence to AirPods Pro 2.

## Strongest counterargument
An accessibility promise tied to a $350 device, a $150 add-on, and a still-unshipped agent integration is a subscription-shaped gate in front of people who need help now. Camera glasses in public raise genuine covert-recording anxiety (users disabling "Hey Meta" in public per the Oct 28 community notes). And Meta's pitch depends on the agent completing tasks reliably in the background — the market-watch coverage notes Meta has published no completion-rate or adoption evidence for the glasses integration.
