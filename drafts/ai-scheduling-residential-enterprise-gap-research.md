# Research Notes: AI Scheduling — Enterprise vs. Residential Gap

## Core Thesis
AI-powered construction scheduling exists and delivers measurable results — but only for projects your homebuyer will never live in. The gap between enterprise construction AI and residential builder reality is enormous.

## Key Data Points

### AI Scheduling Tools — Enterprise Only
- **ALICE Technologies + McKinsey partnership (2026):** Deployed to 35+ clients across infrastructure, data centers, energy, manufacturing. Claims 17% reduction in project duration, 14% in labor costs, 12% in equipment costs.
- ALICE analyzes BIM models and Oracle P6 schedules to generate millions of possible execution paths. Treats labor, equipment, materials, space, and sequence as adjustable variables.
- Zachry Construction used ALICE to reduce a highway project timeline by 28 days.
- Target market: "large complex projects" — infrastructure, healthcare, industrial.
- **Pricing:** Enterprise-level. Not disclosed publicly, but requires BIM models or P6 schedules most residential builders don't have.

### Residential Builder AI Adoption — Almost Zero
- **NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI Survey (July 2025):** Only 20% of single-family builders use AI at all — almost entirely for marketing materials. 11% for market analysis/project planning.
- **Fewer than 5% use AI for any of 10 other business functions,** including scheduling, design, project management, or construction equipment.
- When asked about adopting AI in next 2 years, scheduling scored below 3.0 on a 1-5 likelihood scale.
- Operating automated construction equipment scored lowest at 1.7.

### Construction Delays — The Norm
- **McKinsey:** 98% of construction projects experience cost overruns, delays, or both.
- **KPMG:** Only 25% of projects complete within 10% of their original deadline.
- **North America:** Average project duration extends 37% longer than originally projected (Buildern 2024-2025 data).
- **US Census Bureau (2024):** Average single-family home construction: 9.1 months start to finish. Regional variation: 7.8 months (South Atlantic) to 13.7 months (Middle Atlantic).
- **Census (2023):** Peak was 8.6 months, up from 8.3 in 2022. Homes.com reports trend has started reversing in 2024-2025 due to easing supply constraints.

### Builder Confidence
- **NAHB HMI (July 2026):** 34 — below 50 means more builders view conditions as poor vs good. Has been below 40 for 15 months.

### The Technology Gap
- **Construction schedule software market:** $1.44B in 2025, growing 9.8% CAGR to $2.09B by 2029.
- **ProBuilder/UDenver survey:** 93%+ of builders use some form of scheduling software — but it's basic digital Gantt charts, not AI-optimized. Builders who use scheduling software report frame-to-CO cycle times of ~3 months vs 6.5 month industry average.
- **UK research (Qureshi et al., Frontiers in Built Environment, 2026):** Proposed AI framework for "project resilience" — explicitly a "conceptual framework, not yet a deployed system."

### What AI Could Do for Residential
- Predict weather-related delays and suggest schedule adjustments
- Optimize crew sequencing to minimize trade stack-up
- Forecast material delivery windows and flag supply chain risks
- Run scenario analysis: "What if the window order is 3 weeks late?"
- Buildxact (NAHB-featured) launched AI Estimator — generates category-level estimates in 30 seconds from text prompts. But that's estimating, not scheduling.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home?
**YES.** If you're a homeowner about to build, you should know that the scheduling tools that could predict and mitigate your delays exist for infrastructure projects but aren't available to your builder. This means: (1) ask your builder specifically about their scheduling tools, (2) understand that "we'll start framing in March" is a hope, not a data-driven forecast, (3) build buffer into your own timeline and lease/mortgage overlap planning.
