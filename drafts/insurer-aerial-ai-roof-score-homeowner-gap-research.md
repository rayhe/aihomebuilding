# Research: Insurer Aerial AI Roof Scoring — Homeowner Information Gap

## Core Thesis
Insurance carriers are using AI-powered aerial imagery analysis to score your property's condition from space — assessing roof condition, tree overhang, yard debris, and structures — and using those scores to set premiums, decline coverage, and non-renew policies. Homeowners cannot access the same data, cannot see their scores, and often don't know the AI assessment exists until they receive a non-renewal letter. The same technology could help homeowners proactively identify and fix issues, but it's sold exclusively to insurers.

## Key Data Points

### CAPE Analytics (primary AI vendor)
- Covers 90M+ residential and commercial buildings in the US
- Used by 80+ enterprise clients, including half of the top US property insurers
- Roof Condition Rating (RCR) introduced 2017, first AI+aerial imagery product for insurance
- RCR approved for ratemaking in 15+ states (AZ, CO, IA, KY, IL, IN, MI, MS, NC, OH, PA, SD, TN, VA, WI)
- Generates 80+ property insights: roof condition, roof age, tree overhang, roof geometry, covering material, structure area, wildfire/hail risk scores
- 95% accuracy on roof age detection (precision and recall)
- Raised $31M+
- Source: capeanalytics.com

### Loss Correlations (CAPE-led analysis)
- 40% of insurance claims are roof-related
- Severe/poor condition roofs: 2.5x wind/hail claims frequency vs excellent roofs
- Large tree overhang: 90% higher wind-related losses
- 20% of commercial roofs in poor or severe condition

### Geospatial Insurance Consortium (GIC)
- Industry airplane imagery program covering 99% of US population
- Uses drones, manned airplanes, high-altitude balloons
- Source: uphelp.org

### Consumer Impact Cases
- Joan Van Kuren (California): Spent $200K+ remodeling, CSAA non-renewed after ~40 years as customer because drone images showed "hazards"
- Cindy Picos (Northern California): Dropped by insurer after aerial photos showed "poor roof condition"; insurer refused to show her the photos
- Albert Fox Cahn (STOP founder, NYC): Insurance revoked following AI-powered drone surveillance; resolved only because insurer failed to send required cancellation notice
- Nichole Brink (Farmers Insurance worker): Former employer used aerial photos to drop claims for moss on houses, tree branches; some photos over 2 years old; Farmers mistook shadows for tree limbs in at least one case
- Source: uphelp.org, WSJ, theregister.com

### Regulatory Response
- **California:** AB 1559 (Assemblymember Lisa Calderon) — would require: notify homeowners before capturing aerial photos; prohibit using images >180 days old; share images with homeowners on request; send images with non-renewal notices; allow in-person verification. Also AB 75 with 45-day image freshness requirement.
- California CDI (Ricardo Lara): "investigated numerous complaints where flawed aerial imagery led to wrongful cancellations or nonrenewals... insurers used imprecise drone or satellite photos... resulting in policies being incorrectly dropped due to erroneous data"
- **Connecticut:** March 2024 bulletin requiring carriers to submit underwriting guidelines when decisions based on roof age/condition
- **Pennsylvania:** May 2024 bulletin — aerial imagery alone insufficient for cancellation/non-renewal; requires physical inspection when images inconclusive; "some of the aerial images we've seen barely identify the structure of the home, much less the detailed condition of the roof" (Commissioner Humphreys)
- **Massachusetts:** March 2024 guidance on aerial imagery use
- **United Policyholders proposal:** Share photos with policyholders, detail precise liability, create appeal pathway — NO state has adopted it
- Source: insurify.com, insurancebusinessmag.com, blog.pia.org

### Expert Quotes
- Doug Heller (Consumer Federation of America): "Using images gathered without consumer awareness, or let alone consent, is really problematic"
- Carmen Balber (Consumer Watchdog): "Wild West... technology is all over the map and frequently inaccurate"
- Amy Bach (United Policyholders): "This tech thing is really a very slippery slope for homeowners. We really don't have rules in place to protect the consumer"
- Bob Passmore (APCIA): "just another method of doing something that insurers have always been doing"
- Pennsylvania Commissioner Humphreys: "innovation needs to be reasonable"

### The Gap
- Insurers have AI tools scoring your property continuously from aerial imagery
- Homeowners have NO access to the same data/scores
- No federal regulation requires disclosure of AI property assessments
- Only a handful of states have even begun addressing the issue
- A non-renewal for roof condition can affect eligibility with ALL future insurers
- The same AI could help homeowners: identify problems before they become claims, prioritize maintenance, document improvements for premium reductions
