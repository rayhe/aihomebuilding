# Research: Muse Won't Sit In on Your Meeting. Hand It the Recording and It Does the Part Nobody Wants.

- **Slug:** muse-meeting-recap-after-the-meeting-2026
- **Journalist:** Frank DeLuca (productivity systems, life operations)
- **Date:** November 25, 2026
- **Headline:** Muse Won't Sit In on Your Meeting. Hand It the Recording and It Does the Part Nobody Wants.

## Self-critique gate

- **Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** Muse cannot join your meeting or record it. But through its Zoom and Granola connectors it can read the notes and transcripts afterward, then do the part nobody wants: draft the follow-up in the client's own words, schedule the next step, file the decision. The transcription is 10 percent of the job; the follow-up is the other 90, and the specialist notetakers do not do it.
- **Challenge:** Meeting notes are a crowded market (Otter, Fireflies, Granola, Read AI, Fathom). Is this just another notetaker story? No: the Muse angle is genuinely distinct because the note is the input and the follow-up work is the product, and because the failure mode is different and worse — a notetaker that mishears leaves a wrong sentence in a document, while an agent that mishears sends a wrong email or books a wrong meeting. Plus the California consent-law angle is real and undercovered for agents. Proceed.
- **Kill test:** Helps a reader use Muse better (the exact setup: Granola/Zoom connectors, the draft-before-send rule) and make an informed trust decision (what it can and cannot record, the consent law, the training toggle). Passes.

## Verified facts

### What Muse can and cannot do with meetings

- Muse launched September 8, 2026; Muse for Small Business launched September 29, 2026 in the US and Canada with 15 work connectors, including Zoom and Granola. (eesel.ai, Meta Muse for work; salesgear.io)
- Zoom announced September 29, 2026 that its integration brings Zoom meeting intelligence into Muse via the Zoom MCP server: ask Muse to find relevant conversations and content from Zoom Meetings and My Notes, retrieve summaries and key takeaways, schedule new meetings, and take action on what comes next. (Zoom newsroom, news.zoom.com/zoom-muse-from-meta/)
- Zoom's marketplace listing for Muse: connect Zoom to Muse to search meetings and messages, pull recordings, transcripts and notes, schedule meetings, and create and share Zoom Docs. Scopes include meeting:read, cloud_recording:read, my_notes:read. Recordings need a Pro or higher Zoom plan with cloud recording on. (proprietorsguide.com, verified Nov 11, 2026)
- Granola connector: find and review your meeting notes. Granola captures audio on-device with no bot in the call. (agentready.co; eesel.ai)
- Muse does NOT join and record meetings itself. An independent capability comparison (notis.ai, last checked August 21, 2026) lists "Joins and records your meetings" as a gap for Muse, with nothing documented. No Meta page claims Muse joins calls.
- The documented workflow, from a sales setup guide: "After each Zoom call with a prospect: 1. Read the Granola notes. 2. Draft a follow-up with the three things they cared about, in their words, and the one next step we agreed. 3. Save it as a draft and ask me before sending." (salesgear.io)
- Muse plans: $0, $20, or $100 a month. (eesel.ai) Meeting follow-up rides inside the subscription a user already pays; there is no per-meeting charge, but scheduled runs consume the weekly allowance, so a daily meeting digest costs allowance every time it runs. (alwaysongtm.com)

### The specialist notetakers: what they charge

Prices rechecked on vendors' own pages November 19-21, 2026 (teamshift.io, professionalstoolkit.com, thetechhacker.com, aiwithghost.com):

- Otter Pro: $8.33/user/mo billed annually ($16.99 monthly). 1,200 transcription minutes a month, 90-minute cap per meeting. Business: $19.99/mo annual ($30 monthly), unlimited meetings, 4-hour cap.
- Fireflies Pro: $10/seat/mo billed annually ($18 monthly). Unlimited transcription and AI summaries, 8,000 minutes of storage per seat. Business: $19/mo annual.
- Read AI Pro: $15/user/mo billed annually ($19.75 monthly). Free plan: 5 transcripts a month.
- Fathom Premium: $16/mo annual ($20 monthly). Free plan: unlimited recordings and transcripts.
- Granola Business: $14/user/mo billed annually. Removes the free plan's 30-day history limit; adds unlimited notes and history, pre-meeting briefs, follow-up emails, integrations, MCP across apps.

### Original calculation 1: the 25-hour month

For the same workload — 25 hours of meetings a month, one person, one year, billed annually — a November 20, 2026 side-by-side read of the three vendors' pricing pages (aiwithghost.com) gives: Granola $168, Fireflies $228, Otter $239.88. The arithmetic that matters: Otter Pro's 1,200-minute cap is 20 hours, which is below the 25-hour workload, and Otter minutes do not roll over, so the workload forces the Business tier. Granola charges by history, Otter by transcription minutes, Fireflies by stored minutes. Three tools, three different meters, and the cheapest headline price is not the cheapest bill.

Muse's marginal price for the same workload is $0 on top of a subscription the user already holds ($0/$20/$100 a month), because the follow-up work is not metered per meeting. The honest caveat: it is only $0 if you already pay for Muse, and scheduled runs draw on the weekly allowance.

### Original calculation 2: the follow-up is the job

A rough time budget, stated as an estimate with assumptions shown: a worker with 25 hours of meetings a month sits in roughly 30 meetings. If each meeting generates 15 minutes of follow-up admin — the recap, the email, the calendar invite, the task entry — that is 7.5 hours a month, about a full working day, spent on work that starts after the meeting ends. The transcription itself takes zero of those minutes; it is automated everywhere now. The notetaker tools sell the transcript. Muse sells the 7.5 hours. That is the claim to test, and the test is whether the drafts it writes are good enough to send with a glance instead of a rewrite.

### The law: recording a meeting you are in

- California is an all-party consent state. Penal Code Section 632 makes it illegal to record or eavesdrop on a confidential conversation without the consent of every person taking part, whether in person, by phone, or on a video call. Section 632.7 separately covers calls involving a cell phone or cordless phone without a confidentiality requirement. (righettilaw.com; recordinglaw.com, audited November 2, 2026)
- Civil damages under Penal Code Section 637.2: the greater of $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages. Criminal penalties up to $2,500 and one year in jail for a first offense. (recordinglaw.com)
- About nine states clearly require all-party consent (California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, and others). Federal law and most states require only one party's consent (18 U.S.C. Section 2511). The working rule courts apply: the strictest law among the participants' locations can govern. (laxis.com)
- Multiple class action lawsuits have been filed against AI transcription services, including Otter.ai and Google, under California's Invasion of Privacy Act. (recordinglaw.com)
- Practical rule: if anyone on the call is in an all-party state, or you do not know where everyone is, get everyone's consent. A notice at the top of the call plus staying on the line is generally treated as consent. (laxis.com; righettilaw.com)
- Note: Muse's design sidesteps part of this problem by accident — it does not join the call, so there is no bot to announce. But the recording still happens somewhere (Granola on your device, Zoom cloud recording), and the person who decides to record is the person the law holds responsible, not the software. (laxis.com)

### The trust catches

- One person's agent: no admin console, no team plan, no SSO. IT cannot see what it connects to. Muse stays on the user's personal Meta account when they leave the company. (eesel.ai)
- AI training is switched on by default; the advice from the work guide is to switch the training toggle off on day one. (eesel.ai)
- Meta does not review custom connectors or how they use your information. (eesel.ai, quoting Meta's help page)
- AI-generated notes can miss or misstate things; review them before relying on or sharing them. The transcript is the record of exactly what was said — treat it as the source of truth. (meeting.ai docs)
- Muse's Supplemental Terms (September 8, 2026) ban using it to decide, or be a substantial factor in deciding, whether someone gets a job without meaningful human review — evidence the terms do constrain consequential decisions, and the same logic applies to acting on meeting outcomes. (eesel.ai, recruiting guide)

## Strongest counterargument

The whole premise is shaky in two directions. First, a dedicated notetaker is better at the note itself: Granola's bot-free capture and templates, Otter's speaker identification, Fireflies' 100-plus languages — Muse reads their output, it does not produce it, so if the transcript is wrong, everything downstream is wrong, and Muse adds a second layer of interpretation on top of a first layer that already makes mistakes. Second, the follow-up work Muse offers is the part most worth keeping human: a follow-up email in the client's own words, drafted by an agent that was not in the room, can sound right and be wrong in a way a transcript cannot, because a transcript is a record and a draft is a commitment. The honest version of this product is a fast first draft with a human's name on it, not an autopilot for relationships.

## Limitations (what this piece does NOT prove)

- We did not run a controlled test of Muse's meeting follow-up against Otter, Fireflies, or Granola on the same meeting, so the quality comparison is structural (what each tool does with the note) rather than measured.
- Pricing is list pricing from vendor pages as rechecked November 19-21, 2026 by the cited comparison sites; vendors run promotions (Otter showed a sale price at the time of writing) and prices move.
- The 7.5-hour follow-up estimate is our arithmetic from stated assumptions (30 meetings, 15 minutes each), not a measured time study.
- The notis.ai capability table was last checked August 21, 2026; Muse's meeting capabilities may have changed since, and no Meta page we found claims Muse joins calls as of November 25, 2026.
- The California law summary is general information from law-firm and legal-guide sources, not legal advice.
