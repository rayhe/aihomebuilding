# Research: Foundation Monitoring — Residential Access Gap

## Core Angle
Continuous foundation monitoring technology exists and is sold to residential homeowners — but ONLY through foundation repair contractors. A homeowner who wants preventive monitoring before damage occurs cannot buy it independently. The companies selling the monitoring are the same ones who profit from the repair. Meanwhile, commercial buildings and bridges have had continuous structural health monitoring (SHM) for decades.

## Key Sources

### 1. ORLY Foundation Monitoring System (Olshan Foundation)
- 24/7 continuous monitoring with hourly data collection
- Patented by Steadfast Foundation Technologies LLC, licensed to Olshan
- Alerts homeowners to foundation movement via app
- Also monitored by Olshan's "Certified Structural Technicians"
- Sold exclusively through Olshan
- Acts as "check engine light" for homes
- Claims to increase resale value
- Source: https://www.olshanfoundation.com/services/foundation-repair/foundation-monitoring-system/

### 2. Slabsure Foundation Monitoring System (G.L. Hunt)
- Patented below-ground sensors monitoring foundation movement
- Real-time diagnostic analysis
- G.L. Hunt is "exclusive distributor" in Dallas/Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio
- Also positioned as "check engine light for your home"
- Source: https://glhunt.com/foundation-repair/foundation-monitoring/

### 3. US Patent US20240271384A1 — Smart Home Foundation Prevention
- Filed 2024, published for examination
- Pressure sensors positioned against/within foundation
- AI analyzes pressure measurements over time to detect movement
- Can trigger automatic watering system to prevent soil shrinkage
- Targets the prevention, not just detection
- Not yet commercialized
- Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240271384A1/en

### 4. Smartphone-Based AI Crack Measurement (Scientific Reports 2026)
- Low-cost framework using smartphone + calibration board + deep learning
- Measures crack widths on RC structures
- Validated on residential buildings with differential settlement
- 12-megapixel camera from 1m distance, 400mm x 300mm field of view
- Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-50880-w

### 5. MDPI Systematic Review of AI Crack Detection (2026)
- 146 Scopus-indexed publications analyzed (2015-2025)
- "predictive diagnostics, automated inspection reporting, and decision-oriented SHM are still rarely addressed"
- Most studies focus on detection (YOLO-based), not prediction or prevention
- Research concentrated in limited communities
- Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8392/6/4/75

### 6. Foundation Repair Cost Data (2026)
- National average: $5,176-$5,200 (Angi, Fixr, This Old House consensus)
- Range: $2,200-$8,200 typical
- Underpinning/piering: $10,000-$40,000
- Crack sealing: $200-$1,000
- Olshan's own data: ~$11,950 average in 2026
- Cable Lock ST Plus system: $1,825-$3,350 per pier
- Sources: Modernize, Angi, This Old House, Fixr, Olshan

## Conflict of Interest Angle
Both ORLY and Slabsure are sold through foundation repair companies:
- Olshan = foundation repair company that also sells monitoring
- G.L. Hunt = foundation repair company that also sells monitoring
- Neither product is available at retail (Home Depot, Lowe's, Amazon)
- The monitoring company profits when it detects a problem it can then fix
- This is like your mechanic being the only one who can sell you a check engine light

## The Commercial SHM Gap
- Bridges: continuous SHM is standard practice with accelerometers, strain gauges, GPS, fiber optic sensors
- Commercial buildings: routine SHM required for many high-rises and critical structures
- Residential: nothing. No building code requires it. No independent product exists at retail.
- The SHM market was valued at ~$2.3-3.0 billion globally (multiple market research firms)

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES.
- Homebuyer should know monitoring exists and ask about it
- Homeowner in expansive soil regions should know about preventive monitoring
- The conflict of interest is something every homeowner should understand
- The patent shows where tech is heading — foundation prevention, not just detection
