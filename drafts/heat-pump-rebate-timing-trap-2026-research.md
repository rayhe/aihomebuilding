# Research: The $14,000 Rebate Your Contractor Didn't Mention (HEEHRA order-of-operations trap)

**Slug:** `heat-pump-rebate-timing-trap-2026`
**Journalist:** Priya Greenwood (sustainability / utility bills)
**Angle:** Up to $14,000 per household in federal electrification rebates exists, but the money is point-of-sale, state-run, and timing-gated. Install before your state launches or before your reservation is approved and you forfeit it. Your contractor is the least reliable source of this information.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A homeowner planning HVAC, water heater, panel, or insulation work in 2026 needs to know: (1) check whether their state program is live BEFORE signing a contract, (2) get the income verification and reservation approved BEFORE the install date, (3) their contractor is not required to know about the rebates and half of surveyed contractors said they needed more information to even pick a pathway.

## Primary sources (6)

1. **NH Dept. of Energy HEEHRA page** — rebate schedule and tiers: heat pump HVAC $8,000; heat pump water heater $1,750; stove/range $840; heat pump dryer $840; panel upgrade $4,000; wiring $2,500; insulation/air sealing/ventilation $1,600; installation $500. $14,000 household max. <80% AMI = 100% of cost; 80-150% AMI = 50%. NH program approved fall 2024, launch anticipated spring 2026; projects initiated BEFORE launch not eligible.
   https://energy.nh.gov/funding-opportunities/funding-opportunities-homeowners/home-electrification-and-appliance-rebates

2. **CA Governor's office (Nov 2024)** — California HEEHRA launch: 80-150% AMI up to $4,000; <80% AMI up to $8,000; stackable with other incentives. techcleanca.com/heehra.
   http://www.gov.ca.gov/2024/11/14/california-launches-new-rebates-to-help-cut-home-energy-costs/

3. **CA HEEHRA Phase I filing (Nov 2025)** — income verifications open; reservations required BEFORE install ("Projects installed before receiving an approved reservation are not eligible"); only through TECH-certified and HEEHRA-trained contractors; first-come first-served. Also confirms 25C tax credit expired end of 2025 (heat pumps must be in service by 12/31/2025).
   https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=267738

4. **DOE Market Transformation Plan Guidance** — remodels are missed efficiency opportunities: "when homeowners contract with residing companies, they may not ask about opportunities to insulate, and contractors may be unlikely to suggest insulation if it requires coordination with other companies"; rebates are the lever to engage remodelers/siding companies. 40% of funds to Disadvantaged Communities.
   https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/market-transformation-guidance_010725.pdf

5. **BPA / AnnDyl Policy Group contractor survey (Nov 2022-Jan 2023)** — 1,154 contractors across all 50 states + DC. 49% said they would need to learn more to decide which HOMES pathway (measured vs modeled) to pursue. 57% would market rebates to LMI households but "significant concern with how income qualification would work and how the point-of-sale rebate requirement would work."
   https://building-performance.org/contractors-weigh-in-on-homes-and-heehra-rebate-implementation/

6. **CRS IF12258 (congress.gov)** — statutory text: 42 U.S.C. 18795; $4.275B HEEHRA + $225M tribal; per-measure caps; installer can receive up to $500 additional per project; MFB rules.
   https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12258

**Supporting:** Canary Media via SEJ (Aug 2024) — only NY and WI live at that point; 50 of 56 entities intended to participate; 6 states had not applied. DOE consumer-protection plan requiring post-project surveys. CleanTechnica worked examples (heat pump water heater $2,500 -> $750 out of pocket at <80% AMI).

## Original contribution (worked stacking example)
Heat pump HVAC, $14,000 installed:
- Household at 75% AMI (low-income tier): HEEHRA pays $8,000 (100% up to measure cap). Out of pocket: $6,000.
- Household at 100% AMI (moderate tier): HEEHRA pays 50% = $7,000. Out of pocket: $7,000.
- Same install completed one month before the state's launch (NH: spring 2026) or before reservation approval (CA): rebate $0. Out of pocket: $14,000.
The timing decision is worth $7,000-$8,000. The 25C credit (up to $2,000/yr for heat pumps) expired 12/31/2025, so HEEHRA is now the primary federal money for electrification, not a bonus stack.

## Limitations
- State-by-state status is fluid; NH spring-2026 launch and CA Phase I dates are from official pages/filings but launch dates slip. Cannot confirm live-count as of Sept 2026 from these sources.
- Contractor survey is from 2022-2023 (pre-launch); contractor knowledge may have improved, but the knowledge gap documented is directional.
- Installed-cost figures ($14,000 heat pump) are representative, not a national average; costs vary by region, capacity, and electrical work needed.
- Rebate is point-of-sale: the contractor must be enrolled and front the discount, which many small shops will not do.

## Strongest counterargument
The Public Counsel filing at the CA PUC warns the flip side: HEEHRA targets low-income households with generous rebates, which is exactly the population PACE contractors exploited. Public Counsel recommended contractors not be allowed to market the rebates door-to-door and not be allowed to sell financing alongside them. The same information gap that lets money go unclaimed also creates a predatory-sales vector. The answer is not "trust your contractor to tell you" but verified, state-run contractor lists and income pre-verification before anyone signs anything.
