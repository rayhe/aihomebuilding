# Research: AI Photo Documentation Gap — Commercial vs. Residential Construction

## Angle
Commercial construction now has AI-powered 360° photo documentation that auto-maps imagery to blueprints, catches defects during the build, and creates timestamped legal evidence for disputes. Residential construction — where the homeowner's entire net worth is on the line — gets a guy with a phone taking random photos that get lost in a camera roll. The technology exists. Nobody sells it to the people who need it most.

## Primary Sources

### 1. AI Photo Documentation Tools (Commercial-Only)
- **OpenSpace** (openspace.ai): 360° video capture, document 25K sq ft in 10 minutes, auto-maps to floor plans, Split View for date comparison, BIM Compare, 3D scan with 2-inch measurement accuracy
- **Trimble ProjectSight 360 Capture** (launched Nov 2025, North America availability announced Jun 2026): AI automatically maps user's path, aligns images to drawings, AI-powered face-blurring for privacy. Natively integrated into project management workflows (RFIs, change orders)
- **PlanRadar SiteView**: AI-powered 360° capture, images auto-mapped to 2D plans, "see behind the wall" capability
- **CoStar acquired Matterport for $1.6B** (April 2024) — 3D spatial capture for commercial real estate, NOT residential construction documentation
- **EarthCam Mobile StreamCam Robotic** (Oct 2025): AI-driven 360° autonomous movement, HD streaming, AI analytics for interior construction monitoring

### 2. Rework & Documentation Data
- **CII (Construction Industry Institute)**: Direct rework costs average 5% of total construction costs (359 projects studied)
- **PlanRadar QA/QC Impact Report 2025** (811 professionals, 13 countries):
  - 77% report inconsistent documentation varying across projects, sites, and trades
  - Companies with consistent QA/QC: 56% keep rework under 5% vs 37% without
  - Firms without QA/QC: 21% more likely to experience avoidable rework
  - 50% more likely to face warranty exposure
  - 23% more likely to have subcontractor disputes
  - Companies without defined QA/QC: 43% have NO view of rework costs (vs 22%)
  - Companies with strong QA/QC: 25-28% more likely to achieve profit margins above 3%
- **PlanGrid & FMI (2018)**: Miscommunication causes 26% of all rework; bad data causes 22%
- **Autodesk & FMI (2021)**: Bad data causes 14% of rework
- Rework contributes to 52% of total cost growth in construction projects
- Schedule overruns of up to 22% attributable to rework

### 3. Legal Implications
- **Spoliation doctrine**: Courts have imposed sanctions on parties that destroy or deprive opponents of evidence. Extended from documents to building conditions in construction defect cases.
- Before tearing out defective work, must allow opposing party to inspect, test, and document. Failure = spoliation sanctions, jeopardized right to recover damages.
- **Woullard case**: Stone veneer defective construction cost $61,000 (12% of home's $528K market value). Back porch: $39,700 (8%). Burden on contractors to prove diminution — good documentation matters.

### 4. The Gap
- All major AI photo documentation platforms (OpenSpace, Trimble, PlanRadar, EarthCam) target commercial construction
- Pricing: OpenSpace and similar platforms are enterprise-priced ($3K-$10K+/month)
- A typical custom home ($400K-$800K) gets maybe 50-100 random phone photos taken by the homeowner during site visits
- Commercial project: AI-mapped 360° captures at every stage, timestamped, geo-located, overlaid on blueprints
- Residential project: "I think that photo is from March? Maybe April. I don't know what room that wall is in."
- A $600 camera setup (3D camera + 360° light + battery + charger + monopod) + iPad could do the same thing on a residential site
- The hardware is cheap. The software is enterprise-gated.

### 5. Cost Context
- Average new single-family home construction cost: ~$300K-$500K (varies by region)
- 5% rework on a $400K home = $20,000
- A $600 camera rig + $200/month software subscription = $2,400 for a 6-month build
- If it prevents ONE rework event, it pays for itself 8x over

## Kill Test
✅ Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — a homeowner building a custom home can demand photo documentation as part of the contract, or hire a third-party inspector to do it. A $600 rig and a 10-minute walk-through every week would create the documentation that commercial buildings get automatically.
