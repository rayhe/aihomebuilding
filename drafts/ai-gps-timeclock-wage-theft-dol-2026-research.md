## Thesis

The Department of Labor recovered more than $259 million in stolen wages in a single year, and construction is the worst offender it measures: $847.9 million in back wages assessed against construction employers over two decades, averaging $1,564 per worker. The most common weapon is not a forged signature. It is a paper timesheet with Saturday missing. GPS-verified time clocks now cost about $10 per worker per month, which makes the cheapest DOL-audit insurance a residential GC can buy also the most honest payroll record a worker will ever get. The twist the industry does not talk about: the same record that protects the worker from the boss protects the boss from the DOL, because when nobody kept time records, the courts believe the worker.

## Kill test

Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes, twice. For GCs and builders: a GPS time record is the difference between a DOL investigation that ends in a lecture and one that ends in triple damages; the break-even math is one avoided claim per decade. For homeowners: if your builder's subs are not paid, they can put a lien on YOUR house even though you paid the builder in full (California Civil Code 8400 et seq., with the 20-day preliminary notice). Verifying that payroll is real is a homeowner protection move, not just a labor story.

## Original contribution (the math nobody did)

Break-even for a 10-person residential crew: GPS-verified time tracking at $10/user/mo = $1,200/year. One DOL investigation finding the EPI-average underpayment ($1,564/worker) across 10 workers = $15,640 in back wages + $15,640 in liquidated damages (current law: equal to back wages) = $31,280, before civil penalties. The software costs 1/26th of a single average finding. Under the proposed bills (2x liquidated + penalties up to $10K/employee), the same finding approaches $150,000. Stated plainly: the app pays for itself if it prevents one claim per 26 years at current law, one claim per century-plus under the proposed regime. The record is the defense: under the Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery burden-shifting standard (and the proposed "clear and convincing" rebuttal bar), an employer with no time records loses the argument about hours to the worker's testimony. The GPS log is the employer's alibi.

## The honest AI gap

The "AI" in these tools is thin: GPS breadcrumbing, geofence entry/exit timestamps, photo verification on punch, anomaly flags for impossible travel between sites. None of the major vendors publish a trained model that detects wage theft; the value is deterministic recordkeeping, not machine learning. Say so. The genuine innovation is evidentiary, not algorithmic: a tamper-evident, timestamped, location-stamped record of hours worked, which is exactly what DOL investigators and courts ask for. Do not claim the app "detects wage theft with AI." It creates the record that makes theft provable or disprovable.

## Counterarguments to include (full strength)

- Surveillance cuts both ways: workers reasonably read GPS breadcrumbing as boss spyware, and adoption without trust poisons the crew. Several vendors' app-store reviews are notably worse than their B2B software ratings, and the field experience (battery drain, false geofence exits, punishing rural dead zones) is the workers' daily reality. A tool the crew sabotages produces worse records than paper.
- Wage theft's worst forms (off-books cash, misclassification) are invisible to any time clock: if the worker is not in the system, the system records nothing. The ILEPI finding that ~100,000 workers are misclassified or off-books means the app only helps workers already on payroll.
- Cost is not the barrier for bad actors; intent is. An employer stealing wages will not buy the instrument of their own conviction. The honest framing: this protects honest GCs from DOL findings and from sub-tier theft they did not commit, more than it reforms thieves.
- The proposed federal bills are bills, not law; citing 2x/3x damages as current exposure would be wrong. Current FLSA liquidated damages equal the back wages (1x), doubling the hit.

## Actionable takeaways (required in article)

- If you run a residential crew: $6-$12/user/mo for GPS-verified time tracking (Workyard, busybusy, ClockShark, ExakTime). Require geofenced clock-in/out; export weekly to payroll. Keep 4+ years of records (proposed lookback) even though current FLSA recordkeeping is 3 years for payroll records.
- If you hire a GC: put certified payroll / lien-release language in the contract; require conditional and unconditional lien releases from every sub and supplier before each progress payment; confirm the 20-day preliminary notices arrived (in California they are your early warning system).
- If a sub on your project is not getting paid: the preliminary notice is the document that matters. No notice, no lien rights (with narrow exceptions for individual laborers).
- The number to remember: $1,564. That is the average back-wage finding per construction worker in two decades of DOL enforcement. Multiply by your crew size; that is the check the app is written against.
