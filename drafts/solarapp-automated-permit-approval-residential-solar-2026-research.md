# Research: SolarAPP+ Automated Solar Permitting
**Date:** September 10, 2026 | **Journalist:** Catherine Chen (policy/permitting) | **Slug:** `solarapp-automated-permit-approval-residential-solar-2026`

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES, directly. Anyone adding rooftop solar: whether your city uses SolarAPP+ determines whether your permit takes under a day or up to nine weeks. Actionable: look up your AHJ on the SolarAPP+ adopter list; ask your installer to file through it.

## Core story
A federal lab (NREL) built a free automated plan-review tool that approves code-compliant residential solar permits instantly. In the pilot, review time dropped to under a day and projects finished 12 days faster. Five years later, only a few hundred of ~19,000 US jurisdictions use it, while installers in slow-permit towns charge 10-20% premiums or skip those towns entirely. In Oct 2025, Colorado's DORA adopted it statewide-ish for electrical plan review across 200+ jurisdictions, including battery storage.

## Primary sources (6)
1. **NREL pilot study** (NREL/TP-7A40-81603, 2022): 5 AHJs (Menifee CA; Pima County AZ; Pleasant Hill CA; Stockton CA; Tucson AZ). Findings: 383 hrs spent on implementation; 236 hrs saved on permit/revision review during pilot; average permit review time reduced to less than one day; SolarAPP+ projects installed and inspected on average 12 days faster; inspection passage rates comparable to traditional process. https://nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/81603.pdf
2. **DOE/Energy.gov SolarAPP+ overview** (updated ~2026): projects permitted, installed, and inspected 13 days sooner than traditional; staff save 15-60 minutes of review time per permit; SolarAPP+ Foundation established to transition from NREL to sustainable long-term operation; expanded into storage permitting; exploring heat pumps and other clean-energy tech. https://www.energy.gov/technologycommercialization/articles/streamlining-permitting-solarapp
3. **pv magazine USA** (July 2021, NREL webinar via SEIA): $25 per approved application (installer-paid); up to 3 corrected applications free; pilot jurisdictions reduced their permit fees, netting average $6 savings per permit even with the platform fee; application completable in ~15 minutes with spec sheet in hand; eligibility: 2017 NEC or later + 2018 I-code or later (covers all of California; 1,200+ jurisdictions in 20+ states as of 2021); solar+storage "soon" per program lead Jeff Cook. https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2021/07/02/residential-solar-installers-can-cut-permitting-times-with-nrel-built-automated-app/
4. **City of New Orleans** (Jan 2, 2025): first Louisiana jurisdiction to adopt; 250+ jurisdictions nationwide at that time; platform "reducing permitting timelines by an average of three weeks." http://nola.gov/next/mayors-office/news/articles/2025/january-2025/2025-01-02-new-orleans-becomes-first-louisiana-jurisdiction-to-adopt-solarapp-for-solar-permitting/
5. **SolarAPP Foundation / GlobeNewswire** (Oct 28, 2025): Colorado DORA selected SolarAPP+ for electrical plan review of residential solar AND energy storage systems in 200+ Colorado jurisdictions (joining Alamosa County, Bennett, Denver, Gilpin County, Wheat Ridge); free for jurisdictions; no cost to DORA. https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/10/28/3175658/0/en/SolarAPP-Automates-Electrical-Permitting-for-Over-200-Colorado-Jurisdictions-with-the-Department-of-Regulatory-Agencies.html
6. **DOE/CleanTechnica** (Dec 2023): installer survey — many installers avoid communities with burdensome permitting or charge a 10-20% price premium; NREL 2023 PV cost benchmark interviews: "increased adoption of SolarAPP+" among the most frequently mentioned residential cost reducers. https://www.energy.gov/eere/solar/articles/160-communities-now-automating-solar-permitting-solarapp

## Original contribution: the math
- **Delay cost to homeowner:** 13 days faster to permission-to-operate (energy.gov figure). A typical 8 kW residential system offsets roughly $150-250/month of utility bills (varies by rate). 13 days of earlier operation = ~$65-110 in bill savings per install, before counting soft-cost pass-through. (Calculation: ($150-250 / 30) x 13.)
- **Permit fee math:** $25 SolarAPP+ application fee vs. traditional residential solar permit fees commonly $200-500+; pilot AHJs cut their fees enough that net average savings were $6 per permit even after the $25 fee (pv magazine). Small per-permit, but the time savings dominate.
- **Staff time:** 15-60 min saved per permit; Culpeper County VA reported ~1 FTE equivalent saved in 1.5 years with 130 permits (Piedmont Environmental Council brief). Extrapolating staff-time savings across 250+ adopting jurisdictions is left as an exercise — the per-permit figure is the honest unit.
- **The penetration gap:** ~250-450 adopting jurisdictions (2025 figures) vs. ~19,000 US AHJs. NREL's SolarTRACE tracks 700+ jurisdictions' wait times; the worst permit backlogs cluster in exactly the jurisdictions least likely to adopt.

## Skepticism / what doesn't work
- Self-selected early adopters: the 5 pilot AHJs volunteered; results may not generalize to understaffed rural building departments.
- Coverage is narrow: standard rooftop PV + storage + main panel upgrades only. Ground-mount, complex structural, historic districts, and local amendments (e.g., CA fire setbacks under CRC R324) still need human review.
- Eligibility floor (2017 NEC / 2018 I-code) excludes jurisdictions on older codes.
- Training burden shifted to installer permit techs entering single-line-diagram data; pilot participants (Sunrun, Tesla, SunPower) reported a learning curve.
- Fee model: $25/app to installers; the Foundation's long-term sustainability is still proving out.

## Strongest counterargument
Permitting was never the only bottleneck, and in many markets it is not even the binding one. Utility interconnection queues routinely add weeks to months after the permit is issued, and SolarAPP+ does nothing about the utility. An instant city permit followed by a 10-week interconnection study is a faster line item inside the same slow project. Critics can fairly ask whether automating plan review is optimizing the wrong queue — though the honest answer is that both queues matter and only one of them currently has a free federal tool.

## Limitations
- NREL pilot: 5 AHJs, 2021-22, self-selected, AZ/CA only. The 12-day figure is a pilot result, not a national average.
- The "37% less likely to fail inspection" claim (Piedmont Environmental Council brief) comes from an advocacy source, not a peer-reviewed study; treat as directional.
- Dollar savings per homeowner ($65-110) are the author's calculation from NREL's 13-day figure and typical bill offsets, not a measured outcome.
- Adoption counts (250+ as of Jan 2025; 200+ CO jurisdictions Oct 2025) are point-in-time press figures; the current national total should be checked against the live adopter list.

## Actionable takeaways (for article)
1. Before signing a solar contract, ask the installer which AHJ reviews your permit and whether that AHJ uses SolarAPP+. If yes, expect days, not weeks.
2. The $25 application fee is real but trivial against permit fees and delay costs; don't let an installer blame "the city" without naming the actual review timeline.
3. If your city is slow, the 10-20% installer premium data point is negotiation leverage: get a second bid from an installer active in a SolarAPP+ jurisdiction nearby.
4. Storage is now covered in the newest deployments (Colorado DORA, Oct 2025) — solar+storage in one automated review.
