## Thesis
The daily log is the most boring document on a residential job and the most valuable one in a dispute. AI tools can now generate it from photos, voice notes, and site context for ~$119-199/month. The catch: courts admit daily reports under the business-records exception only if they meet three conditions (timely, author had personal knowledge, kept as regular practice), and an AI-authored log strains at least one of them. The fix is a human sign-off ritual that costs two minutes and converts AI output into admissible evidence.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes — residential GCs lose delay claims constantly over documentation gaps; a $1,400-2,400/year tool that produces court-admissible daily records is the cheapest dispute insurance on the job.

## Original contribution (novel analysis)
Cross-reference the three Arrowood/Miller Nash admissibility conditions against AI-generated daily logs:
- **Timeliness (PASS):** AI logs generated the same day from timestamped photos actually BEAT hand-written logs — the super who writes Friday's log on Monday creates the exact "days or weeks after" problem Miller Nash warns gets reports excluded.
- **Personal knowledge (CONDITIONAL):** the AI was not on site. The fix: a daily 2-minute human review-and-sign converts the document from "AI output" to "a record made from information transmitted by someone with knowledge" — and the rule explicitly allows that if the reviewer verifies. Nobody has published this mapping.
- **Regular practice (PASS with caveat):** automated generation makes logs MORE consistent, but sporadic use ("we turned it on for the dispute job") looks like preparation for litigation, which the rule says impugns objectivity. So run it on every job or none.

Original calculation — the documentation ROI math: a superintendent spending 25 min/day on logs × 250 workdays = ~104 hours/year; at a loaded super cost of $75/hr that's ~$7,800/year of labor. CompanyCam Crew at $119/mo = $1,428/year. If AI cuts log time to 5 min/day (photo capture + 2-min review), the saved ~83 hours = ~$6,200 in labor value, a ~4.3x payback before counting a single avoided dispute. Assumptions stated; numbers use CompanyCam's published pricing and a mid-market super rate.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The strongest case against AI daily logs is that they create a new liability surface. An AI that hallucinates a work description into a legal document does not merely fail to help — it hands the other side's lawyer a weapon: one fabricated entry impeaches the credibility of the entire log series, and spoliation-adjacent questions about who edited what and when can turn a $40,000 delay claim into a sanctions motion. CompanyCam's own FAQ admits the tools need review. A super who signs AI logs without reading them is arguably worse off than one who wrote terse honest logs by hand, because the volume of AI output makes cursory review feel sufficient when it is not. The technology concentrates the exact human weakness it claims to solve: inattention.

## Limitations
- No independent audit exists of AI daily-log accuracy rates; vendor case studies (EJH/Benetics) are marketing, not controlled studies.
- Labor math uses assumed $75/hr loaded super rate and 25 min/day — both vary by market; union markets run higher, small residential crews lower.
- Legal analysis is drawn from published law-firm guidance and a construction contracting textbook, not a court ruling on AI-generated logs specifically — no such ruling is known to exist as of Sept 2026.
- Pricing is CompanyCam's published annual-billing list; Procore and others do not publish list pricing.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you run $2M+ in residential work: put CompanyCam Crew ($119/mo) or equivalent on every job, not just the litigious ones; sporadic use kills the "regular practice" element of admissibility.
- Institute the 2-minute sign-off: super reviews and digitally signs the AI log the same day. This is the step that converts AI output into a business record under the Arrowood conditions.
- Capture photos as the input, not typed notes: timestamped, GPS-tagged photos are the raw material AI logs and courts both trust.
- Send formal delay notices on the contract deadline regardless of log quality — logs support the claim; notice preserves it (miss notice and the claim can be waived entirely).
