# Research: Florida's 2026 Wind-Mitigation Form Reset — The AI That Reads It, and the Credits Homeowners Never Collect

**Slug:** `wind-mitigation-1802-form-reset-credit-paperwork-gap-2026`
**Journalist:** Jake Kowalski
**Date:** September 13, 2026
**Kill test:** Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — Florida homeowners and buyers: how to capture hurricane insurance credits worth up to 88% of the windstorm premium portion, why the state's own $576M program only got 12% of inspected homes a premium drop, and what the April 2026 form rewrite changes.

## Core story
In April 2026, Florida replaced the OIR-B1-1802 Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form for the first time since 2012. The new Rev. 04/26 form demands stricter photo documentation, adds wind zone and roof slope fields, adds a FORTIFIED Home section, and requires permit records and product approval numbers for many credit categories. Around the same time, carriers started deploying AI to read the forms (Exzeo's WindForm Pro, launched May 2026), because the manual pipeline can't handle the stricter docs. Meanwhile the state's own data shows the credits mostly don't reach homeowners: of 109,443 homes inspected under My Safe Florida Home, only 12,570 owners (about 12%) reported their premiums dropped.

## Primary sources (7)
1. **Florida Statute 627.711** (flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/627.711) — insurers must notify policyholders of wind-mit discount ranges at issuance and each renewal; uniform form mandated; signing a fraudulent form is a first-degree misdemeanor (627.711(7)).
2. **OIR-B1-1802 Rev. 04/26** (insurancejournal.com PDF upload, Mar 2026; insurancejournal.com/app/uploads/2026/03/B1-1802-new-version.pdf) — revised form: updated opening-protection classification tables (Levels A/B/C/D/N/X), valid 5 years, adopted by Rule 69O-170.0155.
3. **Home Inspector Pro help center** (help.homeinspectorpro.com, updated ~Sep 10 2026) — new form "applicable as of April 1, 2026"; inspectors needed new template/version 6.9.1+.
4. **Wescon Construction wind mitigation guide** (wesconconstruction.com, ~Aug 2026) — new form effective April 1, 2026; adds wind zone, roof slope, FORTIFIED Home section; demands permit records and product approval numbers; inspection costs $75-150; old 2012 forms remain valid at most carriers if completed before Apr 1 2026 and within 5-year window.
5. **Exzeo Group (NYSE: XZO) WindForm Pro launch** (businesswire.com, May 6, 2026) — AI-powered data extraction from wind mitigation forms for carriers; built for the OIR's stricter data capture/documentation requirements; live May 5, 2026.
6. **My Safe Florida Home data to FL Senate Banking & Insurance Committee** (Feb 2025, via WFTS/tampabay28.com; Senate President memo Mar 26, 2025, flsenate.gov PDF) — 109,443 initial inspections; 25,530 grants awarded; $240M reimbursed through Dec 2024; Legislature appropriated $576M for grants + $28.5M for 178,184 inspections; of grant recipients, 12,570 saw premiums drop, 7,065 stable, 4,190 increased, 1,705 no data. 12,570/109,443 = 11.5% of inspected homes with a documented drop. Portal reopened for ~10,000 inspected-but-unawarded homes; $280M in FY2025-26 budget ($94M grants + $180M nonrecurring).
7. **IBHS FORTIFIED** (ibhs.org, May 20, 2026) — program surpassed 100,000 designations nationwide; >20,000 issued in 2025; FH 2026-02 technical bulletin (fortifiedhome.org, Mar 19, 2026): FORTIFIED documentation may satisfy certain field verification requirements on the updated OIR form beginning April 2026; Alabama DOI Hurricane Sally study (aldoi.gov): FORTIFIED homes outperformed homes built to equivalent supplemental codes without IBHS inspection, which outperformed code-minimum homes. 40,195 policies / 7,417 FORTIFIED houses / $17B insured value.

## Supporting data points
- **Savings scale:** MSFH reports homeowners completing qualifying improvements average >$900/yr savings (via nexgenfl.com). FL OIR 2024 Residential Wind-Loss Mitigation Study: comprehensive protection = 20-30% off total annual premium. FL Division of Emergency Management Wind Mitigation Booklet: max 88% of windstorm portion of premium. Windstorm portion typically 30-70% of total bill (nexgenfl).
- **Credits are not cumulative** (DEM booklet): when one applies, others reduce. Full opening protection + roof credits needed to approach the 88% ceiling.
- **Opening protection is all-or-nothing:** one unprotected opening drops the rating (anchoralatlasinsurance.com, wescon).
- **Form logistics:** valid up to 5 years; must be signed by licensed GC/building contractor/architect/engineer/home inspector; photos required for each feature since Feb 1, 2012 rule (wikipedia: Windstorm inspection).
- **Who does the inspection:** $75-150 typical (wescon, anchoratlas, activerain 2026 buyer guide); HomeInspections.com launched statewide wind-mit reports starting $125 (May 13, 2026, cbherald.com).
- **Statute basis:** FL Statute 627.0629(1) requires every property insurer to offer discounts when qualifying features are documented (nexgenfl).
- **Skepticism material:** former Sen. Jeff Brandes (Aug 2025): simpler to eliminate sales tax on qualifying roofs/windows/doors than run the grant bureaucracy (gray-wflx, WPTV reporting).

## Original contribution (novel analysis)
1. **The 12% conversion math:** 12,570 premium drops / 109,443 inspections = 11.5%. The state appropriated $576M and reimbursed $240M through Dec 2024, yet only about 1 in 8 inspected homes saw lower insurance. That is the paperwork gap, quantified from the program's own Senate testimony.
2. **Cost-per-drop:** $240M reimbursed / 25,530 grants ≈ $9,400 per hardened home. Credits that were supposed to be the payoff mostly didn't land.
3. **The 2026 reset timing:** the form hadn't been revised in 14 years; the revision landed the same year the program's money started flowing again — meaning thousands of homeowners are submitting harder paperwork into a system that already fails to convert.
4. **Actionable calc:** a $100-150 inspection, valid 5 years, documenting features that average >$900/yr savings among improvers — break-even in under 2 months of captured credits. The cheapest insurance move in Florida is paperwork, not plywood.

## Limitations
- MSFH premium-drop data is self-reported by grant recipients to DFS (12,570 reduced / 7,065 stable / 4,190 increased / 1,705 no info); selection and reporting bias possible.
- Credit factors vary by carrier and territory; the 88% ceiling and 20-30% figures are program maximums and study averages, not guarantees.
- Exzeo WindForm Pro is carrier-side tooling; no public data on how many inspections it processes or accuracy rates.
- Average savings figures ($900/yr) come from MSFH program reporting via a roofing company's site; OIR's 20-30% figure is from the 2024 study as cited by same.

## Counterargument (full strength)
The credits may be smaller and rarer than advertised for good reason: insurers price on modeled risk, and a 1980s block home with a new roof still sits in a surge zone. MSFH's own numbers show 4,190 recipients saw premiums INCREASE after hardening — because reinsurance costs, litigation, and assignment-of-benefits abuse drove rates up faster than any credit could offset. The form is paperwork about the house, not the market. If carriers are losing money statewide, no nail pattern saves you.
