# Research: Article #838 — The Deck Safety Law That Missed Your Deck

**Slug:** `deck-safety-law-missed-your-deck-2026`
**Journalist:** Catherine Chen (Policy & Regulation)
**Kill test:** Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. It tells single-family
homeowners that the state's new balcony-inspection regime does not cover them, gives them the
NADRA checklist the law's inspections are modeled on, names the free/low-cost AI photo-screening
tools that can flag the same failure modes, and tells buyers of 3+ unit buildings what to demand
at closing. Deck collapses injure thousands a year and 75%+ of people on a failing deck are hurt.

## The incident

- June 15, 2015: fifth-floor balcony at Library Gardens apartments, Berkeley, collapsed during a
  21st birthday party. Six people killed, seven injured. Cause: severe dry rot in the
  wood-framed cantilevered structure; waterproofing failure let moisture in for years.
  (bornstein.law; roattorneys.com reports "killing seven people" — the widely reported toll is
  six dead, seven injured; use that figure.)
- The rot was hidden inside the structure. The balcony looked normal from the outside.

## The law(s) it produced

- **SB 721** (apartments, 3+ dwelling units): owners must hire a licensed architect, structural
  or civil engineer (or qualifying licensed contractor) to inspect all "exterior elevated
  elements" (EEEs: balconies, decks, stairways, landings, walkways) 6+ feet above grade that
  rely on wood or wood-based supports. First inspection deadline: **January 1, 2026**
  (extended from Jan 1, 2025 by AB 2579). Re-inspection every **6 years**. Emergency repairs:
  permit within 120 days of report, complete work within 120 days of permit.
  (burgelmanhomes.com 2026 guide; oaklandca.gov SB 721 FAQ; bornstein.law)
- **SB 326** (condo/co-op HOAs, 3+ units; Davis-Stirling Act, Civil Code 5551): same EEE
  inspection duty. First deadline **January 1, 2025**. Re-inspection every **9 years**. Applies
  only to elements the HOA is responsible for maintaining; only wood/wood-based load-bearing
  components — not steel. (roattorneys.com)
- Both first-cycle deadlines have now passed. Non-compliant owners face consequences at sale,
  refinance, and disclosure. (burgelmanhomes.com)
- **The gap:** single-family homes and duplexes are exempt from both laws. The overwhelming
  majority of residential decks sit on single-family lots and were never inspected by anyone.

## The scale of the aging-deck problem (NADRA / CPSC)

- NADRA (2026 Deck Safety Month): roughly **30 million decks** across North America have
  outlived their practical service life; many built before bolted ledger connections, before
  corrosion-resistant hardware, before the industry understood pressure-treated lumber's
  reaction with metal fasteners. (news.dawnreporter.com)
- NADRA: **more than 40 million U.S. decks are 20+ years old** and predate current codes
  requiring bolts (not nails) to attach the ledger to the house. **Ledger separation from the
  house is behind the vast majority of deck collapses.** When a deck collapses it is fast and
  without warning; **more than 75% of people on it are injured or killed.**
  (mccraylumber.com)
- Ledger boards must be fastened with lag screws or through-bolts, not nails. Building code
  is explicit. Older decks held by a row of nails are "one heavy gathering away" from pulling
  off the wall. (news.dawnreporter.com)
- CPSC: collapses/failures of decks, porches, balconies caused **~2,900 injuries 2016-2019**
  (magazine.northeast.aaa.com). Industry sources cite ~6,000-6,500 structural-failure injuries
  per year (archadeck.com; railfx.net).
- September 2019: deck collapse on the Jersey Shore injured **22 people** at once.
  (archadeck.com)
- Average wood deck lifespan: ~10-15 years with maintenance. NADRA recommends professional
  inspection for decks 7+ years old. (archadeck.com; mccraylumber.com)
- NADRA's DIY checklist: split/decaying wood (ice-pick test — if a sharp instrument sinks more
  than 1/4 inch, you likely have decay; spongy wood that breaks without splintering is rot);
  flashing; fasteners; railings; stairs; footings. (mccraylumber.com)

## The AI inspection layer (2025-2026)

- **Spectora** (June 2026, businesswire/morningstar): home-inspection software company launched
  AI tools — photo-first report workflow (take photos, record once, confirm after AI analysis),
  an AI scheduling agent that answers calls and books jobs, and an MCP connector/public API so
  inspectors can query their own business data via Claude/ChatGPT. CEO Peter Osberg: built over
  a year with working inspectors because "a tool that gets things wrong doesn't save time, it
  creates more work."
- **Nationwide + Chrp** (Dec 2025, iireporter): AI-powered virtual home inspection for select
  policyholders at renewal. Guided smartphone photo survey; Chrp analyzes images against
  **400+ potential failure points** (corroded plumbing, outdated electrical panels, fire
  hazards). Customized report with mitigation recommendations; hazards escalate to a
  Nationwide rep. VP Helen Simonett. Supporting data: 70% of non-catastrophic homeowners
  claims originate inside the home; average fire loss $88K+, water loss $15K+.
- **Alpine Intelligence** (Colorado, March, 5280.com): Alpine Building Performance released a
  **free** ChatGPT-powered "AI inspection forecaster." Founder Andrew Sams built it with
  business partner Mason Minor after the pandemic boom left agents offering $60K over asking
  sight-unseen; agents now use it at showings to predict what a full inspection will find.
- **Hosta a.i.** (MIT, news.mit.edu): photo-based property assessment — snap phone photos, get
  floor plans, measurements, and material classifications; cuts ~80% of assessment time. Shows
  the photo-to-structure pipeline is real, though its use case is measurement, not safety.
- Skepticism points (important for the article):
  - No photo tool can see inside a ledger board or a sealed balcony cavity — the Berkeley
    balcony's dry rot was invisible from the exterior. AI photo screening catches visible
    warning signs (staining, deflection, corroded flashing, missing hardware), not hidden rot.
  - Chrp/Nationwide is an insurer tool, not a substitute for a licensed inspection; findings
    can affect your policy, not just inform you.
  - Spectora's AI serves inspectors, not homeowners — it makes the pro faster, not optional.
  - Alpine Intelligence is a forecaster ("what will the inspection find"), explicitly not the
    inspection itself.

## Actionable takeaways for the article

1. Buying a 3+ unit building in CA? Demand the SB 721 (or SB 326, for condos) inspection
   report at closing — the deadline has passed, so non-compliance is a disclosure/sale issue
   and a negotiation lever.
2. Own a single-family home with a deck 7+ years old? You are outside the law and entirely
   on your own. Run NADRA's checklist annually: ice-pick test for rot, check the ledger for
   bolts vs nails, look for flashing failures and corroded fasteners.
3. Free AI pre-screen exists: Alpine Intelligence (free forecaster) can tell you what a real
   inspection is likely to flag before you pay for one. Chrp-style photo surveys are coming
   through insurers — useful, but they feed the underwriter too.
4. If your ledger is nailed, not bolted: that is the single most common collapse mode. A
   structural engineer or licensed deck contractor can retrofit through-bolts; it is the
   cheapest life-safety fix on the house.
5. Renters: SB 721 gives you the right to a building whose balconies were inspected by
   Jan 1, 2026. Ask the landlord for the report.
