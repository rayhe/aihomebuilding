# Research: United Bans AI Agents From Booking Flights (Muse Named)

**Slug:** muse-united-bans-agent-booking-2026
**Journalist:** Frank DeLuca (productivity systems, life operations — travel booking is a life operation, and the "seen every automation promise" skepticism fits a story about an airline telling your agent no)
**Date:** November 13, 2026
**Headline (working):** United Just Told Your AI Agent It Can't Book Your Flight. Your Permission Was Never the Point.

## Kill test
Passes. A reader who hands travel to Muse needs to know the single largest US carrier by some measures just rewrote its contract to make that illegal-by-contract, with cancellation as the penalty, and that their own approval of the agent does not matter. It changes what Muse can do, how to use it for travel, and what trusting an agent with real bookings actually risks.

## What happened
On Wednesday, November 11, 2026, United Airlines revised its Contract of Carriage and announced it is restricting access for unauthorized third-party personal AI agents and assistants. United is the first US carrier to do so publicly. The ban covers its website and app and reaches indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators.

## Primary sources (verified)
1. **United Airlines, Contract of Carriage (revised November 11, 2026)** — read directly Nov 13, 2026 at united.com/en/us/fly/contract-of-carriage.html. Rule 1 now defines "AI Agent" as "any software or service, including but not limited to an AI assistant, AI agent or AI-enabled browser that takes autonomous or semi-autonomous action, whether or not Passenger or another individual supervises or approves its actions, to discover, search for, book, purchase, ticket, service or manage any product or service offered by or through United, including flights, seats, upgrades, ancillary products and partner products." It expressly covers (i) AI assistants built into social media, messaging, search or other consumer platforms; (ii) AI-enabled browsers and standalone AI agents; and (iii) any third-party AI assistant reaching United through a travel agency or other intermediary.
2. **United Airlines newsroom release, Nov 11, 2026, "United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents"** — quoted in Business Travel News and QUE.com: the carrier is "taking steps to restrict access - directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators - to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants." Stated reasons: sharing account credentials with third-party assistants can expose sensitive information; agent actions may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles. United also flagged automated check-in as a possible breach of FAA and DOT rules, because online check-in requires a passenger to personally acknowledge baggage safety information.
3. **United Digital Trust Center page on third-party AI agents** — quoted in BoardingArea/Plane N Simple and Cordoval Media: any AI agent, bot, browser extension or browser feature that searches, books, buys, manages or collects information on united.com or the United app without United's permission is unauthorized; giving an assistant permission yourself "doesn't mean United will permit an AI assistant to transact with United." Asked whether it works with any third-party AI agents, United answers "Not yet." Assistive technology for customers with disabilities is not affected. Bookings agents made before Nov 11, 2026 will not be canceled, though United recommends reviewing them.

## The operative rules (from secondary reporting of the contract text)
- **Rule 6.J.6 (new):** "The use of AI Agents to make, modify or service a Ticket, and/or to purchase any service or product" joins the prohibited-practices list that already held hidden-city and throwaway ticketing.
- **Rule 5.H (amended):** "reservations made, modified or serviced by AI Agents" are added to the improper reservations United "will cancel without notice."
- **Rule 6.K remedies** (per PPC Land): run from invalidating tickets and banning passengers to deleting miles, revoking Elite status, and ending MileagePlus membership.
- **The definition's key phrase:** "whether or not Passenger or another individual supervises or approves its actions." Supervision and approval do not cure it. The authorization that counts is United's, not yours.

## What is still allowed
- Agents may still search fares and display United flights, as long as the customer completes the purchase. Research and planning with AI, without the tool touching united.com or the United app, is fine.
- Fares remain bookable through aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights (per Thrifty Traveler, citing analyst Henry Harteveldt).
- Bookings an agent made before November 11 stand.

## The motive question
- United's stated reasons: data security, unintended purchases, FAA/DOT compliance.
- CEO Scott Kirby, at a Chicago event the day before the announcement (per Thrifty Traveler, Nov 11): "To be honest, I don't want to create another middleman." That is the honest sentence in the story, and it reframes the security rationale as, at best, half the story.
- United says it is not currently seeing many AI-agent bookings, so the practical impact today is small. The ban is preemptive: it draws the line before agent booking is common, not after.

## The pattern (original tally)
United is not the first door to close on Muse. Amazon blocked Muse from buying on Amazon.com on September 20, 2026, twelve days after Muse's launch. eBay updated its user agreement to prohibit automated access without permission; Booking.com bars AI assistants without prior permission; Target allows only an approved list; Yelp accepts only agents with paid data-licensing agreements; JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab reportedly block agent access to accounts. Cloudflare's September 15, 2026 default change began blocking mixed crawlers, which swept agents into blocks on ad-carrying pages. Counterpoint: Walmart announced an official partnership with Muse at Meta Connect in September 2026, and Meta is developing open standards with Walmart, Stripe, Sierra and others.
- US carriers now split three ways: United bans agent booking outright; Delta lets authenticated AI assistants complete bookings, with extra safeguards for sensitive transactions such as unaccompanied-minor trips; American says some agents can buy tickets through certain partners. (Sources: Thrifty Traveler, InvestingLive, Nov 11-12, 2026.)
- Same day, the other side of the Atlantic: the European Commission adopted a Digital Fairness Act proposal on November 11 that would let Brussels fine companies up to 6 percent of worldwide turnover and would require AI shopping agents to disclose when a suggestion has been paid for. (PPC Land, Nov 12, 2026.)

## Original contribution for this article
1. **The six-task permission table**, derived by reading the Rule 1 definition against the Trust Center FAQ: search fares, hold a fare, book a ticket, change a booking, check in, spend MileagePlus miles — each mapped to banned / allowed / gray, with the contract language behind each cell. Nobody has published the task-by-task map; coverage stops at "bookings banned."
2. **The authorization insight:** the contract's "whether or not" clause means the passenger's approval is legally irrelevant. Every prior agent-blocking story assumed the risk was the agent acting without you. United's rule bans the agent acting *with* you.
3. **The carrier tally:** three-way split among the big three US carriers, counted from primary and trade sources in one place.
4. **A working travel workflow for Muse users on United:** research and compare with Muse, set a fare watch with Muse, book yourself in the United app, then hand the confirmation back to Muse to track, re-check, and manage the trip. The agent loses the checkout click and keeps everything around it.

## What the article must not claim
- Do not claim United can reliably detect an agent booking that arrives through an intermediary. Multiple sources flag this as unclear; the ban is contractual, and enforcement is unproven.
- Do not claim other airlines will follow. United says it is not seeing many agent bookings; Delta and American have taken different lines.
- Do not overstate the EU angle: the Digital Fairness Act is a proposal, not law.
