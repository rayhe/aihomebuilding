# Research: AI Floor Plan Tools and the Ceiling Height Blind Spot

## Thesis
AI generative floor plan tools operate in two dimensions. They can optimize room adjacency, circulation, and square footage, but they literally cannot reason about the vertical dimension — ceiling height variation, double-height spaces, compression-release spatial sequences. This matters because decades of environmental psychology research proves that ceiling height is one of the strongest architectural variables affecting human cognition, emotion, and spatial experience.

## Primary Sources

### 1. The Cathedral Effect — Meyers-Levy & Zhu (2007)
- Published in *Journal of Consumer Research*
- Three experiments: 10-foot ceilings activated abstract/relational processing; 8-foot ceilings activated concrete/item-specific processing
- Effect is driven by priming: high ceilings → concepts of freedom; low ceilings → concepts of confinement
- No effect when ceiling height goes unnoticed
- Widely cited (1,200+ citations as of 2024)

### 2. fMRI Brain Imaging Study — Vartanian et al. (2015)
- Published in *Journal of Environmental Psychology*, Vol 41, pp 10-18
- 200 room images shown to participants in brain scanner
- Rooms with high ceilings significantly more likely to be judged "beautiful"
- High ceilings activated left precuneus and left middle frontal gyrus — visuospatial exploration regions
- Ceiling height engages spatial navigation instincts, not just mood
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jenvp.2014.11.006

### 3. Ceiling Height Preference Study — Baird, Cassidy & Kurr (1978)
- Found people prefer ceilings of ~10 feet (3.0m)
- Roughly 2 feet above the then-standard 8-foot residential ceiling
- Preference drops for ceilings both HIGHER and LOWER than 10 feet
- Establishes an optimal range, not a "higher is always better" rule

### 4. Thermal Comfort and Ceiling Height — Mofrad et al. / Guimarães et al.
- Ideal residential ceiling height: 2700-2800mm for thermal comfort
- 20cm decrease in ceiling height → 1°C temperature increase
- Ceiling height affects energy performance, not just aesthetics

### 5. AI Floor Plan Generation Research — Polish PlanFinder Study (MDPI, 2025)
- AI-generated apartment plans met 55-74% of design criteria
- 100% effective at spatial organization at macro level
- But: "the generated apartments are not a ready-made product that can be accepted uncritically"
- AI tools generate 2D floor plans — no vertical dimension in any evaluated output

### 6. Cambridge AI Housing Plan Evaluation (2024)
- ChatGPT and Copilot generated floor plans evaluated for spatial organization, functionality, circulation
- All evaluation is 2D — no mention of ceiling height variation in any AI output
- "Proportional inconsistencies" and "spatial adjacencies that compromised privacy"
- AI prioritizes "formal expression over functional rigor"

### 7. Comprehensive Metrics for AI Floor Plans (MDPI, 2025)
- Existing evaluation metrics (FID, PSNR, SSIM, GED, IoU) all operate in 2D
- "None of the existing metrics are effective in evaluating generated residential floor plans"
- No metric exists for evaluating vertical spatial quality

## The Gap
Every major AI generative floor plan tool works in 2D:
- Stable Diffusion / ControlNet: generates 2D floor plan images
- ChatGPT / Copilot: generates 2D spatial descriptions
- PlanFinder: generates 2D apartment layouts
- Graph Neural Network models: optimize 2D room adjacency
- cGAN models: generate 2D daylight distribution maps

None of these tools can:
- Vary ceiling heights between rooms
- Create double-height spaces
- Design compression-release spatial sequences
- Optimize for the "cathedral effect"
- Model vertical daylight penetration through clerestories or skylights

## The Architectural Counter
Great residential architecture uses ceiling height variation as a primary design tool:
- Entry compression (8') → great room release (12-16')
- Kitchen work zone (9') → dining intimacy (8.5')
- Hallway compression (7.5') → bedroom ceiling lift (10')
- Split-level transitions create vertical drama at modest cost

Frank Lloyd Wright's "compression and release" is perhaps architecture's most enduring spatial principle, and AI can't do it.

## Kill Test
Who does this help?
- Homeowners using AI design tools who don't realize the tool can't see the vertical dimension
- Architects competing with AI — ceiling height is their irreplaceable value-add
- Builders using AI-generated plans who need to know what's missing
