## Original contribution: the Fortified payback model
Nobody has combined the grant + insurance + resale data into one homeowner decision model. Inputs (all sourced above):
- Typical Gulf Coast re-roof: ~$15,000-20,000 (industry range; flag as estimate). Fortified marginal per Ghosh et al. 2023: +6-16% on retrofit — on an $18,000 re-roof, $1,080-$2,880 marginal.
- Grant: up to $10,000 (AL SAH, LA LFHP) — covers the marginal Fortified upgrade several times over; often covers most of a full re-roof.
- Insurance: LA auditor avg 22% total premium reduction (median savings $1,250/yr per ainvest summary of audit); AL 25-35% off wind portion for Roof tier. On a $4,800/yr LA coastal premium, 22% = $1,056/yr.
- Resale: +~7% (ACIIR, Baldwin/Mobile, all-else-equal). On a $350,000 home = $24,500.
- Evaluator: $300-500 out of pocket (not grant-covered in AL).
- Break-even: with the grant covering the marginal cost, the homeowner is cash-positive in year one on premium savings alone; without the grant, the marginal $1,080-$2,880 pays back in ~1-3 years of premium cuts at 22%.
- The catch (counterweight): 3.3% of LA participants saw premium INCREASES (auditor); designation expires after 5 years (re-designation required); grants are lottery/first-come lotteries with demand exceeding supply; the discount math is on the WIND portion, not the whole premium, in most states.

## Limitations (for the article's dedicated section)
- The 6-16% retrofit marginal is from Ghosh et al. 2023 (academic estimate via WA OIC); I could not verify current installed marginal pricing for 2026 Gulf Coast re-roofs against contractor quotes.
- The 55-74% fewer claims figure comes from an industry/contractor summary of Hurricane Sally performance, not the primary IBHS study; treat as attributed, not independently verified.
- The 20-50% modeled loss reduction is from lab + catastrophe models; the WA OIC report itself notes stakeholder skepticism of bulk modeled results.
- Premium discount percentages are carrier- and zone-dependent; LA's 22% average comes from a self-selected survey of grant participants (Legislative Auditor 2025), subject to response bias.
- Resale +7% is Baldwin/Mobile counties only; may not generalize to weaker insurance markets or inland zones.
- The AI-documentation angle is emerging practice, not established: no published data shows AI photo-QC measurably raising FORTIFIED first-pass certification rates yet.

## Strongest counterargument
The FORTIFIED program is a paperwork machine that rewards people who are good at bureaucracy, not necessarily people who need the help: Louisiana's own audit found lower-income homeowners underrepresented among grant recipients; the $300-500 evaluator fee, the three-bid process, the 90-day completion clock, and the lottery mechanics all favor organized, liquid, time-rich homeowners. And 3.3% of participants paid MORE for insurance after certifying. A skeptic can reasonably ask whether $100M in public money is buying resilience or buying discounts for people who could have afforded the roof anyway — and whether mandating insurer discounts from January 2027 (Louisiana) will compress into base rates everyone pays. The article must state this at full strength.
