## Strongest Counterargument

**HUD's $10M is a rounding error, microfactories are Katerra 2.0, and robotics won't solve housing.**

$10M total, $3M minimum award = at most 3 awards. Reframe raised $20M private — $3M is 15% of one company's raise. U.S. builds 1.5M homes/year, short 4-7M homes (Pew). Even if each $3M award produces 100 homes (Reframe's 1M unit 20-year goal is 50K/yr), 300 homes is 0.02% of annual starts. The NAHB 2025 survey shows 1% of builders use AI to operate equipment — fundamental trust and fragmentation problem ServiceTitan quantifies (50% lack trust, >50% <$250K revenue). Small operators wearing many hats cannot operate vision-guided robotic arms.

Reframe's 35%/50% claims are unaudited and based on ADU — smallest, simplest housing type — not duplex/multiplex missing middle or custom homes that dominate residential. Katerra burned $2B promising factory-built scale, Veev burned $647M — both had robotics, AI, vertical integration, and failed. Reframe's 20-year 1M unit goal is 10× more ambitious than ICON's 100-home Georgetown TX community that took years. SoCal Rebuild fire-resilient housing sounds good but noncombustible materials and airtight construction add cost, not reduce it — fire premium is $5K-$16K (prior research) — contradicting 35% savings.

BuiltWorlds 79% robotics adoption is commercial GCs with $50M+ projects, not custom residential where <20% use scheduling software (NAHB 2024) and tools are built for $50M+ portfolios. FieldAI/Boston Dynamics/Dusty are commercial tools — residential job sites are smaller, dirtier, less structured, with different safety and accuracy needs.

Permitting AI is overhyped: Honolulu employees hated HNL Build, Denver council member dissented on AI inaccuracies and zoning complexity, Seattle didn't fully adopt despite 87%/92% accuracy — 13% error on completeness means 1 in 8 applications wrong. CivCheck is fact-checker only, not decision-maker — still needs human planner to vet (Symoom). APA researcher Dulko correctly notes 70 cases include planning functions beyond permitting, and planners consider community/inclusion/equity that AI cannot replicate.

HUD funding eligible applicants are governments, not builders — classic top-down program that will fund city consultants writing reports, not microfactories building homes. The Innovation Fund $200M/yr is for local efforts to speed permitting, not robotics — conflating two separate funding streams inflates apparent support.

This counterargument is strong because it uses HUD's own scale (3 awards max), prior failures (Katerra, Veev), NAHB 1% usage, ServiceTitan trust gap, and Honolulu employee backlash to argue microfactory + permitting AI is pilot theatre, not housing supply solution.

## Methodology Transparency

- Cost savings math: 35% Reframe-reported on traditional site-built. Applied to Census Q4 2025 avg $495K new home = $173K theoretical. More conservative half-rate 17.5% = $86.6K. Applied to custom $600K = $210K / $105K half. Carrying cost: 8% construction loan on $400K, 3.6 months saved (50% of 7.2 months Census 2024 avg residential build) = $400K×8%×3.6/12 = $9,600. Temporary housing $3,500/mo ×3.6 = $12,600. These are illustrative, not guaranteed, based on company-reported reductions not third-party audited.
- Factory + permitting combined cycle: 7.2 months build (Census) + 2.5 months permit median Honolulu new (Civil Beat) = 9.7 months total. With 50% build reduction = 3.6 months, with 55% permitting reduction (73→32.5 days = 55.5% reduction) applied to 2.5 months = 1.11 months, combined = 4.71 months, reduction = (9.7-4.71)/9.7 = 51.4%. This combines two independent datasets, not single project measurement.
- Robotics adoption gap: 79% commercial (BuiltWorlds) vs 25% residential meaningful (ServiceTitan) = 54pp gap. Not apples-to-apples populations but directional for lag.
- Piloting surge: 12%→32% = 2.67× increase, 29%→79% = 2.72×.
- Trust gap: ServiceTitan nearly 50% lack trust, NAHB 1% AI to operate equipment — both point to early stage.
- Break-even: $250K robotic cell, $105K savings per home at conservative half Reframe rate on $600K custom = 2.38 homes to break even, so 3 homes. At full Reframe rate $210K savings = 1.19 homes, so 2 homes. These assume savings fully captured by builder, not passed to buyer, and no additional overhead/training/maintenance.
