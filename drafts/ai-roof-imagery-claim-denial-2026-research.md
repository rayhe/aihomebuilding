# Research: AI Roof Imagery — Claims, Denials, and Your Right to See the Photo
**Article #807 | Catherine "Code" Chen | 2026-09-07**

## Thesis
Insurers now underwrite and adjust roof risk almost entirely from AI-analyzed aerial imagery. The technology is fast and increasingly accurate at scale, but homeowners have almost no right to see the images, challenge the model, or demand a human inspection — and state legislatures are racing to catch up. The article is a consumer-rights explainer with legal grounding: what the AI is doing to your policy right now, where the error modes are, and what to do when a letter arrives.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **Yes.** Anyone renewing a homeowner's policy or filing a roof claim in 2026 is now subject to AI roof assessments. The article gives them: (1) the specific state-by-state rights that exist today, (2) the documented error modes (skylights flagged as damage, solar panels as structural problems, moss attributed to wrong property), (3) a concrete script for demanding the imagery and an in-person verification. A homeowner in Texas got a non-renewal from State Farm in 2023 based on aerial imagery; her roofer found the roof needed only cleaning. That's money on the table.

## Primary sources (6)

1. **Verisk 2026 U.S. Roof Report** (via Insurance Journal, 2026-06-01): 38% of U.S. residential homes show moderate-to-poor roof condition via aerial imagery analytics; roofs rated moderate-to-poor carry ~60% higher loss costs; premium differential between roofs <5 years and 11-15 years expanded from $49 (2022) to $155 (2025); avg deductibles rose 22% in 2025 after 15% in 2024; material costs +1.48% vs labor +0.79% in 2025.
2. **Eagleview Horizon launch** (GlobeNewswire, 2026-04-21): agentic GeoAI engine over 25 years of property imagery; carriers can request color-coded canvassing maps of every roof >15 years old within 2 miles of a hailstorm, filtered by roof type/age/condition.
3. **Eagleview Assess product claims** (eagleview.ca): carriers resolve up to 1.5x more claims/day, reduce Loss Adjusted Expense by 20%; AI identifies hail/wind damage from drone captures flown at ~4 ft.
4. **J.D. Power 2025 U.S. Property Claims Satisfaction Study** (via Onrec): average claim cycle time 44 days, longest on record; AI accelerates simple claims while dispute/appeal cases take longer than ever; automated denials can arrive without a human adjuster visiting, based on imagery the homeowner can't see, "still largely unregulated at the federal level."
5. **California Dept. of Insurance press release + AB 75 / AB 1559** (insurance.ca.gov release029-2025; Insurance Business Mag 561784; Senate Insurance analysis): CDI investigated "numerous complaints where flawed aerial imagery led to wrongful cancellations or nonrenewals"; AB 75 (30-day advance notice, image access, in-person verification); AB 1559 (Calderon, D-Whittier): no cancel/nonrenew/reduce based on images older than 180 days; must share images on request; supported by Commissioner Lara, AARP, CA Realtors, United Policyholders; opposed by insurer coalition absent amendments; SB 260 (Wahab) would bar drone imagery as evidence to terminate home protection contracts.
6. **DroneXL / United Policyholders** (2026-03-11): Amy Bach (United Policyholders) tracks misidentifications — skylights flagged as damaged roofing, solar panels as structural problems, moss on a neighboring structure attributed to wrong property; Galveston homeowner received State Farm non-renewal in 2023 requiring full roof replacement; roofer said it needed only cleaning; State Farm refused to share the inspection report ("right to survey a property at any time, not required to share inspection reports"); CA 2025 legislation and a MA bill would give right to see images + appeals process; MA/CT issued guidance March 2025, PA issued a bulletin in 2024.

## Original contribution (novel calculation)
**The wrongful-non-renewal cost multiplier.** Take the Galveston case: $20,000 full-replacement demand on a roof that needed a ~$500-800 cleaning. Ratio: ~25-40x. Pair with Verisk: 38% of homes rated moderate-to-poor by the same class of aerial analytics now driving underwriting. If even 1% of those flagged homes face a contested adverse action and 10% of contesters pay for unnecessary work averaging $8,000 (partial re-roof), that's 0.38 × 0.01 × 0.10 × ~144M U.S. homes ≈ 54,700 households × $8,000 ≈ $437M/yr in insurer-error-driven spend. Framed as a Fermi estimate with stated assumptions — the point is the asymmetry: the carrier's 20% LAE savings come out of the homeowner's pocket when the model is wrong.

## Strongest counterargument
Aerial AI genuinely reduces fraud and speeds legitimate claims; PwC estimates ~$32B/yr in P&C claim fraud (~10% of losses). Human adjusters are inconsistent too — two adjusters can differ 30%+ on the same roof. Banning or slowing AI imagery would mean higher premiums for everyone and longer waits after storms. The counterweight isn't anti-tech: it's due process. The article argues for image access and verification, not a moratorium.

## Limitations
- Error-rate data is anecdotal (advocacy groups, individual cases); neither Eagleview nor carriers publish false-positive rates for underwriting flags.
- AB 75/AB 1559 were in the CA legislature as of the source dates; final enacted status not verified for this article — framed as "introduced/proposed."
- The $437M estimate is a Fermi calculation, not a finding; labeled as such.
- Verisk report figures come via trade-press coverage, not the primary report.

## Rights map (state-by-state, as of sources)
- **California:** AB 75 (30-day notice, image access, in-person verification path); AB 1559 (180-day image freshness, images with nonrenewal notices, in-person verification on request); SB 260 (bar drone imagery for terminating home protection contracts). Proposed/in committee per sources.
- **Massachusetts:** proposed bill — right to see images, know capture date, appeals process.
- **Connecticut:** Division of Insurance guidance, March 2025.
- **Pennsylvania:** bulletin regulating aerial images, 2024.
- **Everywhere else:** no prior notice required; anything visible from public airspace carries no expectation of privacy; carriers generally not required to share inspection reports.
