Fannie Mae Will Count Your Backyard Cottage as Income. The Cap Is 30%.

By Catherine Chen September 12, 2026

On October 8, 2025, Fannie Mae published a document with the unglamorous title Selling Guide Update SEL-2025-08. Inside was a sentence that changed the arithmetic of buying a house with a backyard cottage: rental income from an accessory dwelling unit could now be counted toward mortgage qualification. Before that date, most lenders would not count a dollar of ADU rent unless the borrower already had landlord experience or was refinancing. A first-time buyer planning to rent out the granny flat to afford the mortgage was, in underwriting terms, imagining income that did not exist.

Now it exists. Sort of.

The rule, as mortgage brokers have spent the past year explaining to clients, works like this. The lender counts 75 percent of the ADU's market rent toward your qualifying income, the standard haircut for vacancy and expenses. Market rent is established by a signed lease or by the appraiser's rent schedule, Form 1007 or 1025, which keeps the number honest because a licensed appraiser pulls the comps, not you. Only one ADU's income counts, even if the property has three. And the whole thing is capped: qualifying ADU income cannot exceed 30 percent of your total qualifying income.

That cap is where the story gets interesting, because caps always have a distributional conscience, and this one's conscience is uncomfortable.

The Math, Worked Honestly

Take a buyer earning $6,000 a month in base qualifying income. The backyard cottage rents for $2,200, the Bay Area median for new ADUs in the Terner Center's statewide owner survey. The lender counts 75 percent: $1,650. Cap check: $1,650 against a new total of $7,650 is 21.6 percent, comfortably under the limit. Total qualifying income becomes $7,650. At a 36 percent debt-to-income ceiling, the monthly housing budget rises from $2,160 to $2,754, a $594 increase. At 6.75 percent on a 30-year note, roughly $6.49 of principal-and-interest per $1,000 borrowed, that $594 supports about $91,500 more in mortgage principal.

Real money. Now run the case the rule was supposedly written for.

A buyer earning $3,500 a month, same $2,200 cottage rent. Uncapped, the $1,650 in qualifying rent would be 32 percent of the $5,150 total, which breaches the 30 percent cap. So the qualifying ADU income gets solved down: it must equal 30 percent of ($3,500 plus itself), which works out to $1,500. The borrower loses $150 a month of the rent's underwriting value to the cap. The buyers who most need the income boost receive the smallest boost. Fannie Mae's stated rationale was affordability and small-scale infill; the arithmetic says the subsidy flows uphill.

None of this is hidden. It is just in a selling guide nobody reads except the people paid to read it.

What the Cottage Costs

The best rigorous cost data remains UC Berkeley's Terner Center survey from 2021: a median California ADU construction cost of $150,000, about $250 per square foot, with 71 percent of projects under $200,000 and median rent of $2,000 a month. No newer academic study exists, which a September 2026 review of city fee schedules confirmed. Everything current is contractor-published, so treat it as marketing with numbers.

With that caveat: a Los Angeles builder publishing transparent 2026 pricing puts detached ADUs at $300 to $500 per square foot all-in, garage conversions at $180 to $320. A 750-square-foot detached one-bedroom lands at $250,000 to $375,000. Soft costs, design, engineering, permits, Title 24 compliance, consume 10 to 20 percent. The line item that ambushes homeowners is site and utility work: sewer and water extensions run $5,000 to $20,000, and a panel upgrade adds $4,000 to $12,000.

Run the payback on the middle of that range. A 750-square-foot cottage at $350 per foot is $262,500 all-in. At $2,000 a month in rent, $24,000 a year, the gross yield is 9.1 percent. Net of roughly 35 percent for taxes, insurance, maintenance, and vacancy, call it $15,600 a year, which is a simple payback of about 17 years. Finance the whole thing on a 7 percent HELOC and the interest-only payment is $18,375 a year against $24,000 in rent, positive carry of roughly $5,600 before a dollar of principal is repaid. The numbers work in high-rent markets and at garage-conversion prices. They strain at the $400,000 Bay Area extreme the Terner researchers flagged.

The AI Told You Your Lot Qualifies. Maybe.

Before any of this math matters, someone has to answer the first question: can you legally build the thing on your lot. A small industry of AI zoning tools has grown up to answer it. Type in an address, get a feasibility report: setbacks, height limits, lot coverage, hazard overlays. FutureLot sells exactly this to builders, with a human review layer and a few free reports for homeowners. An architecture firm called cove has embedded its Vitras.ai zoning engine directly into feasibility studies so architects see the constraints in the first conversation instead of week six.

The honest evaluation of these tools arrived this year from the Urban Institute, which tested a retrieval-augmented generation system against Minneapolis's 467-page zoning code. Two personas: a professional developer on a multifamily project, and a single-family homeowner asking about an ADU. The finding, reported without varnish: even with customization and system prompts, the AI's answers were often not helpful. A zoning answer can be confident, well-structured, and wrong, missing a site-specific exception, an overlay district, or an amendment adopted last month. The researchers' verdict deserves quoting: zoning interpretation is not a search problem, it is a reasoning problem.

That is the correct posture toward every AI feasibility report you will ever receive. It is a first pass. The planning counter is the determination. Budget for the possibility that the report missed the thing that kills your project, because in 2026 testing, that happened often.

The Appraisal Is the Bottleneck

Here is the part the income math obscures. Fannie Mae unlocked income, not value. An appraiser valuing your house-with-cottage needs comparable sales of similar properties, and in markets where ADUs are new, those comps are thin. You can qualify on the income and still watch the deal die on appraisal, because the cottage you spent $262,500 building does not automatically add $262,500 to the appraised value. Appraisers value what comparable buyers paid, not what you spent.

Fannie Mae knows this, which is why the expanded ADU eligibility, up to three ADUs on a one-unit property, manufactured homes with ADUs, two- and three-unit properties adding ADUs up to four total units, is gated behind the new Uniform Appraisal Dataset 3.6. The redesigned appraisal format is the data plumbing meant to fix the comp problem. Its mandatory deadline is November 2, 2026, seven weeks from this article's publication date. Between now and then, lender implementation remains uneven. The policy published in October 2025; 2026 trade reporting describes adoption as "underway," which is the industry's word for incomplete.

A Case Against Building It

The strongest argument against the cottage starts with the appraisal gap and does not stop there. The buyer the rule was written for, the first-timer stretching into a house with a cottage, can qualify on income and fail on value in the same transaction. Lender overlays lag the policy by a year or more, and "underway" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The AI feasibility tools that are supposed to de-risk the front end produce confident, well-structured, sometimes wrong answers about the single most project-killing variable, the zoning. Only 8 percent of California ADUs are short-term rentals, per the Terner survey, so this is a long-term-tenant income story with all the landlord risk that implies, not an arbitrage story. The 30 percent cap and the one-ADU limit bound the upside by design. And the payback table above assumes the rent arrives every month for 17 years. Ask any landlord how often that happens.

There is genuine force in all of it. Set against it: rents are real, the housing shortage is real, and a garage conversion at $120,000 renting for $1,800 a month pays back in under six years on a gross basis. The policy did not create that math. It finally allowed the mortgage system to see it.

What This Means for Your Project

If you are buying a home with an existing ADU, ask your lender, before you fall in love with the house, whether they have implemented SEL-2025-08 in their underwriting system and whether their appraisers are ordering UAD 3.6 reports. "Fannie Mae allows it" and "our bank does it" are different sentences. Get the market rent documented on Form 1007 or 1025 early, because the 75 percent calculation runs on the appraiser's number, not yours, and a thin comp set cuts both ways.

If you are building, run an AI feasibility report on your address and then verify every material claim against your planning department before spending design money. Price the sewer lateral and the panel upgrade before you price the finishes; the utility work is where budgets die. Consider a pre-approved standard plan from your city or the state, which compresses plan check and design fees because the structural and code work is already vetted. And do the cap math on your actual income before assuming the rent qualifies you for the bigger house. The 30 percent rule is not a detail. For a $3,500-a-month buyer it is $150 a month of phantom income.

The code does not care about your feelings, but for once it noticed your cottage. Check whether your lender did too.

Limitations

The most rigorous ADU cost data is the 2021 Terner Center survey; construction costs have moved since, and the 2026 pricing figures are contractor-published or journalist-compiled, not peer-reviewed. The payback table is directional, not a quote. The underwriting math assumes a 36 percent DTI ceiling and a 6.75 percent rate; actual lender overlays, caps, and rates vary, and the $91,500 figure covers principal and interest only, while taxes and insurance on the larger purchase consume part of the budget increase. Policy mechanics are summarized from trade-press reporting and Fannie Mae's UAD 3.6 policy document; the full Selling Guide text governs, and the November 2, 2026 UAD 3.6 deadline should be confirmed against current Fannie Mae announcements. The Urban Institute RAG findings are summarized from a secondary analysis; consult the underlying study for methodology. No independent testing of commercial AI zoning tools was performed.
