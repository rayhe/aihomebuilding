# Research: Buildots Intelligence Lab — First AI-Powered Construction Benchmarks

## Topic
Buildots Intelligence Lab launched June 25, 2026 — first AI-powered construction research hub publishing freely available benchmarks from hundreds of projects, hundreds of millions of sq ft. Key findings: final 20% of activity takes 27% duration (long-tail), 20-50% MEP output gap, top teams 3x faster, schedule adherence 39-65% by sector.

Residential relevance: No residential-specific data yet — only commercial (data centers, healthcare, education, commercial/industrial). Gap for custom homebuilders to apply. Need to translate to residential carrying cost and schedule risk.

## Kill Test: Does this help someone building or buying a home?

YES.

- For homeowner hiring GC: If builder says "8 months", Buildots data says final 20% takes 27% — you should budget 10.5-11 months mentally, and put liquidated damages after 10 months not 8. 
- For custom builder ($2-5M projects): MEP gap 20-50% explains why electrical/plumbing weeks always slip; Buildots suggests tracking weekly MEP output vs plan and intervening when <80% of planned. Top teams 3x faster = hiring the right MEP sub matters more than schedule compression tricks.
- For buyer: Schedule adherence <39-65% even in commercial with full-time PMs — residential with part-time supers will be worse. Build 2-month buffer into lease overlap.
- Original calculation: 6.3-month residential timeline × 27% long-tail on final 20% = 1.7 months of "punch list hell". At 8.5% construction loan on $450K average cost-to-build, carrying cost = $450K × 8.5% × (1.7/12) = $5,418 + $2,400 extended insurance/permits = ~$7,800. Plus rent overlap at $2,500/mo = $4,250. Total $12K+ hidden cost nobody budgets. AI benchmark makes it visible.

## Counterarguments / Limitations

- No residential-specific data — Buildots dataset is commercial-heavy (data centers, healthcare). Residential extrapolation is directional not definitive.
- Opt-in bias: Only Buildots customers opt in — likely more tech-forward, better-run projects, so true industry averages may be worse than reported.
- Small builders lack head-mounted cameras — can't directly replicate Buildots tracking, must use manual weekly output counts.
- Free today — Buildots is VC-backed ($45M at $300M valuation per RESEARCH.md). Free lab could become paid tier later.
- Privacy: Aggregated anonymized but still derived from customer projects — competitive concerns for GCs sharing data.

## Skepticism

- Katerra ($2B), Veev ($647M) failed promising factory efficiency — data alone doesn't fix labor.
- Schedule adherence 39-65% is not new — CPM scheduling has always been optimistic. AI just measures the optimism.
- Top teams 3x faster — is it team skill or project selection? Buildots doesn't control for complexity.
- Residential builders run 1-3 projects, not 100s — benchmarks help less when n=small.

## Actionable Takeaways (Required for article)

- If you're a homeowner: Add 25% buffer to builder's schedule. Final 20% = 27% rule means October promise = early December reality. Budget rent overlap.
- If you're a GC under $5M: Track weekly MEP output vs plan. If <80% for 2 weeks, intervene. Don't compress final 20% — staff it heavier.
- If you're a builder choosing tech: Buildots free lab benchmarks are free — pull schedule adherence by type and compare to your own last 3 projects. Takes 30 minutes.
- ROI: Avoiding one month delay saves $5K-$12K on $450K build — more than Buildots subscription for small portfolio.
- Limitation honesty: Residential adherence likely worse than 39% education floor — plan accordingly.
