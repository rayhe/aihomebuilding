# Research: AI Permit Review — Pre-Check vs. The Real Bottleneck

## Angle
Seven+ US cities have launched AI-powered building permit review tools in 2025-2026. Every one of them does the same thing: pre-screening applications for completeness and obvious errors before a human reviewer sees them. None of them do the actual plan review. The technology that gets the press release is solving the easy 20% of the problem. The hard 80% — a qualified reviewer reading your structural calcs, checking fire/life safety, verifying code compliance — still takes weeks and still requires a human signature.

Meanwhile, cities are cutting permitting staff (Denver: -59 positions) while deploying these tools. The AI isn't replacing the bottleneck. It's making the line move faster to the same bottleneck.

## Primary Sources

### Denver (CivCheck)
- $4.6 million / 5-year contract with Clariti (CivCheck platform)
- Only 30% of all submittals approved on first round; goal is 80%
- Community Planning and Development Department cut 59 budgeted positions for 2026 (251 total)
- 180-day "shot clock" with $10,000 refund promise if missed
- Already cut single-family/duplex possession time by ~45% since 2023
- Source: PYMNTS (May 2026), Route Fifty (May 2026)
- Quotes: Robert Peek (director of development systems performance), Julia Richman (Clariti VP govt relations)

### Honolulu (CivCheck)
- Launched Dec 8, 2025; optional, phased mandatory later
- Pre-CivCheck 2023: 6-month waiting period for prescreen
- Dec 2025: 5-week backlog
- Current: 7-day wait, 174 projects in prescreen
- Residential only initially; commercial to follow mid-2026
- Source: GovTech (Jan 2026)
- Quote: Davis Pitner (DPP public info), Dheekshita Kumar (CivCheck CEO): "designed to augment plan reviewers, not replace them"

### Seattle
- Mayor Harrell executive order (June 2025)
- New "Permitting and Customer Trust (PACT)" team
- AI pilot began April 2025, public rollout expected 2026
- Claims: "could cut housing review cycles by 50% or more"
- Pre-screening focus: completeness, compliance, reducing back-and-forth
- Source: GeekWire (June 2025)

### Governor Newsom / LA Fire Recovery (Archistar)
- Free Archistar software for LA fire area rebuilding permits
- Partners: LA Rises, Steadfast LA, Autodesk, Amazon
- Uses computer vision + ML + automated rulesets to check designs against local zoning/building codes
- Pre-check tool: "pre-check their building plans before submission"
- Source: Governor's office press release (April 2025)

### Blitz AI
- Pueblo County, CO: First in Colorado (Jan 2026)
- Naples, FL: Partnership with CityView permitting system
- Trained on Florida Building Code and local ordinances
- "evaluate building plans in minutes, flag potential noncompliance"
- Patent-pending technology
- Source: Press releases, HousingWire (April 2026)

### AutoReview.AI (University of Florida)
- First US system to use AI for full building code checking (claims)
- Built on entire Florida Building Code (800+ pages)
- From UF Warrington College
- Source: UF Warrington (March 2026)

### Canada NRC Challenge
- Just opened July 7, 2026 — seeking AI solutions for compliance checking
- Deadline: August 5, 2026
- Requires TRL 5-9 (prototype to deployment)
- "Deterministic artificial intelligence-assisted compliance checking"
- Focus: both 2D drawings (PDF/CAD) and BIM models
- The fact that this is a CHALLENGE proves no existing solution does the full job
- Source: ised-isde.canada.ca

### Nature/Scientific Reports Study
- AI-driven framework for evaluating permitting processes
- Analyzed ~300 state/county/city-level documents
- Average local permitting document scored 1.8 out of 5 on clarity
- "half of the requirements developers face are ambiguous"
- Database accuracy ~95%
- Source: Nature Scientific Reports (June 2026)

### MDPI Academic Paper
- "An Agentic AI System for Roof Design Compliance" (July 2026)
- Uses computer vision + RAG + LLMs for roof assembly compliance
- Acknowledges: "no internal feedback mechanism" between review stages
- Shows even cutting-edge academic work focuses on single compliance domains, not full review

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **YES.** If you're a homeowner or builder waiting for a permit, you've heard your city "uses AI" for permits now. But your plans still sat for 4 weeks waiting for a structural reviewer. This article explains why: the AI does intake triage, not plan review. Knowing this helps you: (1) submit cleaner applications that actually benefit from the AI pre-screen, (2) understand that the real delay is downstream, (3) recognize that staff cuts are the real story behind the AI press releases.

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing all known AI permit deployments to show they ALL do the same thing (pre-check), calculating the gap between what's automated and what still requires human review, and showing the simultaneous staffing cuts that expose the real motivation.

## Counterargument
The pre-screen tools genuinely help. Denver's 30% → 80% first-try approval would be transformative. Honolulu's 6-month → 7-day prescreen improvement is real. The AI vendors are transparent about "human-in-the-loop." The problem isn't the tools — it's the press releases that imply the full bottleneck is solved.
