# Research: AI Contract Review vs. the Home Improvement Contract — The Arbitration Clause That Locks You Out of Free State Help

**Date:** September 14, 2026
**Journalist:** Catherine "Code" Chen (policy, legal, building codes)
**Slug:** `ai-contract-review-arbitration-cslb-home-improvement-2026`
**Kill test:** Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. Anyone hiring a contractor for a remodel, addition, or repair signs a home improvement contract. What is in that contract determines whether they can get paid back when the job goes sideways, and almost nobody reads it.

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
California law gives homeowners powerful contract protections, including a free state-run arbitration program for contractor disputes up to $25,000. But a single boilerplate arbitration clause buried in your contractor's contract disqualifies you from that program, and an AI contract review can flag it in minutes before you sign.

## Key facts for the article
- CA down payment cap: $1,000 or 10%, whichever is LESS. $8,000 job → max $800. $40,000 job → max $1,000.
- 3-day cancel right applies unless negotiated at contractor's place of business or price under $25. Seniors 65+: 5 days (since 2021).
- Missing the cancellation notice → the cancel right extends indefinitely until the contractor complies.
- CSLB free mandatory arbitration: damages ≤ $25,000, BUT disqualified if your contract already has a private arbitration clause. This is the trap: the clause that looks like it gives you a dispute forum actually takes away the free one.
- AAA consumer arbitration: consumer pays $225 filing + $50-$75; business pays the $300/hr arbitrator. Small claims in CA: individuals up to $10,000, filing fee $75.
- AI contract review tools exist with free tiers (Genie AI); enterprise ones (Harvey $15.5B valuation, Sep 2026) target firms, not homeowners.

## Original contribution
1. **The cross-reference nobody published:** CSLB's own Mandatory Arbitration Program Guide, condition (5) — a private arbitration clause in your signed contract disqualifies you from the state's free arbitration. AI review can flag this specific clause and name the specific consequence (loss of BPC §7085 eligibility).
2. **Worked dispute-cost math** for an $18,000 kitchen remodel gone wrong: (a) CSLB mandatory arbitration — free, cap $25,000; (b) private AAA arbitration per the contract clause — consumer $225+ filing, business bears $300/hr arbitrator, but clause may shift costs or pick a distant venue; (c) small claims — $75 filing, $10,000 cap, no lawyers needed. Shows the "standard" clause can be the most expensive path.
3. **10-point AI review checklist** derived from CSLB's sample contract: license number present, down payment within cap, 3-day/5-day notice present and in 12-point bold, start and completion dates, materials specified, payment schedule tied to phases, Notice to Owner, no payment for uncompleted work, changes-in-writing clause, arbitration clause flagged with consequence.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
Arbitration is not always the villain. For a $60,000 addition dispute, CSLB's $25,000 cap makes the state program useless, and small claims caps out at $10,000 — private arbitration or court may be the only viable forum. AAA consumer rules deliberately load costs onto the business ($300/hr arbitrator, $600 final fee), which is more consumer-friendly than it looks. And AI contract review has a documented hallucination problem: fake citations, plausible-sounding wrong holdings — ABA Formal Opinion 512 says even lawyers must verify AI output, so a homeowner treating an AI review as legal advice is taking a real risk. Finally, no contract clause, however perfect, protects you from a bad contractor the way checking the license and calling three references does.
