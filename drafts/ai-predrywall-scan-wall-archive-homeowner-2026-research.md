# Research: The Pre-Drywall Scan Archive Homeowners Never Get

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A buyer closing on a new build can demand the pre-drywall photo/scan archive at closing for near-zero cost, and that archive pays for itself the first time anyone opens a wall. Actionable, specific, money on the table.

## Thesis
Builders already capture pre-drywall 360 photos and scans for their own QC, punch lists, and liability defense. The homeowner, who paid for the house, almost never receives the archive. A $350-500 scan (or a free phone walkthrough) becomes a permanent "X-ray" of every wall: every stud, wire, pipe, and duct, mapped to the floor plan, timestamped. First remodel, first leak, first insurance claim, it pays for itself many times over.

## Primary sources

1. **OpenSpace product docs (openspace.ai)** — 360 capture maps images to floor plans automatically; "Reveal Mode" slider compares current vs. covered-up conditions; "Offline Deliverable" = zip file of all captures "to keep for a lifetime"; 25,000 sq ft captured in 10 minutes; ~75,000 projects, 50B+ sq ft documented. AI visual search finds images by keyword. Used by Gilbane, Tishman Speyer. (https://www.openspace.ai/products/capture/)

2. **OpenSpace Field GA announcement (PR Newswire via Morningstar, Feb 2026)** — T&H Investments: inspection process went from 3-4 hours to 30 minutes with every item photo-documented at exact location. Shows the capture workflow is now standard enough for mid-size residential builders. (https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260203sf76967/openspace-announces-general-availability-of-openspace-field-bringing-visual-intelligence-directly-into-field-execution)

3. **Fine Homebuilding, "Documenting projects" (Howard Furst, builder tip)** — the pre-AI version of this practice: photograph every wall before drywall, tape measure in frame for scale, leave photos with owners at job end. Establishes this is long-standing best practice among good builders, now industrialized by AI capture. (https://www.finehomebuilding.com/1987/01/01/documenting-projects)

4. **Matterport scan pricing (multiple 2026 sources)** — professional residential scan $300-800 (coohom, Apr 2026); thefuture3d: residential up to 3,000 sq ft starts at $350; hosting $20/month; smartphone/360-camera capture supported (lower accuracy). A builder self-capturing with a $300-500 360 camera pays only hosting. (https://www.thefuture3d.com/matterport/ ; https://www.coohom.com/article/understanding-matterport-costs-for-home-tours)

5. **NewSpace360 (YouTube, 2026)** — pre-drywall scan pitched explicitly as "standard, essential jobsite documentation": blueprints show what was planned, the scan shows what was installed, every pipe/wire/duct/stud. Protects margins, manages risk, kills guesswork on remodels. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XIpF0kyxxM)

6. **HomeAdvisor (2026)** — interior demolition $2-7/sq ft; typical jobs $2,000-20,000. Exploratory opening of walls to find in-wall conditions is pure waste if a photo archive exists.

7. **Realtor.com (demolition surprises)** — burst pipe from hitting an unknown water line: up to $10,000 in flood damage; full-home rewire ~$20,000 (Bungalo). Hidden live wires behind walls are among the most common demo surprises. A pre-drywall photo showing pipe/wire routing directly prevents the "kick through a wall and flood the house" failure. (https://www.realtor.com/advice/home-improvement/nasty-surprises-during-demolition/)

8. **GCI Consultants (construction litigation)** — destructive testing/investigation is the "last resort" in defect disputes: open the wall to see what was built. With a timestamped pre-drywall archive, many disputes never reach that stage.

## Original contribution (the math nobody did)
Cost of capture vs. cost of ignorance, per typical 2,400 sq ft new home:
- Capture: one pro Matterport/OpenSpace-style scan at pre-drywall = $350-500 one-time; builder self-capture with owned 360 camera = ~$0 marginal + $20/mo hosting (one month needed, then export offline deliverable).
- Avoided costs, each a single real event: exploratory wall openings during a remodel ($2-7/sq ft demo × even 50 sq ft of exploratory opening = $100-350 + patch/repaint $400-800); hitting an unknown pipe ($10,000 flood per realtor.com); mislocated blocking forcing cabinet rework; insurance claim disputes resolved by timestamped photos instead of destructive investigation ($1,500-5,000 for forensic opening per GCI-style investigations).
- Break-even: the archive pays for itself if it prevents ONE exploratory opening in the home's life. Expected remodel count over 30-year ownership: 2-4 (kitchens/baths). Probability at least one wall gets opened blind: high.
- Novel framing: the "wall archive" as a closing deliverable, alongside keys and manuals. Cost to buyer at negotiation: $0 (builder already captured it for their own QC). This reframes it from tech purchase to paperwork ask.

## Counterargument (full strength)
Photos don't show what's wrong, only what's there: a scan won't tell you the plumber used the wrong nail plate or the electrician over-bored a stud beyond code. It creates a false sense of certainty. Archives rot: Matterport links die when subscriptions lapse, file formats change, and a zip of 4,000 panos on a USB stick in a drawer is useless if nobody can open it in 15 years. Privacy: a full interior scan is also a perfect burglary scouting package if it leaks. And builders have a real reason to withhold: the archive is also evidence, discoverable in a defect lawsuit against them.

## Limitations
- No survey data on what share of residential builders capture pre-drywall imagery (OpenSpace's 75,000 projects skew commercial). The "builders already capture it" claim is strongest for production builders with QC programs; custom one-off builders vary.
- Scan positional accuracy from phone/360 capture (±inches) is fine for "which bay is the pipe in" but not for measurements; don't oversell precision.
- Long-term format readability (15+ years) is asserted, not proven.
- No verified case of a homeowner winning an insurance claim specifically on a pre-drywall scan; the litigation value is inferred from how destructive investigation works.

## Kill-test answers for the article
- Should I use this? If buying new construction: demand the archive at closing, in writing, in the contract. If remodeling: $350 scan of open walls before insulation/drywall.
- Cost: $0 (ask builder) to $500 (hire it yourself).
- Gotchas: get the offline files, not just a link; confirm coverage of every room; subscription-hosted links die.
- ROI: one avoided blind wall opening.
