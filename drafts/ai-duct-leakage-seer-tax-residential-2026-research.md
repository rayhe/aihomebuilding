# Research: AI Duct Leakage — The SEER Thief

**Article #911 | Journalist: Jake "Jackhammer" Kowalski (construction tech)**
**Date: September 19, 2026**

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A homeowner with hot/cold rooms and high bills gets: (1) a $300-500 duct blaster test before any sealing decision, (2) real cost and savings numbers for Aeroseal vs. manual sealing, (3) the new-construction code test that applies to builders, (4) the post-2025 tax-credit reality. A builder learns the IECC R403.3 testing mandate and the test-first workflow.

## The core fact
Duct leakage is one of the largest, least-inspected sources of residential energy waste:
- **ENERGY STAR:** a typical house loses 20-30% of air moving through its duct system to leaks, holes, and poor connections.
- **DOE:** "Most duct systems lose 25-40 percent of the energy." (cited in ACHR News)
- **EPA:** "as much as 30 percent of energy is lost due to duct leakage." (cited in ACHR News)
- LBNL's Max Sherman: "It does not make sense to hook up a high-efficient heating and cooling system to a leaky, inefficient duct system."
- 2026 EIN/MaxSeal release (San Jose): 25-40% air loss in older Silicon Valley stock; Federal agencies including DOE and ENERGY STAR identify duct leakage as one of the largest residential energy wastes; duct systems are among the least inspected home components.
- Equipment-downgrade claim: a 13 SEER unit connected to leaky ducts performs like a 7-9 SEER system (EPA research cited by HVAC trade sources). The homeowner paid for efficiency they never receive.

## The fix: Aeroseal / internal aerosol sealing
- Process: polymer sealant mist injected into ducts, computer-controlled; seals leaks up to ~15mm gaps from the inside. Big disconnected sections must be hand-repaired first.
- Cost: $1,300-$3,000 for a typical single-family home ($1.00-1.50/sq ft); complex/large systems up to $4,000-6,000. Includes pre/post leakage test, report, cleanup. 3-6 hours.
- Performance: seals up to 95%+ of duct leakage; providers claim 15-20% reduction in heating/cooling energy bills; duct tightness improvement up to 90%.
- Traditional manual sealing (mastic/foil tape): $500-$1,500, reaches ~70% of leaks, can't reach hidden runs inside walls.
- Caveats: sealant needs clean ducts first (debris gets blown into living space); won't fix structural disconnections; ducts inside the thermal envelope leak mostly to themselves (less of a problem than attic/crawlspace ducts).

## The code angle (new vs. existing)
- **IECC R403.3.3 (mandatory for new construction):** ducts must be pressure-tested at 25 Pa via rough-in or postconstruction test. R403.3.4 (prescriptive): leakage ≤ 4 CFM25 per 100 sq ft conditioned floor area (3 CFM25/100SF if air handler not installed at rough-in).
- Exception: ducts entirely inside the thermal envelope don't need testing — the code implicitly admits the penalty is ducts outside the envelope (attics, crawlspaces, garages).
- **The gap:** this only covers new construction. The 140M+ existing US homes are never tested. Most homeowner duct systems have never had a duct blaster test, ever.
- Duct blaster test for an existing home: typically $300-$500 — the cheapest diagnostic in home performance, and the one most homeowners skip.

## The tax-credit twist (2026 reality)
- IRS FAQ on Public Law 119-21 (One Big Beautiful Bill, signed July 4, 2025): the **25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit is not allowed for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025.** Duct sealing previously qualified for 30% under 25C.
- Article queued for 2027-02-21: article must state the 25C window is CLOSED, not market it. State/utility rebates (HOMES/HEAR, utility programs) remain; don't overclaim federal credits.

## The AI angle
- AI-assisted diagnostic: Diamond Air Design (Pensacola, May 2026) pairs AI-assisted diagnostic tools with technician assessment to identify duct leakage, overcooling, equipment strain — evaluates humidity, particulates, airflow, thermal consistency against ASHRAE 62.2 benchmarks.
- Robotic duct inspection: Multibot (TEINNOVA) camera robot — Full HD inspection, cleaning, coating inside ducts; found a fully uncoupled duct section pre-Aeroseal job (Aeroseal only seals gaps ≤15mm, so the inspection caught what the sealant would have missed).
- FLIR thermal cameras: scan for spot leaks at joints, insulation loss, disconnected sections — fast triage before a duct blaster.
- Honest limit: no AI replaces the duct blaster test. The pressure test is the measurement; cameras and algorithms are the targeting.

## Original contribution
1. **The SEER-tax math:** take a $12,000-$18,000 16 SEER2 install; at 25% duct loss the effective rating drops toward ~11-12 (proportional derate illustration — labeled as back-of-envelope), meaning roughly a quarter of the equipment premium paid for efficiency is vented into the attic. The test that quantifies it ($300-500) costs less than the upsell on a single SEER tier.
2. **The enforcement-gap ratio:** new construction gets a mandatory 25 Pa pressure test; existing homes get nothing. One side of the market has a written report signed to the building official (IECC R403.3.3); the other side has ~95% of systems never tested. The same leak is illegal in new construction and invisible in resale.
3. **Test-first decision tree (original synthesis):** duct blaster → if ≤10% leakage, leave it; 10-20%, manual seal the accessible runs; >20% or attic ducts, Aeroseal; disconnected/unclean, repair+clean first. Plus: the dirty-secret — sealant won't stick to dust-caked ducts, so a $1,999 "seal it today" quote without a cleaning plan is a red flag.

## Strongest counterargument
Duct sealing is not always the highest-ROI move. Ducts inside the conditioned envelope leaking 15% waste far less energy than attic ducts leaking 10%. Some 15-20% savings claims are provider-sourced, not third-party audited. And if the system is oversized or the ducts undersized (design problem, not leak problem), sealing improves pressure but not capacity. An honest test-first approach sometimes concludes: your ducts are fine; your problem is equipment or envelope.

## Limitations
- Aeroseal savings claims (15-20%) come from providers/vendors, not independent randomized trials; no public third-party audit of fleet-wide performance exists.
- EPA/DOE 20-40% loss figures are national averages across housing stock, mostly older; a 2020-built home with R403.3-tested ducts will be far lower.
- Average household heating+cooling spend varies 3-4x by climate; the article should use percentage savings, not absolute-dollar payback, unless climate is pinned down.
- The 13 SEER → 7-9 SEER downgrade figure comes from trade publication citing EPA research; the underlying study isn't directly verified.
