# Research: AI Data Center Construction Draining Residential Electrician Workforce

## Angle
The $600-700 billion AI data center investment wave is directly competing with residential housing for skilled electricians. Data centers pay double what homebuilders pay, homes take months longer to build, and the electrician pipeline can't backfill fast enough. The irony: the AI that promises to make everything cheaper is making housing more expensive by consuming the labor homes need.

## Key Sources

### 1. Texas Tribune/Governing.com — Abilene Case Study (April 2026)
- Gene Lantrip, 69, Abilene TX builder: homes now take 2 months longer to build
- Building 86 homes this year (vs usual 60-70), but can't keep subs staffed
- "My electrician lost two of his lead men and several of his helpers to the data center"
- Data center wages double residential: $35/hr+ vs $15-20/hr
- Scotty Wristen, WE Electric owner: lost 5 workers to data centers
- Can only afford $20/hr vs $35/hr at data centers (+ overtime, per diem)

- Texas has ~71,000 electricians employed (federal labor data)

## Original Contribution
Calculate the "data center premium" per home: If an electrician costs $35/hr at a data center vs $20/hr at residential, and a typical new home requires ~400 electrician-hours, the premium to retain an electrician on a home is ~$6,000 per house. With 86 homes under construction, that's $516K/year just for one Abilene builder. Nationally, at 1.4M housing starts and some fraction affected by data center competition...

Also: the timeline cost. If homes take 2 extra months, and the builder's carrying cost (land, insurance, interest) averages ~$1,500/month on a $350K house, that's $3,000 per home in extra carrying costs. For Lantrip's 86 homes: $258K in delays alone.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES. If you're building in a data center corridor (Texas, Virginia, Arizona, Georgia), your home will take longer and cost more. You need to know why your electrician can't show up. If you're buying, the 10%+ home price increases in these areas are partly data-center-driven. And if you're a builder, you need to decide whether to raise wages, train your own, or accept longer timelines.
