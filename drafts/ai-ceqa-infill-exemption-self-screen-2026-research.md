# Research: AI + California's CEQA Infill Exemption (AB 130 / SB 131)
**Article #886 | Journalist: Catherine "Code" Chen | Date: 2026-09-17**

## Angle
California rewrote its most-abused environmental law 14 months ago (signed June 30, 2026), and most small builders still don't know their infill project may be fully exempt from CEQA. Meanwhile AI is being deployed on BOTH sides: consultants use AI to accelerate the remaining environmental review, and opponents used AI to flood agencies with 20,000+ fake public comments. Original contribution: a builder-facing self-screen checklist for the AB 130 exemption (PRC § 21080.66) + "marginal criterion" analysis of SB 131's near-miss provision.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — a California builder or infill developer can self-screen exemption eligibility before spending on CEQA counsel; buyers learn why stalled infill projects may restart.

### 3. ABAG — "High-Level Summary of Key Provisions in AB 130 (2025) and SB 131 (2025)" (updated 2026-04-21)
- AB 130 exemption codified at Public Resources Code § 21080.66: COMPLETE CEQA exemption for all aspects of a qualifying "housing development project," including permits and public improvements.
- Applies in all incorporated cities (incl. charter cities) and Census-defined unincorporated "urban areas."
- "Housing development project" = Gov Code § 65589.5(h)(2): residential units incl. single-family; mixed-use with ≥2/3 residential sq ft (or 50% if other conditions met); transitional/supportive housing; farmworker housing.
- Urban infill site test (any one): (1) previously developed with urban use; (2) ≥75% of site perimeter adjoins urban-developed parcels; (3) ≥75% of area within 1/4-mile radius developed with urban uses; (4) four-sided site: ≥3 of 4 sides developed urban + ≥2/3 of perimeter adjoins urban parcels.
- Broader eligibility than Class 32 categorical infill exemption; fewer barriers.
- URL: https://abag.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/AB-130-SB-131-2025-Summary-Updated-04212026.pdf

### 4. Santa Barbara County counsel briefing via citizenportal.ai (Jan 8, 2026)

- SB 131 "near-miss" pathway for projects failing one narrow exemption criterion; new categorical exemptions (parks, wildfire mitigation, broadband ROW, water/sewer).
- SB 131 narrows administrative-record requirements for CEQA litigation.

### 6. Lexology — "Prioritizing Infill Housing Over CEQA" (AB 130/SB 131 detail)

## Original contributions (article must include)
1. **§ 21080.66 self-screen checklist**: first builder-facing pass/fail screening synthesized from ABAG + H&K + county-counsel sources (project type → location → infill test → 20-acre cap → half-Mullin-density floor → zoning/objective-standards consistency → sensitive/hazardous ground exclusion).
2. **Marginal-criterion analysis**: SB 131's near-miss provision makes the SINGLE cheapest exemption criterion the highest-leverage design variable. Worked example (Santa Barbara County, 30 du/ac Mullin → 15 du/ac floor): a project at 14 du/acre fails full exemption but qualifies for streamlined single-condition review; adding ~1 unit/acre (or trimming site area) flips it to full exemption. Nobody has published this design-arbitrage framing.
3. **Litigation-vector math**: Hernandez 2020 (47,999 units targeted) × "most frequent target = housing approved in existing communities" → the infill exemption plausibly covers the majority of the historical anti-housing litigation vector; 4-5 year litigation timeline × lender flight = the economic case, quantified with stated assumptions.

## Strongest counterargument
Exemptions don't end lawsuits; they move them. Opponents can still challenge the exemption DETERMINATION itself (was the infill finding supported by substantial evidence? was the Phase I adequate?). The near-miss streamlined review is untested in appellate courts. And AI cuts both ways: the same models that screen your eligibility can mass-produce comment letters and "fair argument" challenges. SB 1159 (AI "persons" clarification) is only introduced, not law.
