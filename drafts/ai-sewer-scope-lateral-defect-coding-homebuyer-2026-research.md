Key facts: 800,000 miles of wastewater sewers + 500,000 miles of lateral sewers in the US; operators apply 200+ coding variations; subjective operator interpretation influences coding; "presently there are no AI systems certified by NASSCO for recognizing every PACP/LACP/MACP observation"; accepted workflow is AI as first pass + certified operator QC. URL: https://nassco.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/NASSCO_PACP_AI_PositionPaper.pdf

Trained on 135M+ feet of inspected pipe. 2,000+ cities and national contractor network. Secured strategic investment from JMI Equity (announced June 2026). In City of Phoenix pilot (2022-2023), evaluated 5 AI vendors on 30 CCTV videos / 1.7 miles; SewerAI won highest accuracy + lowest net cost per linear foot; program expanded to 107 miles. 3. **City of Minneapolis — Private Sewer Lateral Repairs & Replacement guide (updated Jan 2026).** Homeowner owns the lateral all the way to the city main, INCLUDING the wye connection under the street. Average replacement cost: $13,000. Range: $2,150–$62,000 (based on recent projects). Healthy line can last 100 years. Recommends: inspect before buying. URL: https://www.minneapolismn.gov/media/-www-content-assets/documents/Private-sewer-lateral-repairs-and-replacement.pdf

4. **Palo Alto Online (Aug 2023) — why homebuyers should get a sewer inspection.** Palo Alto, Menlo Park, Woodside, Mountain View do NOT require lateral inspections. Defects: roots crack pipes, clogs, shifting, corrosion. Materials by era: pre-1960s clay (brittle, cracks at joints), 1970s cast iron, 1980s+ plastic (best/longest-lasting). Replacement typically $3,000–$6,000, easily $10,000+ depending on soil, length, depth. URL: https://www.paloaltoonline.com/real-estate/2023/08/02/why-homebuyers-should-get-a-sewer-inspection/

5. **ValueGuard Inspections (Philadelphia, updated ~Aug 2026).** Sewer scope is explicitly NOT part of a general home inspection (non-invasive visual exam of accessible systems; buried lateral is outside scope, same bucket as radon/mold/termites). The scope checklist: cleanout presence, pipe material (PVC / cast iron / clay / Orangeburg), offset joints, standing water (belly), and defect distances (a defect at 8 ft vs 46 ft = very different excavation). URL: https://valueguardinspections.com/home-inspections-101/dont-get-flushed-why-a-sewer-line-inspection-is-critical-for-philadelphia-homebuyers/

## Skepticism to include
- NASSCO: NO AI system is certified to recognize every PACP observation. AI is a first pass; a certified human still signs off. Any vendor claiming full automation is ahead of the standard.
- Residential scopes are simpler than municipal mains but the coding discipline is the same; a sloppy camera operator (too fast, bad lighting) defeats any AI.
- Not every defect means dig: roots can be cut ($250), a belly can be monitored, only collapse/offset at depth means excavation. AI flags; judgment decides.
- Material matters more than age for risk: Orangeburg (bituminized fiber, 1940s-70s) is the real horror pipe, deforms under pressure.

## Actionables (required)
- Buying: order a sewer scope as a contingency add-on ($250–$400), alongside radon/termites; demand footage with distances and material callouts, not just "pipe looks OK."
- Owning older home: scope once to baseline; clay pre-1960 = annual root treatment consideration; cast iron = descale inspection every few years.
- For builders: pre-pour and pre-backfill lateral camera runs catch crushed lines before concrete hides them (the NoCo plumber case: brand-new PVC crushed by equipment).
