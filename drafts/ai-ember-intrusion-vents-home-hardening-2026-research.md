# Research: AI Reads the Holes in Your House Before Embers Find Them
**Article #1050** | Journalist: Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design) | Slug: `ai-ember-intrusion-vents-home-hardening-2026`

## Angle
Architecture is the art of the opening: every door, window, and vent is a negotiation between inside and outside. Wildfire redesigned that negotiation. Up to 90% of destroyed homes were first ignited by embers, not the fire front, and the ember's preferred doorway is the vent: a 2-inch hole the architect drew as ventilation and the fire reads as an invitation. New AI computer-vision tools (WyldSafe, launched Dec 2025) now scan a facade from a phone video and flag exactly these openings, while California's new code (2025 WUI Code, effective Jan 1, 2026) and the Safe Homes grant program (AB 888, effective Jan 2026) finally price the fix. Original contribution: a cost-per-opening hardening ladder, computed from real vent/gutter/fence prices, showing the cheapest intervention per ignition-pathway is the one architects and homeowners have been ignoring.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes, directly. For anyone building or retrofitting in California WUI zones (and increasingly Colorado, Oregon, AZ/NM/UT/TX): which envelope openings matter most, what fixing each costs, what the new code requires, and what the state will now pay for. A homeowner with a video phone and $1,000 can close the single most exploited ignition pathway in their house.

## Primary sources (6)
1. **WyldSafe launch** (EIN Presswire, Jan 6, 2026): free AI app by Thousand Oaks founder Nate Siggard; patent-pending computer vision analyzes a video walkthrough frame-by-frame; flags combustible vegetation, vulnerable roof materials, unscreened vents, wood fencing; delivers risk ratings, priorities, cost estimates. Launched Dec 2025 timed to California Safe Homes grants. Siggard's own ADU was engulfed in ~10 minutes after an ember landed on a small door during the 2018 Woolsey Fire. Claims unverified by third party.
2. **California Safe Homes grant program (AB 888, Calderon)**: signed into law, effective January 2026; administered by CA Dept. of Insurance; ~$3M seed funding secured. Prioritizes replacement roofs and the 5-foot noncombustible Zone Zero. Eligibility: admitted insurer or FAIR Plan policy, ZIP overlapping high/very-high fire hazard severity zone, income at/below county low-income limit (e.g., family of four up to $159,550/yr in Santa Clara County qualifies). Application portal targeted March 2026; grant amounts TBD.
3. **IBHS ember research** (March 2019, Richburg SC test chamber): duplex ember-cast demonstration; as many as 90% of damaged/destroyed homes were first ignited by embers or ember-set fires, not the main fire front. Embers travel miles ahead of the front; enter through vents or broken windows; land on dead vegetation/dry wood/combustibles. Headwaters Economics + IBHS study: wildfire-resistant materials/design features add negligible cost vs. typical new construction.
4. **California 2025 WUI Code** (effective Jan 1, 2026; consolidates/replaces CBC Chapter 7A): prohibits cedar shakes and wood shingles in WUI/very-high zones regardless of treatment; formalizes Zone 0 (0-5 ft ember-resistant perimeter); ignition-resistant standards for roofing, siding, vents. Colorado adopted its first statewide Wildfire Resiliency Code in 2025 (enforcement July 2026) with matching Class A roof mandates.
5. **Ember-resistant vent guide** (US Made Supply, 2026): six vent categories; standard 1/4" mesh = no ember protection; 1/8" mesh = minimal; baffle-style (Brandguard) $25-50/vent, multi-layer mesh (Vulcan) $20-45/vent, intumescent $40-80/vent, all ASTM E2886-tested. Retail spot checks: Vulcan foundation vent $38.99, Brandguard eave vent $92.82. San Rafael Fire runs a free retrofit program: contractor bills the city directly for screening over existing vents in High Fire Severity Zones.
6. **Gutter/fence ignition pathways** (IBHS + CalFire + NFPA, via Firestorm Building Products Dec 2025): gutters packed with dry leaves/pine needles are among the most frequent ignition points; vinyl gutters melt and collapse, dumping burning debris on the exterior; metal gutters and metal gutter guards are the fix. Wood fencing attached to the house is a documented fuse (cited in WyldSafe's vulnerability list).

## Original calculation: the hardening ladder (per typical 2,000 sq ft home)
Assumptions: 16 vents (soffit + gable + foundation), DIY-or-contractor retrofit, 2026 retail prices.
- Vents: 16 x $35 mid-range baffle = $560 materials + ~$500 labor = **~$1,060** (one-time). Pathway blocked: ember entry to attic/crawlspace, the mechanism behind up to 90% of home ignitions.
- Gutter guards (metal): ~150 linear ft x $6-10/ft installed = **~$900-1,500**. Pathway blocked: roofline ignition from debris accumulation.
- Zone Zero clearing: labor + disposal, **~$500-1,500** DIY-contracted. Pathway blocked: ground-level ember catch.
- Wood fence separation (5 ft metal replacement where fence meets house): **~$800-2,000**. Pathway blocked: the fuse into the structure.
- Class A roof replacement: **$15,000-25,000**. Largest surface, biggest cost.
Conclusion: vents are the cheapest intervention per ignition-pathway by an order of magnitude, yet the 2025 code's vent provisions and the Safe Homes priority list both lead with roofs and Zone Zero. The article argues the ladder should start at the smallest hole.

## New-construction note
IBHS/Headwaters: specifying wildfire-resistant materials at plan stage adds negligible cost vs. typical construction. Retrofitting vents costs ~$1,000; specifying them costs the $10-25/vent delta over standard mesh. The design decision is nearly free; the retrofit is cheap; ignoring both is expensive.

## Strongest counterargument
Hardening is a probabilistic spend against a rare event, and the math is individual while the risk is collective: a hardened house in an unhardened neighborhood can still burn in a conflagration (IBHS's own duplex demo only saved the treated side). Insurers may still non-renew after you spend the money; Safe Homes grants are $3M against 2.1M at-risk homes, a rounding error; WyldSafe's accuracy claims are startup marketing with no published validation; and no camera sees behind a vent screen, so the most dangerous ember landing spots (attic fuel, insulation) remain invisible to the very AI selling the assessment. The honest hierarchy: maintenance (free) > vents ($1k) > everything else, and none of it substitutes for community-scale mitigation.

## Limitations
- WyldSafe: no independent accuracy data; "patent-pending" CV claim unverified; cannot see attic interiors, behind-screen conditions, or roof fastener details.
- Vent cost math: labor varies by market; assumes 16 vents (verify against your own home); intumescent vent long-term durability data is thin.
- 90% ember-ignition figure: IBHS 2019 finding, widely cited, not a per-pathway breakdown; no published data splits vents vs. gutters vs. roof.
- Safe Homes grant amounts and portal status: TBD as of early 2026 reporting; eligibility verified from AB 888 analyses.
- Retrofit cost figures are California-Bay-Area-anchored; other markets differ.
