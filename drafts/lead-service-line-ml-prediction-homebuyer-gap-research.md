# Research: Lead Service Line ML Prediction — Homebuyer Gap

## Core Angle
Water utilities across the U.S. are using machine learning models to predict which homes have lead service lines, achieving 80%+ accuracy. The EPA endorses this approach. But when you buy a home, nobody tells you whether the algorithm flagged your address. The prediction exists. It's just not in your disclosure packet.

## Key Data Points

### Scale of the Problem
- EPA estimated up to 9 million homes with lead service lines (2024 DWINSA survey)
- EPA revised estimate down to 4 million in Nov 2025 press release (NRDC disputes this, calls it unreliable)
- Previous estimates ranged from 6.1 million (water utility trade association, 2016) to 10 million (EPA, 1991) to 9-12 million (NRDC, 2021/updated)
- ~67,000 public water systems affected by LCRI requirements
- Sources: NRDC analysis, EPA press releases, DWINSA survey

### ML Prediction Tools
- **BlueConduit** (Ann Arbor, MI): Founded 2019. Covered 5+ million service lines in 300+ communities. 80-90%+ recall and 80-90%+ precision. Co-founded by Jacob Abernethy and Eric Schwartz (U Michigan).
- **VODA.ai LeadZero**: Rock Hill, SC case study — 30,000+ laterals would cost ~$7.5M to individually verify (~$250/visit). ML modeling dramatically reduced that.
- **Esri ArcGIS**: Lead Service Line Inventory solution — analytics, mapping, mobile apps
- **Three key predictors** (per Atlantic/FastCompany profiles): age of home, value of home, location/neighborhood
- Sources: Rockefeller Foundation press release, BlueConduit case studies, Ferguson Waterworks case study

### Flint, Michigan — Proof of Concept
- 2016-2017: BlueConduit ML model achieved ~80% hit rate finding lead pipes
- 2018: City abandoned ML, used traditional methods → hit rate dropped to 15% (10,531 digs, only 1,567 lead found)
- 2019: Court order forced return to ML model
- July 2025: All lead service lines covered by settlement agreement replaced
- Source: MLive reporting, BlueConduit case studies, Michigan EGLE bulletin (Feb 2026)

### EPA Endorsement of ML
- EPA Guidance for Developing and Maintaining a Service Line Inventory, Section 5.5: Predictive Modeling
- Cites Abernethy et al 2018 (BlueConduit founders' research)
- Multiple states issuing guidance on statistical/predictive modeling (Massachusetts, Maryland, New Jersey)
- Source: BlueConduit blog, MassDEP guidance, NJ DEP rationale document

### LCRI Regulatory Context
- Finalized October 2024 under Biden
- Requires 10-year full replacement of all lead and GRR service lines
- Baseline inventory due November 1, 2027
- ~99% of systems must complete replacement within 10 years
- $15B in IIJA funding for lead service line replacement
- Trump administration upheld LCRI but issued guidance critics say provides "flexibilities" that could delay replacements (E&E News, May 2026)
- Water utilities challenged LCRI in court; NRDC defending; oral argument expected Fall 2026
- Sources: EPA fact sheets, NRDC court case page, E&E News

### The Homebuyer Gap
- Home inspectors do NOT typically assess service line material (underground, outside inspection scope)
- Real estate disclosures don't require lead service line disclosure in most states
- Water utility inventories exist (required since Oct 2024) but homebuyers don't know to check
- BlueConduit launched LeadOut Map (leadoutmap.org) with Rockefeller Foundation + Google.org — free, public tool
- But: few homebuyers or real estate agents are aware of it
- When a utility classifies a line as "unknown" (~millions of lines), the buyer bears the risk
- Replacement cost: typically $4,700+ per line (varies widely by region)
- Individual verification: ~$250/visit just to identify material

### Health Stakes
- "No safe level of lead exposure" — EPA, CDC, AAP
- Water can constitute 20%+ of total lead exposure (EPA estimate)
- Children most vulnerable — absorb more lead per pound than adults
- Neurotoxin affecting brain development, nervous system

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? **YES.**
- If you're buying a pre-1986 home, you should check the lead service line inventory
- You can use BlueConduit's LeadOut Map to estimate risk at your address
- You should ask the seller about the service line material
- You can request a $250 inspection before closing
- If the line is lead, you may be eligible for free replacement under LCRI programs

## Strongest Counterargument
The inventory data is getting better fast. The LCRI requires all service lines to be identified within 10 years. As utilities complete inventories, the prediction-vs-disclosure gap will narrow. And some argue that scaring homebuyers about lead pipes could depress property values in older neighborhoods — which are disproportionately communities of color and low-income — causing economic harm to the very communities environmental justice advocates aim to protect.
