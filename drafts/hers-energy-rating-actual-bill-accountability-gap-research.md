# Research Notes: HERS Energy Rating vs. Actual Bill Accountability Gap

## Slug: hers-energy-rating-actual-bill-accountability-gap
## Journalist: Priya Greenwood (sustainability & green building)
## Date: 2026-07-24

---

## Core Thesis

Home energy rating systems (HERS Index, DOE Home Energy Score) produce a number that consumers treat as a bill prediction. It's not. The rating compares your home to a 2006-code reference house — a benchmark nearly two decades old. Nobody verifies whether the promised energy performance materializes after occupancy. Commercial buildings have post-occupancy evaluation and commissioning. Residential homes get a certificate and nothing else. AI-powered monitoring tools exist (Emporia Vue, Sense, Span) but none systematically reconcile actual performance against the rating. The feedback loop is broken.

## Primary Sources (3+)

### 1. Mahdavi et al. (literature review of 144 energy performance gap studies)
- Mean energy performance gap of ~30% in residential buildings
- Range from underestimation by 25% to overestimation by 96%
- "Significant variability" is the norm
- Source: Referenced in MDPI Sustainability 16(17), 7792 (2024)

### 2. Green Builder Media — "Beyond Energy Ratings Basics"
- HERS baseline is 2006 IECC. A modern code-built home scores ~70 automatically.
- Average HERS score nationwide is 61 (RESNET data)
- "HERS Index-reported energy savings have become highly overstated for new homes"
- RESNET certificate cites savings "relative to an average U.S. home" — ambiguous (new? existing? all?)
- "The only assumption I can make is that homebuyers will interpret the savings cited on a customized certificate for their home as the utility bill they should experience — a huge over-promise."
- Source: greenbuildermedia.com/blog/beyond-energy-ratings-basics

### 3. D.R. Horton HERS Disclaimer (largest US homebuilder by volume)
- "D.R. Horton does not warrant or guarantee that the home as-built or as used by its occupants will achieve the stated HERS Index score"
- "D.R. Horton does not warrant or guarantee the monthly or annual utility bills for the home"
- "D.R. Horton does not represent that all homes sold by D.R. Horton have been individually rated"
- Some homes rated based on "average energy performance for specified home categories" — not the actual house
- Source: drhorton.com/hers

### 4. Bai et al. (76 low-energy building case studies)
- Actual energy use was 86% LOWER to 483% HIGHER than predicted
- Average relative difference of 58%
- Source: Referenced in MDPI Sustainability 16(17), 7792 (2024)

### 5. LBNL 1996 Study
- Rating systems overestimated energy costs ~50%
- Found "no clear relationship between rating score and actual cost"
- Nearly 30 years old — and the problem hasn't been fixed
- Source: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

### 6. Dutch Renovation Study
- 57% of nearly 90,000 renovated dwellings had lower energy savings than expected
- Gap tended to be larger when pre-renovation performance was worse
- Source: Referenced in MDPI Sustainability 16(17), 7792 (2024)

### 7. Builder Online — HERS mechanics
- Reference home is a 2006 code-built house of same size, shape, climate zone (scored 100)
- In Arizona, 63% of new-construction homes are HERS rated (average score: 59)
- HERS originated from mortgage bankers in the 1980s who wanted energy savings in mortgage calculations
- Source: builderonline.com

### 8. DOE Home Energy Score
- "asset rating" — reflects structure and systems, NOT how occupants use the home
- "does not necessarily reflect the actual energy use of a building"
- Source: energy.gov

### 9. Fine Homebuilding
- Jeff Ross Stein (Home Energy): HERS Index predicts "individual potential" for energy use, not actual energy use
- 10,000-sq-ft house with HERS 50 vs 2,000-sq-ft house with HERS 100 — bigger house uses more energy despite lower score
- Source: finehomebuilding.com

### 10. Emporia Vue — residential energy monitoring with AI
- $200 monitor, 16 CT sensors, 24,000 samples/sec, ±2% accuracy
- AI Energy Assistant analyzes data for personalized insights
- Monitors ACTUAL consumption — but doesn't reconcile against HERS prediction
- Commercial building management (BMS) is a $7B+ market; residential monitoring is consumer DIY

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES:
- If you're buying: know that the HERS score on the marketing material is not a bill guarantee
- If you're building: understand the gap between what you're selling and what the homeowner will experience
- If you're a homeowner: a $200 monitor will tell you more about your energy costs than a HERS certificate
- Policy angle: should HERS raters be required to do post-occupancy verification?
