# Research: NOAA Atlas 15 and the Residential Stormwater Design Gap

## Angle
Every home built in America right now has its stormwater system designed using rainfall data that assumes the climate isn't changing. NOAA Atlas 15 — dropping September 2026 — will be the first federal precipitation standard to account for climate trends. When it does, the drainage pipes, detention basins, and grading plans that passed code review last month will be officially undersized. There is no AI tool that helps residential builders or homeowners model lot-level stormwater risk under the updated data.

## Kill Test
YES — directly helps someone building or buying a home. If you're breaking ground in 2026, your civil engineer is using Atlas 14 data (some states still use TP-40 from 1961). Atlas 15 will show higher design storm volumes in most regions. Your drainage is undersized before you move in.

## Primary Sources

### 1. NOAA Atlas 15 — Official
- Source: NOAA National Weather Service Office of Water Prediction
- URL: https://water.noaa.gov/about/atlas15
- Key data:
  - First U.S. precipitation frequency standard to account for non-stationarity (climate trends)
  - Two volumes: Vol 1 (trends in observations), Vol 2 (climate model projections through 2100)
  - Pilot released for Montana Sept 2024
  - CONUS preliminary estimates: September 2026
  - Full publication: CONUS 2026, rest of US 2027
  - Funded by Bipartisan Infrastructure Law
  - Will supersede Atlas 14 (current standard referenced in nearly all engineering codes)
  - Quote: "Atlas 15 will become the federal government's new authoritative dataset for the planning and design of infrastructure Americans rely on every day." — Ed Clark, NOAA National Water Center director

### 2. Nave Newell Engineering Analysis
- Source: Nave Newell (civil engineering firm), May 2026
- URL: https://www.navenewell.com/noaa-atlas-15/
- Key data:
  - Atlas 15 is a "fundamental recalibration of how we model risk, size infrastructure, and develop land"
  - IDF curves will reflect changing climate → altered design storm volumes
  - Practical impact: likely need to upsize stormwater management systems
  - Larger detention/retention basins → bigger physical footprint → reduced buildable lot area
  - Conveyance infrastructure (storm drains, culverts, swales) need enhanced capacities
  - Even infrastructure designed to Atlas 14 "may warrant careful review"
  - Projects mid-permitting could face "unexpected regulatory transitions"

### 3. Nature Cities Study (University of Michigan, 2024)
- Source: Nature Cities, Vol 1, pp 626-627
- Authors: Valeriy Ivanov et al.
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44284-024-00128-3
- Key data:
  - Metro Detroit Aug 11, 2014: record rainfall → $1.8B damage, 100,000+ homes flooded
  - Traditional stormwater design "can exacerbate flooding during severe weather events"
  - Problem: failure to recognize "flood connectivity" — how surface runoff, pipes, channels are all interlinked
  - Current design approach: localized solutions → unintended downstream consequences
  - Recommends: holistic, system-wide approaches; advanced hydrologic/hydrodynamic models; diversified design scenarios
  - FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Maps focus on riverine floodplains, "overlooks risks in urban areas distant from permanent water bodies"

### 4. Burns & McDonnell White Paper
- Source: Burns & McDonnell Engineering
- URL: https://info.burnsmcd.com/white-paper/rethinking-stormwater-how-development-impacts-the-land
- Key data:
  - "commonly used IDF standards and existing hydrologic criteria are based on data from the 1960s"
  - Standards "focus on severe weather events rather than the changing weather patterns, such as back-to-back rain events"
  - Stormwater design "no longer stands alone — it connects directly to land planning, roadway layout, and construction sequencing"

### 5. Trump Administration Resumed Atlas 15 (NBC Palm Springs, July 2025)
- URL: https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2025/07/19/trump-administration-resumes-work-on-atlas-15-flash-flood-risk-database
- Key data:
  - Work had been paused on Atlas 15 Phase 2 (climate projections)
  - Resumed through FY2026
  - "Current U.S. infrastructure relies on Atlas 14's decades-old data, which experts say no longer reflects today's reality of more frequent and severe rainfall events"

### 6. RTI International (Atlas 15 Developer)
- URL: https://www.rti.org/water-resources-tools/noaa-atlas-15
- RTI developing Atlas 15 for NOAA Office of Water Prediction
- Novel nonstationary statistical and dynamical modeling methodologies
- Precipitation frequency estimates for present AND future time periods

## The Gap
- Existing stormwater design tools (HydroCAD, Bentley PondPack, Autodesk Storm & Sanitary Analysis) are professional-grade, manual-input, require civil engineering expertise
- No AI tool that:
  1. Automatically ingests updated precipitation data (Atlas 15 when released)
  2. Models lot-level stormwater for a specific residential parcel
  3. Compares design adequacy under Atlas 14 vs. Atlas 15 rainfall estimates
  4. Identifies underdesigned drainage before the homeowner experiences their first flood
  5. Recommends cost-effective green infrastructure (bioswales, rain gardens, permeable pavement) as alternatives to upsizing gray infrastructure
- BEAM (Building Emissions Accounting for Materials) proved the model: take complex professional data (EPDs), make it accessible to residential builders. Nobody has done the same for stormwater.

## Strongest Counterargument
Most jurisdictions already build in safety factors (designing for 10-year or 25-year storms rather than minimums). Some argue Atlas 15 differences will be modest in many regions. But: the Montana pilot showed meaningful increases in some durations, and the whole point is that the stationary assumption underlying every existing safety factor is invalid.
