# Research: Pattern Book Programs × AI — The Generation Gap

## Angle
Congress just funded pattern book programs (ROAD Act, law July 11, 2026). Cities are launching them: Rogers AR (13 designs), Ohio (2 designs), Overland Park KS, LA County (wildfire rebuild with Canibuild AI). Preapproved plans cut $5K-$10K in design costs and reduce approval time by up to 80%. But every existing program has the same problem: a handful of architect-drawn designs that work on a fraction of lots. AI can generate thousands of code-compliant variations and fit them to specific parcels, but nobody is connecting generative design tools to the pattern book infrastructure Congress just funded.

## Kill Test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? YES — if your city launches a pattern book, you skip weeks/months of permitting and save $5K-$10K. If you're a builder, this is the fastest path to more homes. If you're a city planner, this tells you how to implement the ROAD Act provision. If you're a homeowner on a non-standard lot, you learn why the current 13-plan libraries don't help you.

### 2. 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act
- Became law July 11, 2026 (without presidential signature)
- House vote: 358-32; Senate vote: 85-5
- Key provisions for pattern books:
  - Creates competitive grants for communities to develop "pattern books" of preapproved housing designs
  - Eliminates permanent chassis requirement for manufactured housing (saves $5K-$10K per unit)
  - Streamlines NEPA environmental reviews for housing
  - Single-stair apartment building guidelines
  - Prohibits institutional investors (350+ homes) from buying new single-family homes
- Quote: "Not having that chassis immediately wipes several thousand dollars off that price" — Kate Wood, NerdWallet
- Source: Wikipedia article, multiple news sources (ABA Banking Journal, Pew, Iowa Public Radio, Wyoming News)

### 3. Rogers, Arkansas — Pattern Zone Program (June 2026)

## The Gap (Article Thesis)
Every existing pattern book program operates at artisanal scale: 2 plans (Ohio), 13 plans (Rogers), a handful (LA County). Each was drawn by an architect, reviewed by human staff, and works on a specific subset of lots.

Meanwhile:
- AI can check a design against every applicable building code in minutes (Archistar, AutoReview.AI)
- AI can evaluate what's buildable on a specific parcel (Canibuild)
- Generative design tools can produce thousands of variations from constraints (Autodesk, others)

Nobody has connected these capabilities into a system that generates a preapproved design for every lot in a jurisdiction. The ROAD Act just funded the demand side (grants for pattern books). The supply side (AI generation + validation at scale) exists in pieces but hasn't been assembled.

13 plans. 20,000+ unique zoning configurations in the US. The math doesn't work without AI. And nobody is building the bridge.
