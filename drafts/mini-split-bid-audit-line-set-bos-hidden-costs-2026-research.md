# Research: Mini-Split Bid Audit — The Line Set / Balance-of-System Cost Gap

**Slug:** mini-split-bid-audit-line-set-bos-hidden-costs-2026
**Journalist:** Jake Kowalski (construction tech, tools, robotics)
**Date:** 2026-09-26

## Thesis
A mini-split bid's headline number hides the balance-of-system costs: the factory refrigerant pre-charge covers 98 ft of TOTAL piping (not per run), standard bids include only 15-25 ft of line set per zone, and condensate pumps, line-hide covers, extra copper, dedicated circuits, and permits routinely add $1,100-$2,900 to a 4-zone install. The article gives homeowners a 6-line-item bid audit they can run before signing.

## Original contribution
A worked 4-zone bid audit: 4 runs of 35/45/50/60 ft = 190 ft total piping vs Mitsubishi's 98 ft chargeless piping length → 92 ft excess × 0.216 oz/ft = 19.9 oz (~1.24 lb) of added R410A — honest small dollar figure (~$78 at $63/lb) — contrasted with the real money: Fine Homebuilding's measured BOS finding (33-40% of equipment+materials cost) applied to a typical $10,500-$12,500 4-zone bid, with each of the 6 adders priced from 2026 sources. Nobody has published the pre-charge math next to the adder table as a consumer bid-audit tool.

## Key facts
- Mitsubishi MXZ multi-zone: factory charge covers 98 ft of total piping across ALL indoor heads; additional charge billed at 0.216 oz/ft (MXZ-4C36NA3) or 0.21/0.55 oz/ft by liquid-line diameter (ported type).
- The 98-ft figure is aggregate, not per-run — a 4-head layout with 35/45/50/60 ft runs = 190 ft, exceeding the allowance by 92 ft.
- Added refrigerant for the example: 92 ft × 0.216 oz/ft = 19.87 oz ≈ 1.24 lb. At Angi's 2026 average R410A recharge price ($63/lb), that's ~$78 — the honest small number; undercharging is a performance risk (botched charge can kill a compressor), not a big invoice line.
- Standard bids include 15-25 ft of line set per zone (allbetterapp); extensions run $75-$200 per 10 ft (pickcomfort) or a flat $200-$500 long-run adder.
- Condensate pumps: $150-$300 each installed (minihomehub); needed when the head can't drain by gravity (interior walls, below grade).
- Line-hide/line set covers: $150-$400 (allbetterapp) / $100-$400 (minihomehub); Fine Homebuilding measured 9.9% of equipment+materials on a real job.
- Dedicated circuit: $300-$800 (filterbuy via prior research) / $150-$400 (pickcomfort). Permits: $100-$400.
- Fine Homebuilding deep-dive: even a "no-frills" 1-ton install had BOS at 33% of equipment+materials; elaborate installs approach 40%.

## Counterargument (full strength)
Mini-split bids are competitive and most reputable contractors DO include realistic allowances — the "gap" is often already priced in, and a bid that looks $2,000 cheaper may simply be the one that itemized honestly. DIY pre-charged systems (MRCOOL, Zone Air) sidestep most of this. Also: the factory pre-charge is generous enough that most single-zone installs never need added refrigerant, so the charge math matters mainly for multi-zone layouts.

## Limitations
- Cost figures are 2026 national ranges; labor varies 15-40% by metro (pickcomfort).
- Mitsubishi-specific charge math; Daikin/Fujitsu publish similar tables but rates differ — readers must check their own unit's manual.
- Did not verify per-installer markup on copper line sets (copper price volatility noted by plumbingsupplyandmore).
- IRA 25C federal credit status for 2026 unclear post-Dec-2025 expiry claims (ductless4less says expired; allbetterapp describes 30%/$2,000 IRA 25C) — article will flag the uncertainty rather than assert.
