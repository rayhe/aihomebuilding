# Research #783 — AI floor plan generators vs. solar orientation (Elena Vasquez)

**Slug:** `ai-floor-plan-generator-west-facing-glass-cooling-penalty-2026`
**Headline (working):** "Your AI-Designed Home Has a Window Problem. It's Facing West."
**Journalist:** Elena Vasquez (Architecture & Design)
**Date:** 2026-09-05

## Angle
Text-to-floor-plan AI tools (Maket, FloorDrafter, ZURU/AWS Dreamcatcher pipeline) generate
house plans from natural-language prompts. None of them treats solar orientation as a
first-class design input. FloorDrafter lists "orientation" as an optional user-specified
constraint ("anything that needs to stay fixed"), which means the default behavior is to
ignore it. The result: plans optimized for room adjacency and curb appeal that can place
large glazing areas facing the afternoon sun, locking in decades of cooling cost that no
rendering ever shows.

## Kill test
PASS. Anyone using an AI plan generator (or buying a plan generated by one) needs to know
that window placement relative to the sun is a 30-year operating-cost decision, and that
the tool will not make it for them. Actionable: specify orientation in the prompt, audit
west-facing glass area against the 110 ft² PNNL benchmark, or budget ~0.5 ton of extra
cooling.

## Primary sources (7)

1. **PNNL-18038** (DOE/Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, 2009): Technical report
   documenting development of a west-facing glazing limit for the IECC. Key findings:
   west-facing glazing has the greatest impact on cooling loads (solar geometry + high
   late-day outdoor temps + AC demand when occupants return home); proposed prescriptive
   cap of **110 ft²** of west-facing glazing ("west-facing" = 225°–300° azimuth); the
   IECC "has never effectively regulated the orientation of glazing in new homes."
   https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-18038.pdf

2. **IECC 2021 Table R402.1.3** (ICC): Prescriptive fenestration SHGC caps — 0.25 in
   Climate Zones 0–3, 0.40 in CZ 4–5, no requirement CZ 6–8. Regulates glass *quality*,
   not glass *placement*.
   https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IECC2021P2/chapter-4-re-residential-energy-efficiency

3. **Maket.ai** (product): AI floor plan generator — "Describe your project and let AI
   draft layouts" ("Generate a 2,400 sqft bungalow with 3 bedrooms..."). Text-prompt
   editing, 3D visualization, PDF/DXF export. No orientation input in the generation flow.
   https://www.maket.ai/ai-floor-plan-generator

4. **ZURU Tech × AWS Generative AI Innovation Center** (ZenML case study): text-to-floor
   plan via LLMs (Claude 3.5 Sonnet prompt engineering + Llama fine-tuning on SageMaker);
   109% improvement in instruction adherence, 54% in spatial-relation math. Evaluation
   dimensions are instruction adherence and dimension correctness — not energy, not
   orientation.
   https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/text-to-floor-plan-generation-using-llms-with-prompt-engineering-and-fine-tuning

5. **FloorDrafter** (product, via SimilarLabs): text-to-plan where the user may specify
   "total area, number and size of rooms, orientation, and anything that needs to stay
   fixed." Orientation is user-supplied optional context, not a modeled constraint.
   https://similarlabs.com/p/floordrafter

6. **IPCC AR4 WGIII §6.4.4** (2007): "In most climates, [reducing cooling load]
   include[s]: (i) orienting a building to minimize the wall area facing east or west...
   (vi) using selective glazing on windows with a low solar heat gain... and avoiding
   excessive window area (particularly on east- and west-facing walls)."
   https://archive.ipcc.ch/publications_and_data/ar4/wg3/en/ch6-ens6-4-4.html

7. **MDPI EngProc 22(1):2** (window configuration × orientation case study): worst case
   for space cooling is 80% window-wall ratio in the west orientation; best case is 15%
   WWR facing north. Confirms orientation × glazing-area interaction.
   https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/22/1/2

## Original contribution: the west-glass penalty calculation
Scenario: 2,000 ft² single-story home, Sacramento CA (IECC CZ 3, hot dry summers).
Compare two plans with identical total glazing:
- **AI-default plan:** great-room glass wall puts 160 ft² of glazing facing west
  (the "sunset view" trope every image model loves).
- **Orientation-aware plan:** 40 ft² west-facing; the rest redistributed to south
  (with overhangs) and north.

Delta: 120 ft² of west glass.
Peak afternoon solar gain through code-minimum west glass ≈ 50 BTU/hr·ft²
(650 W/m² incident on a west facade at low sun angle × SHGC 0.25, the IECC 2021
prescriptive ceiling for CZ 3).
Extra peak cooling load: 120 × 50 = **6,000 BTU/hr ≈ 0.5 tons**.
Annual extra energy: 800 equivalent full-load cooling hours (Sacramento assumption) ×
6,000 BTU/hr = 4.8M BTU ÷ EER 11 (SEER2 14.3 minimum system) ≈ **436 kWh/yr** ≈
**$140/yr** at $0.32/kWh. Equipment upsizing for the half-ton: **~$800–1,200**.
30-year present cost of the AI's window placement: roughly **$4,000–5,000** in today's
dollars, before demand charges and before the comfort penalty (west rooms running
5–8°F hot on August afternoons).

Methodology note: single-zone steady-state estimate, not a whole-building EnergyPlus
run. Assumes code-minimum SHGC 0.25 glass; many AI plans specify no glass at all.

## Skepticism / counterargument (steel-man)
In heating-dominated climates (CZ 6–8, where the IECC sets no SHGC requirement), west
glass is a smaller penalty and winter solar gain has real value. Views and daylight are
legitimate design goods — a west-facing glass wall over a canyon sunset is a choice,
not a mistake, *if* it is priced honestly. High-performance glass (SHGC ≤ 0.20),
exterior shading, and thermal mass can cut the penalty substantially. The claim is not
"never glaze west"; it is "the tool doesn't know what west costs, so you have to."

## Limitations
- Penalty math is a steady-state estimate, not a simulated building; real numbers move
  with shading, overhangs, thermal mass, and thermostat behavior.
- 800 EFLH and $0.32/kWh are Sacramento-plausible assumptions, not metered data.
- No public dataset exists of AI-generated plans' window distributions — the "AI puts
  glass west" claim rests on product design (orientation not modeled) plus the visual
  tropes of generated renders, not a plan corpus audit.
- PNNL's 110 ft² cap was proposed, never adopted into the IECC.

## Actionable takeaways (for the article)
1. If you generate a plan with Maket/FloorDrafter/etc., put orientation and lot
   azimuth in the prompt explicitly — it is optional input, and defaults to ignored.
2. Audit west-facing glass (225°–300°) against the 110 ft² PNNL benchmark before you
   fall in love with the render.
3. Every 100 ft² of west glass beyond an oriented design ≈ 0.4 tons of cooling and
   ~$115/yr in Sacramento-like climates. Price the view.
4. South glass with proper overhangs is the free lunch the AI never suggests; north
   glass is cheap daylight. West glass is the one you pay for twice.
