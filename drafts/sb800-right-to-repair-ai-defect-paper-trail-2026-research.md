# Research: SB 800 Right to Repair + AI Defect Paper Trail (Article #767)

**Journalist:** Catherine Chen (Policy & Regulation)
**Slug:** `sb800-right-to-repair-ai-defect-paper-trail-2026`
**Date:** September 3, 2026

## Angle
California's SB 800 (Right to Repair Act, Civ. Code §§895-945.5) forces homeowners to give builders a structured, months-long chance to fix defects before filing suit. A reform bill (AB 1903) that would have demanded more detailed pre-litigation evidence from claimants died on Aug 31, 2026 without a final vote. Meanwhile AI photo-documentation and warranty platforms now let homeowners build the evidentiary record the dead bill wanted, voluntarily. Original contribution: a computed maximum statutory timeline (262 days from notice to repair completion) nobody has published as a single number, plus a step-by-step map of where AI documentation changes leverage at each statutory stage.

## Kill test
Does this help someone building or buying a home? Yes. A California buyer of a new home who finds defects must navigate this exact procedure; missing the notice formalities or the inspection-cooperation duties can forfeit leverage. Builders need compliant intake. Both sides now face AI-generated evidence.

## Primary sources (8)

1. **Cal. Civil Code §§910, 913, 916, 917, 941** — read directly on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, Sept 3, 2026.
   - §910: pre-suit written notice via certified mail/overnight/personal delivery; must describe claim "in reasonable detail sufficient to determine the nature and location"; notice "shall have the same force and effect as a notice of commencement of a legal proceeding." Warranty/customer-service requests do NOT satisfy §910.
   - §913: builder must acknowledge in writing within 14 days.
   - §916(a): initial inspection/testing within 14 days after acknowledgment, at mutually convenient time; builder bears all costs, restores property within 48 hours; claimant may observe and video-record/photograph. §916(c): second inspection within 40 days of initial if builder gives written reasons within 3 days. §916(d): builder's failure to inspect on time releases claimant to file suit.
   - §917: within 30 days after inspection, builder may offer in writing to repair, with step-by-step statement of violation/scope/location, reasonable completion date, contractor names + license numbers, insurance evidence; homeowner may request up to 3 additional contractors to choose from.
   - §941: 10-year outside limit from substantial completion.
2. **CSLB official page** (cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Construction_Defect.aspx): confirms SB 800 gives builders the opportunity to repair before legal action; applies to new residential homes purchased after Jan 1, 2003; procedures at §§910-938; builder non-compliance lets homeowner proceed to filing.
3. **McMillin Albany LLC v. Superior Court (2018) 5 Cal.5th 443**: CA Supreme Court held the Right to Repair Act is the exclusive remedy for residential construction defect claims, including common-law claims; homeowner must give builder the pre-litigation repair right.
4. **The Close (Sept 2026), via CalMatters**: AB 1903 (Wicks/Wilson) died at the Aug 31, 2026 deadline despite 40-0 Senate and 70-0 Assembly votes — no Assembly concurrence vote. Final text would have required claimants to give builders more detailed descriptions and available evidence of defects pre-litigation; allowed repair-for-release agreements effective 1 year after repair; barred insurers from excluding certain repair costs from deductibles starting July 1, 2027. None takes effect.
5. **UC Berkeley Terner Center, July 2024**: developers pay 3-4x liability insurance on condos vs. comparable rentals; defect-liability expenses add 1.9-4.4% to hard costs on typical LA condo = ~$8,100-$18,300/unit (2024$).
6. **WarrantyWeek (Apr 2025)**: 27 US homebuilders paid $1.071B in warranty claims in 2024 (-2% vs 2023).
7. **ECI Software Solutions, Feb 24, 2025 (IBS launch)**: AvidWarranty, AI warranty management platform trained on 1.4M homeowner claims; automates responses, prioritizes urgent repairs.
8. **American Bar Association, Construction Litigation newsletter**: AI-assisted 360-degree jobsite capture (hardhat-mounted cameras, auto-tagged to floor plans) used for risk mitigation and dispute evidence; "frictionless, structured visual data capture."

## Original calculation: the 262-day statutory maximum
Worst-case timeline from homeowner notice to completed repair, using statutory maximums:
- Day 0: §910 notice sent (certified mail).
- Day 14: §913 acknowledgment deadline.
- Day 28: §916(a) initial inspection deadline (14 days after acknowledgment).
- Day 68: §916(c) second inspection deadline (40 days after initial).
- Day 98: §917 repair offer deadline (30 days after inspection).
- Day 128: §918 homeowner response deadline (30 days to authorize or request alternate contractor; per Wolff Law summary of statute).
- Day 142: §921 repair commencement deadline (14 days after acceptance).
- Day 262: §921 completion target ("every effort" to complete within 120 days).
Total: 14+14+40+30+30+14+120 = 262 days before the homeowner who cooperates fully can say the process failed and file suit. Mediation provisions can extend further. Caveat: most claims resolve faster; builders who miss deadlines forfeit the stay (§916(d)).

## Where AI changes leverage at each stage
- §910 notice: AI-assisted defect description (photo + measurements + §896 standard mapping). Vague notices ("cracks in walls") vs. specific ones ("diagonal stair-step cracking, 3mm, north foundation wall, consistent with §896 settlement standards").
- §916 inspection: homeowner's statutory right to video-record/photograph the builder's inspection; 360-capture apps create timestamped records.
- §917 offer: AI comparison of builder's repair scope vs. independent estimates (ClaimArchitect-style rebuild valuations exist for insurance; same pattern applies).
- Builder side: AvidWarranty-style triage on 1.4M-claim datasets; 360 site archives as defense evidence.

## Skepticism / counterargument
- AI-drafted §910 notices are not legal advice; a defective notice (wrong agent for service, wrong delivery method) can get the claim tossed. Builders must maintain a §914 agent with the Secretary of State; notice to the wrong address fails.
- Photo evidence cuts both ways: builder's own 360 archives can defeat claims.
- §924-937 builder opt-out: builders can use alternative contractual pre-litigation procedures if disclosed at sale; SB 800's statutory track isn't universal.
- No public data on how many §910 notices are filed annually or average time-to-resolution; the 262-day figure is a statutory-maximum synthesis, not an observed average. Say so.
- AB 1903's evidence requirements died with it; nothing currently requires homeowners to produce AI-grade documentation.

## Limitations
- Timeline math assumes each actor takes the full statutory allowance; real cases vary widely.
- §918/§921 details verified via secondary (Wolff Law) summary; §§910/913/916/917/941 verified against primary statute text directly.
- Warranty-claim dollars ($1.071B) are national and include non-defect warranty work, not SB 800 claims specifically.
- No independent verification of AvidWarranty performance claims.

## Actionable takeaways (for article)
- CA new-home buyer finding defects: send §910 notice via certified mail to the builder's registered agent (check Secretary of State), describe nature + location specifically, photograph/video everything before the builder's inspection, exercise your §916 right to record their inspection.
- Calendar the deadlines: 14-day ack, inspection windows, 30-day offer. Builder silence = your ticket to court (§916(d)).
- Builders: maintain the §914 agent registration; miss a deadline and you lose the repair right.
