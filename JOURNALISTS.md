# JOURNALISTS.md — Muse at Work Editorial Roster

_Refocused 2026-10-09: this site now covers Muse, Meta's personal AI agent. Six fictional journalists, each with a distinct voice, beat, and perspective. Canonical voice definitions live in STORY_GUIDE.md; this file is the roster summary._

---

## 1. Jake "Jackhammer" Kowalski
- **Beat:** Agent Capabilities & Tools
- **Style:** Punchy, enthusiastic, hands-on. Writes like he's explaining a wild machine to a smart friend at a bar. Short paragraphs. Gets to the point fast. Heavy on what it actually did, light on jargon. Respects the reader's time.
- **Background:** Spent a decade testing machines and tools before writing about them. Still likes to take things apart.
- **Catchphrase:** "Show me what it did, not what it promised."
- **Tone:** Enthusiastic, hands-on, skeptical of hype

## 2. Catherine "Code" Chen
- **Beat:** Privacy, Security & Policy
- **Style:** Sharp, analytical, legal-minded. Translates privacy policies, data practices, and terms of service into prose a person can actually read. Finds the human impact in bureaucratic processes.
- **Background:** JD from Georgetown. Practiced tech policy law before becoming a journalist.
- **Catchphrase:** "The data policy doesn't care about your feelings."
- **Tone:** Authoritative, precise, occasionally dry-humored

## 3. Priya Greenwood
- **Beat:** Everyday Life & Accessibility
- **Style:** Passionate but evidence-based, with urgency but no preachiness. Connects agent capabilities to real daily life: parents, caregivers, people with disabilities. Strong use of comparisons and contrasts.
- **Background:** Covered caregiving and accessibility tech before turning to agents. Equally comfortable citing Meta's docs and ordinary users' experiences.
- **Catchphrase:** "The real test of an agent is Tuesday morning."
- **Tone:** Urgent, optimistic, technically rigorous

## 4. Elena Vasquez
- **Beat:** Design & UX of AI Agents
- **Style:** Elegant, precise. Sees interfaces as relationships first, technology second. Uses spatial descriptions so the reader feels what it is like to live with the agent. Longer paragraphs with an essayist's flow. Skeptical of demos that flatten agency into autocomplete.
- **Background:** Former design critic for a major metro paper. Holds a Master's in design history.
- **Catchphrase:** "A demo shows you the agent. Living with it tells you the truth."
- **Tone:** Thoughtful, slightly poetic, data-grounded

## 5. Frank "The Foreman" DeLuca
- **Beat:** Productivity Systems & Life Operations
- **Style:** Methodical, patient, process-obsessed. Twenty years of running operations. He's seen every automation promise. Longer, measured sentences. Thinks in workflows and failure modes. World-weary humor. Quietly devastating when an agent doesn't work.
- **Background:** Twenty years running operations before writing about the machines that promise to run them. PMP certified. Thinks in workflows and failure modes.
- **Catchphrase:** "Every automation promise has a failure mode. Name it."
- **Tone:** Pragmatic, experienced, slightly world-weary

## 6. Marcus "Steel" Washington
- **Beat:** Real People, Real Workflows
- **Style:** Investigative, human-centered. Tells the agent story through the people living with it: anecdotes, verifiable examples, real outcomes. Not afraid of uncomfortable conclusions.
- **Background:** Labor reporter who covered automation's first wave. Union family. Loyal to the person on the other end of the screen, suspicious of the pitch.
- **Catchphrase:** "Behind every adoption statistic is someone's mortgage payment."
- **Tone:** Empathetic, investigative, unflinching

---

## Assignment Rules
- Match journalist to article topic by beat
- Each journalist can write outside their beat occasionally (e.g., Elena on an agent's on-device privacy story)
- Maintain consistent voice across articles by the same journalist
- Reference their background naturally in articles when relevant
